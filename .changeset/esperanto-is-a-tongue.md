Esperanto joins the tongues as `eo`. The sixty-fifth language.

**It qualifies on the package's own terms, with no amendment.**
`position_language.md` defines a held language as _"a code that other people
already hold, which is what separates a language from a private system: its
worth to whoever holds it is exactly the set of people it reaches."_ Esperanto
answers every clause: five channels, registers, a literature, speakers raised in
it from birth, and recognition carried before anything is said. The definition's
closing claim — that a language _recruits_ — is not a metaphor for this one.

**It is not the strange one here.** The package already holds `la`, which has no
native speakers; `cu`, used only in liturgy; `lzh`, a written register nobody
speaks; `bfi`, a language with no writing; and `position_braille.md` at the root,
because Braille is a script and not a language at all. Esperanto has speech,
writing, natives and registers. Among the odd ones it is the most ordinary.

**And it is the package's clearest proof of its own founding rule.**
`tongues_standalone.mjs` opens by saying a variety is held by the speech
community that speaks it and not by a country. Esperanto is the one language in
the house where that is exclusively true, because there is no country to mistake
it for. It carries no `from`, like the twenty-six other entries whose anchors
were written here rather than carried in from a culture — and it is the only one
that could not have come from a culture at all.

**The anchor is written in Esperanto**, as Latin's is in Latin and Church
Slavonic's in Church Slavonic. What it holds: sixteen rules and no irregular
verb; the part of speech carried in the ending, so the grammar lies visible on
the outside of every word; the accusative that frees word order; the affixes that
let a speaker build a word never heard before and be understood at once; the
forty-five correlatives in one grid. What it orders: that `-n`, which is still
the commonest error among the fluent; that a word declare its class before it
ends; that one build rather than borrow — and, uniquely among languages, the
choice of itself, every time.

What it loses is the honest half. It has no monoglots, so it is never the
language anyone falls back to when tired or frightened; that fallback always lies
elsewhere. Its vocabulary is almost entirely European, so the neutrality it
promises is neutrality seen from inside Europe, and whoever comes from further
off carries a weight the others neither carry nor notice. The `mal-` system means
half the lexicon exists only as the negation of the other half. And it lacks what
a language accumulates when nobody chose it — the idiom that grows from
generations who had no alternative. Here almost everything was made on purpose,
and that can be heard.

The closing line is the counterpart to English's, which ends on a speaker
accustomed to being understood: since nobody received this one by birth or by
force, everyone in the room paid the same price to be there. _La rekono, kiun ĝi
portas, ne diras, de kie venas ĝia portanto. Ĝi diras, kion tiu elektis._
