**The defining question gets the answer it keeps finding: a cultural moment, not
a constitutional one.**

The order already asked whether a play stages what defines its culture. What it
did not say is which way the answer usually fails, and the failure is not staging
too little. A play can carry ten plots and still answer the wrong question,
because a culture named after a country makes the country the obvious material:
foundings, treaties, borders, the dates a state acquired or lost its shape. Those
are cheap to research and they read as complete. A play that stages only the
statehood has described a passport.

Read against all three DACH cultures after Austria and Switzerland were staged,
and each showed the same shape — something asserted in the chapter where the play
describes itself, and no scene carrying it:

- Switzerland's **Name** chapter says `Confoederatio Helvetica`, and the Helvetii
  appear in no other file. Its Arc rests on four languages; the only plot about
  them is the 1938 recognition of the fourth. The constitutional act is staged
  and the late-antique settlement that put four languages there is not.
- Austria writes `barock` in its Arc, its pitch, its culture-position, Salzburg
  and the Belvedere — and in no plot. It has a place whose name means _salt
  fortress_ and no salt, no Hallstatt, no Noricum.
- Germany's cast is Luther, Bismarck, Adenauer, Kohl and two contemporaries: no
  poet, no composer, no scientist. Goethe and Weimar appear in `REFERENCES.md`
  and in the file for the motorway. A culture whose Arc rests on `Können statt
Rang` had staged every chancellor and no craft.

So the order gains the tell, which is mechanical enough to use on purpose:
**take the words the play uses about itself in its Arc, Name, Stakes and pitch,
and ask which appear in a plot.** The ones that do not are the shortlist. It
stays a dialogue and never a gate — whether a gap matters is the judgement this
order exists to protect — but it is where to look first, and it found three in an
afternoon.

`CLAUDE.md` carries the rule in two sentences and points at the order for the
rest.
