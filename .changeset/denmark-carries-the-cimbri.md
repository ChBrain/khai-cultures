**Denmark carries the Cimbri, which it did not before.** A new `plot_04`, Cued on the
28 May 1891 bog find at Gundestrup in Aars parish, Himmerland — two peat-cutters, no state,
no archaeologist — with `place_himmerland`, `piece_gundestrupkarret` and `piece_cimbrertyren`.

The plot is not about the Cimbri marching on Rome. That is Italy's story. It is about
**Himmerland deciding it is Cimbrian**, and the Tension is that none of the three links
holding that decision together survives scrutiny:

- the cauldron is **Thracian or Dacian** work from the Lower Danube, not Cimbrian — silver
  is rare in Celtic art and common in Thracian;
- the archaeology finds **no clear trace of a mass migration** from Jutland in the early
  Iron Age, so the Jutland Cimbri and the Cimbri who broke Roman armies may not be one
  people;
- and the bull that fixed the claim in bronze is from **1937**, paid for by a distillery
  and planted on its own closed factory.

What Himmerland found was evidence of _contact_ with a world far to the south. It read it as
evidence of _descent_. The dates chain tightly and all in one parish: the bog is in Aars, and
Aars Municipality put the bull on its arms.

It lands 27 years after 1864, inside the mood `plot_03` already names — _hvad udad tabes,
skal indad vindes_ — which is why it sits where it does rather than before `plot_00`. The
Cimbri are ~100 BC and Denmark's origin is the Danevirke from 500; making them the origin
would have claimed the very thing this plot exists to doubt.
