## plot_00: Kanata

`plot_01` is Confederation, 1867, which is a founding and the passport besides. The
origin is older than the country by millennia and it is the reason the country has the
shape it has.

Two men lift a boat out of the water and carry it over their heads to the next lake.
**It weighs less than either of them.** Bark, split root and pitch, built here out of
what grows here, and mendable on any bank along the route with the same three things.

There is no road across this country and will not be for a very long time. There is
rock, forest, cold - and water, a chain of lakes and rivers from the Atlantic to the
middle of the continent. **The people who had lived here for thousands of years had
already solved it**, and the solution is the canoe.

When the French come for fur, they do not invent a route. **They are taken along one.**
The trade runs on Indigenous canoes, Indigenous routes, Indigenous knowledge of the ice
and the season, and Indigenous consent - because a European alone in this country in
February is a dead man. Out of that working life comes a people who did not exist
before it, the Metis, born of the trade itself.

So the country's shape is the shape of the water: **the line the railway follows in
1867 is the line the paddlers were already following, and the cities stand where the
portages were.** And the name is of a piece with it - Cartier asked what the place was
called, was told _kanata_, a word for the village in front of him, and wrote it down as
the name of everything. **The country is called by a borrowed word for a smaller thing,
taken down slightly wrong, and it kept it.**

The Tension, and it is why this plot had to exist: a country that begins as a
partnership on the water learns its first skill early - **you do not have to become
each other in order to get somewhere together.** That is the mosaic before it has a
name, and it is why this federation would later be built by joining rather than
breaking.

**And the shadow is in the same fact, because a partnership lasts exactly as long as it
is needed.** The trade required Indigenous knowledge, labour and permission, and the
respect it showed was the respect of dependence. Once the rail was laid and the fur was
worth less, the need ended - and what had been a relationship became, within one
lifetime, the Indian Act and the schools. **The reckoning this country owes is not the
story of strangers who arrived and took. It is the story of a working relationship that
stopped being useful to one side, and of what that side did next.**

## The middle: the maple leaf into plot_03

Folded in with a paragraph and a Stage line, on the montenegro precedent, and it is
worth the paragraph. Until 1965 the country flew an ensign with another nation's flag
in the corner, and the replacement was fought over bitterly for months. What went up
instead had **no crown, no cross, and no other country's canton - a leaf off a tree
that grows here. It is the first emblem this dominion ever had that did not point
somewhere else.**

## plot_99: Forty Million

In June 2023 a clock on a government website ticks past forty million. `Jun` sees it in
a queue at the drive-through and thinks little of it, except that his family is in that
number twice: once when they came, and again for the cousins who came last year.

Canada grows faster in these years than any country in the G7, almost all of it
arrival - over a million added in 2023, forty-one million within a year of forty. **A
country whose whole answer to who it is has always been "whoever comes and keeps what
they brought" gets the largest possible confirmation of itself.**

**Then it stops.** In late 2024 the targets are cut sharply and the population is
planned to shrink for the first time in modern memory. **Nothing had changed about the
mosaic. What had changed was that there was nowhere to live**, and the argument that
arrives is not about culture at all. It is about arithmetic.

And in the same decade both things that stood in for "us" turned out to be something
other than they looked. Hockey - the pond, the backyard rink, the parents hollering
from the boards - had a national federation found in 2022 to have quietly settled an
assault claim from a fund built partly out of minor-hockey registration fees; the
sponsors left, the board went, **and the game had to be handed back to the people who
play it.** And the Timmies run, named for a hockey player and running in every town in
the country, **has been owned since 2014 by a multinational headquartered somewhere
else.**

In `le Quebec`, `Therese` watches her province legislate in 2022 to hold the French she
never once had to leave, in the same years the country breaks its record for newcomers,
most of whom arrive in English.

The Tension: a country built by joining rather than breaking has always answered _who
are we_ procedurally, by letting more people in and letting them keep what they
brought. This decade it let in more people than ever and then said not so many. **The
question the mosaic never had to answer was how many, and a country whose identity is
its openness has no vocabulary for a limit** - so the limit arrives as housing starts
and target numbers rather than as anything anyone would call a decision about who
belongs.

**And the rhyme with the beginning is not comfortable.** This country started as a
working arrangement across difference, and that arrangement held for exactly as long as
both sides needed it. **The mosaic has never yet been tested by scarcity.** The decade
does not decide whether it scales. It asks the question a country defined by taking
people in has never had to ask itself: **what it is on the day it stops.**
