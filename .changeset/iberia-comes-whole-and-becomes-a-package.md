## plot_00: The Shared Ground

A muleteer walks west along a paved road laid to reach the ocean. Nobody stops him
and nobody asks anything. **The speech around him changes a little every few days,
and he could not say at which village it stopped being one thing and started being
another.**

Rome makes the peninsula one administrative space and binds it with roads that still
decide where the towns are. Al-Andalus holds most of it for centuries and leaves its
words in both the languages that come after. Then the long push south goes in
**parallel columns** - Castile down the middle, Aragon down the east, and an Atlantic
county that becomes Portugal down the west.

**The line between them is simply where two armies going the same way at the same
time stopped touching.** Fixed at Alcañices in 1297, it is the oldest continuous
border in Europe.

The Tension is the group's whole reason to exist: **these two are not neighbours who
grew apart, they are one movement that split lengthwise.** Which is why everything
structural is shared - the reconquest, the turn to the ocean, the empire, a long
dictatorship each, the return to Europe on the same day - and why almost nothing
everyday is. **A border that old stops being a frontier and becomes a habit**: nothing
to negotiate along it, nothing to fight about, and therefore no reason to look. The
peninsula gives them one geography and does not give them one another.

## plot_99: One Grid

At the same second on an April afternoon, a traffic light dies in Lisbon and a
traffic light dies in Madrid. **Neither driver knows yet that the other has stopped
too, and neither has any habit of thinking about the other at all.**

In 2025 almost the whole peninsula loses power in seconds - Spain, Portugal and
Andorra, some fifty-five million people - and it becomes very plain for one afternoon
that they are **a single electrical island joined to the rest of Europe by a thin link
over the Pyrenees.**

And that is the pattern of everything here: the longest land border in the EU with
hardly any rail across it; rivers that reach the Portuguese sea and all rise in
Spanish rain, argued about only in drought years; **the interior emptying on both
sides of the same line, in the same decade, for the same reasons** - which is
literally spain's own `plot_99` and portugal's, written separately in #628 and #616
and only here read as one thing. Meanwhile the coasts fill with the rest of the
continent moving south.

The Tension: seven centuries of not looking, and the peninsula keeps demonstrating it
is one system anyway - **and none of it has produced a single shared institution
beyond the ones Brussels built for them.** The proverb still current in Portugal,
_from Spain, neither a good wind nor a good marriage_, is not hostility; it is the
settled form of indifference, which is harder to argue with. The decade does not ask
whether the two will merge - they will not and neither wants to. It asks **whether
being one system by physics obliges anything at all.**
