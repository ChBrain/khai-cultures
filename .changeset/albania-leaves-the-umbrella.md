## plot_00: Shtëpia e Zotit dhe e mikut

`plot_01` is Skanderbeg, which is the passport: the hero, the twenty-five years, the
fortress. The origin is what governed these mountains when nobody did.

At dusk a stranger knocks at a stone `kulla` in the northern highlands. **He is not
asked his name, where he is from, or what he believes.** He is given coffee, raki and
the best room in the house, and from the moment he crosses the threshold the host
answers for his life with his own.

These mountains stood for centuries with no state - Ottoman on the map, ungoverned in
fact. What governed was the Kanun, **a law written down nowhere**: held in memory,
passed mouth to mouth, applied by village elders, and collected and printed only in
the twentieth century, after the man who wrote it down was dead. Its pillar is `besa`,
the word kept to death, and its most absolute clause is the guest. **Hospitality here
is not generosity, it is obligation**, and the formula leaves no room: the Albanian's
house belongs to God and to the guest.

The same law built the `kulla` too. The thick walls and small windows are not style,
they are cover, because the Kanun also regulated the blood feud. **The same code that
makes a stranger untouchable made a neighbour a target, and both are inside the same
stone.**

Its hardest test came much later. In the Second World War Albanians, Muslim and
Christian, hid Jews in their houses and did not hand them over, and **the country came
out of the war with more Jews in it than it had before.** No state did that; there was
barely a state. An old rule did: **whoever has come under your roof is not yours to
give.**

The Tension: a people ruled by a promise rather than by a power learns that the word
is the only enforcement it has. **And the shadow is in the same sentence.** The code
that made the guest sacred made blood a duty, and a law kept in memory rather than on
paper can be invoked by anyone who claims to remember it - so when the state fell
again after 1991, the feud came back to the mountains with it. **The given word holds
you up when you have nothing else, and holds you down once you finally have something.**

## The middle: Nënë Tereza into plot_03

Mother Teresa died in 1997 and belongs to neither end, so she is folded into
`plot_03`, on the belgium and romania precedent - and the fold is the point, not the
convenience.

`plot_03` is Hoxha declaring Albania **the world's first officially atheist state** in 1967. And in exactly those years the most famous Albanian alive was a nun. She was not
allowed to come. **She came only in 1989, after he was dead, to see the graves of her
mother and her sister, neither of whom she saw alive.** The regime could declare the
nation atheist; it could not make the world, on hearing the word Albanian, think of
anyone else first.

## plot_99: Shqiponja me dy duar

At a village wedding half the guests have flown in - Milan, Athens, Zurich, Pristina,
Detroit. When the singing starts, hands go up and thumbs hook: **the eagle, made with
two hands. Nobody teaches this. Everybody knows it, and knows it identically in five
countries.**

The 2023 census counts about 2.4 million people, near a seventh fewer than twelve
years earlier. **There have never been so few Albanians inside the country and never
so many outside it.** Meanwhile the coast that only locals knew now takes over ten
million visitors a year, four times the population. `Drita` still receives a guest
exactly as she always did - coffee, raki, the best room - **except that now the guest
pays to sleep there and leaves on Monday.**

And the song holds. `Iso-polifonia`, where one voice takes the melody, another answers
and the rest hold the drone beneath, is on the world's heritage list and is now sung
as often in diaspora halls as in the village square. **It is the one song of this
country nobody can sing alone: without the people holding the drone it does not
stand up.**

The eagle went further than the song. In 2018, at a World Cup match, two footballers
of another national team made the sign in front of the world's cameras, and within a
day people who had never heard of the Kanun knew what it meant.

The Tension: this play's Arc says the nation is held not by a border but by the
language, the besa and `shqiptaria` - and this decade proves it better than any other,
because **the border no longer even holds the people.** There are more Albanians
outside than in, and the nation stands: on a hand sign everyone recognises, a song
where somebody has to hold the drone, and a language no neighbour understands, which
is exactly why it will not let you forget which you are. The decade does not decide
whether the country becomes a place of return. It decides something harder: **a nation
that has already learned to hold together with no state, no border and no shared faith
is not in any hurry to be taken back - and that very ease of staying Albanian anywhere
takes the urgency out of bringing anyone home.**
