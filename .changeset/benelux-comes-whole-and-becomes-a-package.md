## plot_00: The Delta

A bargeman ties up where the river braids into its last channels. His cargo came
down from the Alps and the Ruhr and goes onto a sea-going hull tomorrow. **He has
changed language twice on the way down and paid toll to three different lords, and
none of it has changed the direction of the water.**

The Rhine, the Meuse and the Scheldt all reach the sea in one low, wet corner, and
everything half a continent makes must pass through it. That single fact builds the
Flemish cloth towns, then Dutch shipping, then Antwerp and Rotterdam, and makes this
the richest ground per square mile in Europe for most of a thousand years. **It also
makes it where every larger neighbour's war goes**, because whoever holds the delta
holds the back door to France, Germany and England at once.

So the three become **one economy centuries before they are three countries, and
somebody else's battlefield just as long.**

The Tension is the group's whole justification, and it earns it from the members'
own origins - each of which was written separately in #621, #622 and #625 and only
now reads as one thing. Belgium's ground never had a master of its own; Luxembourg's
rock was valuable to everyone and therefore nobody's; the Netherlands' equality was
a requirement of the water. **Three founding stories about living under somebody
else's decision, and the same three trades learned from it.** But a unity that comes
from a river system rather than a choice explains why they act together and says
nothing about what they are to each other. **The delta made them partners. It did
not make them a we.**

## plot_99: The Border That Is Not There

A woman drives north out of Antwerp and notices, minutes later, that the road
markings have changed colour. **That is the only thing that happened. She has
crossed a frontier her grandparents were shot at, and the car did not slow down.**

The customs union worked so completely that it vanished into its own result: what
these three prototyped in London became the single market, they host Brussels,
Luxembourg and The Hague between them, and the Benelux Union was quietly renewed in
2008, indefinitely, with almost nobody able to say what it currently does.

And in the same decade **each of the three found the same question at home** - the
three `plot_99`s written across #621, #622 and #625, which is where this one comes
from. Belgium's two communities greeting each other in English. The Netherlands
finding its own tongue optional. Luxembourg asking who counts as being from there.
**Three of the most integrated countries in Europe, each unsure at the same moment
who "we" is.**

The Tension: **this group solved the problem between its members and has no
vocabulary for the problem inside them.** That is not a failure of the union - it is
the shape of what a customs union is for, and worth naming rather than papering
over. The decade does not ask whether the arrangement holds; it holds so well it is
invisible. It asks whether _small states that negotiate_ is still an identity once
the negotiating is finished.
