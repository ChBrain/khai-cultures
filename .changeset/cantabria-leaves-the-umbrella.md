Cantabria gets a plot line and leaves the umbrella. Sixth of the eight.

**A textbook floor.** This play opened on _the discovery of Altamira in 1879_ —
a nineteenth-century date standing in for something forty thousand years old.
That is exactly the invisible failure `order_plot_zero.md` describes: nothing
looks missing below the first plot, because there is nothing below it to look at.
plot_00 puts the thing itself under the date of its finding.

**Two plots.**

- `plot_00_una_mano_en_la_pared.md` — someone puts an open hand against the rock,
  fills their mouth with pigment and blows. What is left is the outline in
  negative, five fingers, on a wall that never sees daylight. That happened here
  more than forty thousand years ago and it is still there. The strip is narrow
  and steep, the range comes down to wet its feet, and the stone is limestone,
  which means the ground under this land is full of caves. Those two facts
  explain nearly everything since: when the ice came down and the continent
  became uninhabitable, this narrow shore stayed temperate and wet, with
  shellfish, salmon, deer and bison to hand. People did not leave. They stayed,
  millennium after millennium, and painted. _Altamira no es una rareza
  afortunada: es el ejemplar más vistoso de la prueba de que aquí no se marchó
  nadie._ And with no flat ground there is no large farm, only hanging meadow —
  hence the Pasiego way of keeping cattle, a transhumance in miniature and
  vertical, the whole family moving with the cows from cabin to cabin a few
  hundred metres up and down, several times a year. _El valle no se recorre, se
  sube y se baja._
- `plot_99_saber_parar.md` — the Bay of Biscay anchovy ran out early this century
  and the answer was not a lower quota, it was closing. Five seasons with no
  fishing from 2005, the canneries living on anchovy bought elsewhere while the
  ground rebuilt, and a reopening under caps around 2010. _Lo que enseñó no es
  que la naturaleza se recupere: es que se recupera si alguien aguanta cinco años
  de no cobrar._ The same was done to the cave and more sharply: breath and warmth
  were eating the pigments, so Altamira was shut — first entirely, then under a
  regime where a few visitors drawn by weekly ballot go in for minutes in
  overalls and masks while everyone else sees an exact replica built next door.
  The most important thing here is a room almost nobody may stand in, and the way
  in is a lottery. And the estuaries were returned by plan, so the Santoña
  marshes are now a reserve for tens of thousands of migrating birds in the same
  water the anchovy comes from.

The pairing with `es_asturias` two changes ago is worth naming, since both are
green-Spain coastal cultures and the temptation was to write the same plot twice.
They came out opposite. In Asturias what returned — the forest, the bear —
returned **because the people left**, by accident. In Cantabria what returned
returned **because people decided to stop**, on purpose, and paid for it. The
unresolved half here is the other side of that same skill: knowing when, and what
for, to open again.
