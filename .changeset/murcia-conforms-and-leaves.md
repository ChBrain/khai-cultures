and the last flat Spanish file that was still under it.

**The repair.** `position_language_es_es_mc.md` arrived flat, body and declared

The tongue then moves into `packages/khai-cultures-tongues/es/` as the
eighteenth regional Spanish variety, steps 3 and 4 riding together under the
limit set at #608. Of the eighteen it keeps the most Arabic still doing daily
work: _acequia_, _noria_, _azud_, _alberca_ are not heritage words here, they
are the vocabulary for naming water that arrives, in a place that has always
had to name it exactly.

**The culture is authored** — _Es una manera de ser español, no un segundo
pasaporte._

**Two plots.** Four dead entries, and one through-line the whole culture runs
on.

- `plot_00_el_agua_que_viene_de_fuera.md` — this is the driest corner of
  continental Europe, and a river crosses it that is not from here: the Segura
  rises two hundred kilometres away in wet mountains and passes through the dry
  without belonging to it. Everything follows from that disproportion. A river
  crossing a _secano_ gives no huerta on its own — it has to be taken out of the
  channel and shared, so the azud, the acequias, the norias, and a line in the
  ground where the water stops and the scrub starts again. And water that is
  brought cannot be administered like rain: rain falls on everyone and nobody
  allots it, whereas a channel arrives at one point in an exact quantity, so
  somebody must say who irrigates, for how long, in what order. That is why this
  land is ordered by the water turn rather than by land title, and why it has
  had an irrigators' tribunal for a thousand years. Meanwhile Cartagena looks
  the other way entirely — one of the best natural harbours in the
  Mediterranean, wanted by everyone who ever ruled that sea. Half this territory
  faces its own ditch and half faces the open water.
- `plot_99_la_laguna_que_fue_a_juicio.md` — the Mar Menor went green in 2016 and
  lost the seagrass meadow holding it up; in 2019 a _gota fría_ washed years of
  accumulation into it at once and thousands of fish and crustaceans came out of
  the water to breathe and died on the shore; it happened again in 2021. The
  2020 regional law was not enough. Then a popular legislative initiative
  gathered more than six hundred thousand signatures and in 2022 the Cortes
  recognised the lagoon as a legal person — the first ecosystem in Europe with
  rights of its own, able to appear in court through guardians who speak for it.

Which is less strange here than it sounds elsewhere. This land has had a
tribunal sitting to allot water and hear complaints for a thousand years. What
changed is who sits at it: first the irrigators, then the administration, and
now the water.
