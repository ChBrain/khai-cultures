## plot_00: Donde se acaba la tierra

A man stands on the cape at the end of the afternoon watching the sun go into the
water. **Westward there is nothing** - no island, no coast, no rumour that anything is
there. Behind him is his father's holding, already split among four brothers, and due
to be split again next generation.

The Romans reached this coast and named it _finis terrae_, and it was not an
administrative metaphor: from here, as far as the world then reached, **nothing
continued.**

**But the sea gives.** The coast is not a line, it is a run of drowned river valleys
where fresh water meets salt in the lee of the storm, and that mixing makes one of the
richest shellfish grounds in Europe. In the ría you eat without a boat and without a
licence: you go down to the sand when the tide goes out.

**The land does not.** Granite, rain, and a custom that divides the inheritance among
all the children and then among all the grandchildren, until the parcel is the size of
a room. **The minifundio keeps you. It never keeps yours.**

From the first comes the shellfishing worked on the knees, belonging to the whole
parish. From the second comes the boat, and behind the boat the ship, and behind the
ship the steamer to Buenos Aires. And over all of it, **a road that ends here**: Europe
has walked to Santiago for a thousand years and stops, because past it there is nowhere
to go. **Everything arrives at this corner. Nothing crosses it.**

The Tension: a place where the road ends and the field is too small teaches two things
at once - that what feeds you is common and depends on the tide, and **that leaving is
not a failure but a stage of life.** Hence _morriña_, so ordinary that the language
keeps a word for it and the culture has made it a genre. **And the shadow: a people
that expects to leave invests in the return and not in the place.** The holding stays
small because nobody stays to make it bigger, and the best houses in the village were
built by people who no longer live in it. The _depende_ this culture answers almost
everything with is the exact prudence of someone who has learned to trust neither the
weather nor the tide nor the price - **and it is also a very fine way of committing to
nothing.**

## plot_99: Depende

Low tide, winter. The mariscadoras go down to the sand as they always have, rake in
hand and knees in the mud, and by the end of the morning the basket weighs a fraction
of what it weighed five years ago. **Nobody has done anything differently. The clam is
not there.**

In these years the thing that had never failed in Galicia failed: **the water.** The
cockle and clam harvests in the rías collapsed across 2023 and 2024 to lows the
cofradías had no record of, with rainfall and falling salinity named as the probable
cause. Shellfishing is women's work under a parish licence, and **for the first time
the problem was not how the licences were shared but that there was nothing to share.**

With the octopus the opposite happened and meant the same thing. It is scarce, the
price is up, and the boiled octopus that was the food of any romería has become a dish
you think twice about **at your own fair.** The pulpeira is still at her stall with the
cauldron; what changed is who can pay.

`Xoán` goes out regardless, carrying the ría in his head better than any chart, and
spending more of the year each year reading quotas and closures written in an
administrative language that is **neither of his two.**

And underneath, the quieter count. For the first time since it has been measured,
**fewer than half of Galicians give Galician as their habitual language**, and the fall
among children is far steeper than among the old: the tongue the Rexurdimento raised in
1863, and the Statute made official in 1981, **is being lost in the generation now being
born.**

And yet the gaita has never had so many pupils. `Rosalía` tunes and delivers
instruments from her Ourense workshop, in the most aged province in Spain, **with a
waiting list and apprentices.**

The Tension: the culture that answers _depende_ has had a decade in which it did indeed
depend. **Both certainties of `plot_00` moved at once** - the sea, which always gave,
stopped giving; and the language, which passed on without anyone teaching it, stopped
passing on. What is left standing is **what can be enrolled in.** The bagpipe has a
school, a grant, apprentices and a waiting list, and so it survives; Galician had the
street, and the street cannot be subsidised. The decade decides neither the clam nor the
playground. It leaves something more uncomfortable in view: **this place has managed to
conserve the instrument and not the tongue - and the instrument was saved precisely
because it had to be taught, while the tongue was lost during the years everyone assumed
it did not need to be.**
