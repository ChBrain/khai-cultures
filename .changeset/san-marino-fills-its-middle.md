**San Marino's plot line ran 301, 1600, 1849 - a 1299-year hole, the largest in
the house - and the hole was the writing, not the record.**

`tests/next.mjs` named this culture as the head of the queue on four counts at
once. The gap was dismissed on a first reading, because the record looked thin,
and the Human refused the dismissal: seventeen centuries without cultural change
is not a thing that happens. Three of the house's own files were already saying
so, and nobody had put them together.

- `plot_02`'s own Cue said it codified _"le sue antiche consuetudini medievali"_ -
  the line named the medieval centuries as what made this culture, and gave them
  no plot.
- `piece_balestra` and `process_balestrieri` sat in the play's Company, cast by no
  plot. A medieval tradition on the board with no scene.
- `position_language_it_sm.md` held **zero** combining marks in 338 words:
  accent-stripped Italian declaring `it`.

And there was a fourth, found only by reading: `position_culture_sammarinesita.md`
described a people _"rifugiato su una sola montagna e non ne è mai sceso"_. Four of
the nine castelli are not on the mountain. **The missing plot and the overclaim
were one omission**, and the 1463 scene could not be written while the other stood.

## The reframe, which is the house's own rule

The first plan was a spine of seven confrontations - the republic against Rimini,
the Pope, the Malatesta, the Risorgimento, the Union. That is state formation, and
`order_the_passport.md` refuses it: every Cue needs a **non-state subject**. What
replaced it is a culture of custody - of land held against a stronger neighbouring
church, of decision-making through shared office, of a widened and unfamiliar
territory, of a mountain whose stone makes identity physically legible. The Cues
now belong to an abbot, two consuls, a delegate and a household.

## Legend is not a lesser register

The 301 foundation and the words attributed to Marinus are traditional: the
_Vita Sanctorum Marini et Leonis_ survives in a manuscript tradition of the late
ninth or early tenth century, and UNESCO's own nomination material treats the
saint's words as the long-standing basis of perpetual freedom rather than as a
fourth-century record. **`plot_01` is not corrected.** A legend that defines a
people is part of what that people is, and `position_liberta` already held it
properly, as _"la memoria della fondazione"_.

What the new plots refuse is the other thing - staging a legend as though it were
a document. So:

- The Placito Feretrano is a **property judgment**, heard at Cerreto before
  Montefeltro authorities, inside the regional order rather than outside it. It
  does not declare independence, and `plot_02` does not say it does. What it
  stages instead is the culturally true part: a community's first appearance in
  writing is in someone else's inventory, and later readers made an ancestral
  proof out of a suit over fields.
- The 1243 act names **consuls**, not Captains Regent, and gives no reason for
  the pairing or the six-month term. `plot_03` keeps the anti-tyranny reading as
  the later reading it is, and does not put constitutional theory in a
  thirteenth-century mouth.
- **Faetano's mechanism is unrecoverable.** No minute, no assembly, no delegate,
  no text of accession. `plot_04` leaves the decision offstage, because a
  document can call an accession voluntary while it stays ambiguous for whoever
  lived through it.

## What the research corrected in the first draft

Four claims were carried in from search summaries and did not survive checking, so
none of them is in the prose: that the Placito declared independence; that the
mountain slid 80km to the Adriatic; that there is a Roman fort under the Cesta
tower; and that the _Vita_ is a twelfth-century text. The last inverts into the
best fact of the pass - **the legend and the first record are near-contemporaries.**
This community did not acquire an origin story centuries late. It produced one at
about the moment a bishop first wrote it down as property.

The original `plot_00` died with them. The Cava dei Balestrieri is not ancient
civic ground: it was quarried between 1884 and 1894 for the Palazzo Pubblico and
became a crossbow arena in 1971, so it proves stonecutting is materially real and
proves nothing about Marinus. It appears in `plot_99`, where it belongs.
