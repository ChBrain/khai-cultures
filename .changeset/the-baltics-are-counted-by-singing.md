**A group whose Arc says "sang themselves free" and had one plot, which was not the singing.**

The Baltics had a single plot — the 1989 chain — and a Triggers chapter naming
three themes (_The shared fate_, _The Singing Revolutions_, _The Baltic Way_) and
linking none of them. No origin, no present, no Company of its own. The material
this group is made of is almost entirely non-state, and none of it was staged.

**Four plots added and the chain renumbered to `plot_03`.**

## `plot_00` The Song Grounds, from 1869

A farmhand walks two days to Tartu to stand in a field and sing. His grandfather
was a serf, the land belongs to a German baron, the empire above the baron is
Russian, and the language he is about to sing in is what the people who own the
land call the peasants' dialect. Eight hundred and fifty singers turn up and
fifteen thousand come to listen.

Nobody is permitted to hold a political meeting; everybody is permitted to hold a
choir. The barons and governors who license it are not wrong that singing is
harmless — **they are wrong about the arithmetic.** A song celebration does not
petition or resolve. It _counts people_, and the count is read correctly by
everyone standing in the field long before anyone in a chancellery notices there
is one. The three do it separately, in three languages, two related and one not.
They are not cooperating; they are each independently discovering the same
instrument.

## `plot_01` An Hour to Pack, 1941 and 1949

Somebody knocks before dawn and a family is told it has an hour, and what you take
is decided by people who will spend the rest of their lives revising the decision.
Around forty thousand in one week in 1941; some ninety thousand in 1949, weighted
toward farming families, because the way you collectivise a countryside is to
remove the people who know how to farm it alone.

**This is where the group actually comes from, and it is worth being exact:** the
three are not a family because they are alike — two languages are cousins and the
third is not a relative, one is Catholic and two Lutheran, one remembers being a
great power. _They are a family because the same thing happened to them at the
same hour._ The fourteenth of June is a day of mourning in all three and has never
needed to be negotiated between them.

## `plot_02` The Night Song Festivals, 1987 to 1988

People drive to the grounds after work for no announced reason and sing until it
gets light, several nights running, and the number keeps going up until a crowd of
around three hundred thousand — something like a quarter of the Estonian people —
is in one field. **Nothing about it is improvised except the timing. The instrument
is a hundred and twenty years old and everybody already knows how to use it.** An
armoured column can clear a square; it can do nothing at all with a hundred
thousand people who are only singing, and everyone in the field knows the
authorities know it.

## `plot_99` The Last Cord, February 2025

The three had been independent thirty-four years and had spent all of them on
Moscow's electricity clock — whatever the treaties said, the current in a Vilnius
kitchen was kept in step from a control room in Russia. They cut it in one weekend,
together, because there is no way to do it one at a time. **The bond is an
engineering relationship now** — a gauge, a grid, a fence — conducted in
high-visibility jackets rather than in a field. Less moving, much harder to
reverse.

And the part that does not resolve: the instrument that made these nations counts
_a people singing in its own tongue_, and something like a fifth to a quarter of
the two northern countries would be singing something else. The plot does not
pretend to have an answer.

## Its own Company

- **`position_the_festival`**, declared as `laulupidu, dziesmu svētki, dainų
šventė` — one institution under three names in three languages, two unrelated to
  the third. **What the three have instead of a shared language is a shared
  method.** It orders the count over the argument and participation over
  performance, because a professional choir would prove nothing and a hundred
  village choirs prove exactly the thing in question.
- **`persona_the_singer`** — on the stand since she was nine, forty songs by heart
  without ever deciding to learn one, her grandmother's name on a list. Her Shadow
  is the live one: she speaks perfect Russian and does not advertise it, and has
  not worked out what she thinks about the neighbour who speaks only that, **only
  that she does not like being asked.**
