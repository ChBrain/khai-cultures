The Balearic Islands get a tongue and a plot line, and leave the umbrella. Fourth
of the eight.

**The third unlinked mother tongue, and the cheapest.** Both personas' mother
tongue is _el catalán mallorquín_, written as a bare phrase linking nowhere,
while their second language links correctly to `es_es_ib` — the fault found in
`es_aragon` at #664 and `es_asturias` at #666. It differs in kind and in cost:
those were **missing languages**; this is a **missing variety** of a language the
house already held. So `ca_ib` joins `ca` beside `ca_vc`, varieties 148 → 149
with the language count unchanged, and nothing rippled. Steps 3 and 4 ride
together under the limit set at #608, since only this culture links it.

What the variety holds above all is **the salted article** — _es, sa, ets, ses_,
from Latin IPSE rather than ILLE — so it is _sa casa_ and _es cotxe_ and never
the other thing, and that is what identifies this speech before anything else.
And it keeps the other article for what is solemn, _el Bon Jesús_, _la Mare de
Déu_, so the language carries inside it a gesture for telling the everyday from
what is not. What it loses is the same feature: the salted article is barely
written outside dialogue, replaced by the literary one at school and on paper, so
_el tret més propi d'aquesta parla és justament el que els seus parlants aprenen
a deixar a la porta quan la cosa va de veres._ Flagged for native review.

The culture keeps `position_insular_bilingualism.md`, which is about
co-officiality and school normalisation — the language's standing, not the office
of the tongue, the same distinction drawn for Asturian in #666.

**Two plots.**

- `plot_00_torres_para_mirar_el_mar.md` — long before anyone arrived with a name
  we can spell, the people here built dry-stone towers. Not a few: hundreds, on
  every height from which the water can be seen. The first thing built here, and
  the most built, was a place to watch the sea. Because an island in the middle
  of a sea is not isolated, it is exposed — these sit on the crossing of every
  route in the western Mediterranean, and everyone who wanted that water wanted
  them. _El aislamiento de esta cultura es verdad en un solo sentido: no puedes
  irte cuando quieras. Nunca ha significado que no venga nadie._ And the land
  gives nothing: no rivers, thin soil, and the water around it undrinkable, so
  everything that keeps a person alive here was made by hand — the cistern, the
  water gallery, and the mountain cut into dry-stone terraces to make field out
  of slope. _Un olivo de la Tramuntana crece en un suelo que alguien subió hasta
  allí._ The one thing nobody made is the Posidonia meadow, older than any city
  on earth, which fixes the sand, keeps the water clear and feeds what is
  fished. The famous coves exist because of a plant.
- `plot_99_dieciseis_millones.md` — a million residents and more than fifteen
  million arrivals a year. Not a high season: a proportion. It shows first in
  housing, where people who work the islands all year sleep in a caravan, a
  shared room or a boat, and in 2024 that reached the street. It shows
  underneath, where an aquifer drawn beyond its recharge beside the sea does not
  run out but turns salt. And it shows on the bottom, where the 2018 anchoring
  decree protects a plant that takes a century to regrow a metre from the chains
  of boats that came for the clear water the plant makes.

The two ends meet where they should. Towers were built to see who was coming;
now nobody comes in secret, and the question is not who but how many. Everything
this culture is good at — keeping water, holding a terrace, not tearing the
meadow — governs what is inside, and never what arrives.
