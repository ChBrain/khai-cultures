`npm run skeleton <culture> [--ask]` prints a culture's structure - its plots in
order, who is staged in each, and the Company's composition - in about ten lines.

Those ten lines carry every decision that matters, and they are made before any
of the two or three thousand that follow. Read as a plan, they are what to argue
about. Read on someone else's branch, they are a lens: #487's seven plots each
staging the entire Company are seven identical lines here, and were invisible in
a 2,803-line diff.

`--ask` reports overlaps with the rest of the house: a cast name in two cultures,
a year anchoring plots in two cultures, an identical structural shape. It never
fails, and none of its output is a finding. Sharing is often the truth -
Schleswig-Holstein and Denmark both anchor 1864, and that is one war seen from
two sides. It is also how a template shows through, and no counter can tell those
apart. So this mandates the asking and mandates no answer, which is the stance
the Defining Question already takes and for the same reason: a fuzzy signal made
into a hard gate becomes the next thing to satisfy.

Run over the house as it stands, it asks 248 questions. One is that 56
sub-national cultures - most of the US states, several Spanish regions - carry
an identical shape: three plots, the same Stage sizes, the same Company counts.
That question is not answered here.
