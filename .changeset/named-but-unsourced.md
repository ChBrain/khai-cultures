Record the third way a variety can fail, found by applying the rule to Frisian. The
design record already separated two: the author cannot name what the variety is, a
hard stop, and no speaker has read the prose, which `review: "native"` covers and
which ships. Frisian is neither. North Frisian and Saterland Frisian are recognised
languages with ISO codes and their own literatures, so naming them is trivial — what
stops them is that the hand at the desk cannot write them and has no source text to
work from, and unlike Sorbian nothing can check the result, because franc has models
for neither. Prose written blind and flagged `review: "native"` would be a lie by
mislabel: that flag says no speaker has read this, not the author was guessing. So it
is a provenance gate, not a review state, and the answer is a source rather than a
flag. Also records what North Frisian forced structurally — a family with no neutral
form gets no prose anchor, its varieties sit as siblings, and letting the largest
carry the family would be the same substitution this house has undone four times —
plus the Fering and Saterland features to build on when text arrives, and the finding
that Danish is anchored in the wrong place, inside `cultures/denmark`, which is
exactly what this package exists to undo. Governance only; the house ships nothing.
