## plot_00: El Tribunal de las Aguas

The Arc already names it - _"regantes asamblearios del Tribunal de las Aguas"_ - and no
plot staged it. `plot_01` is the expulsion of the moriscos in 1609, which is a wound and
not an origin.

Thursday, noon, at the cathedral door in Valencia. Eight men in black smocks sit in a
ring of chairs on the pavement, in full view of anyone walking past. A farmer stands and
says his neighbour has not respected his turn at the water. **No lawyer, no document,
nothing written down.** They ask, he answers, the other answers, the court rules. It
takes minutes, and it is obeyed.

**That court has sat like this, outdoors, at that door and at that hour, for more than a
thousand years** - through the Andalusi period, the conquest, the loss of the fueros
after Almansa and three centuries of Castilian law, without missing a week.

It exists because this coast is dry. **Everything green here is built**: the acequias
that spread one river across a whole plain, elaborated under Al-Andalus and maintained
ever since by the people who irrigate. Water does not come when you want it; it comes by
`tanda`, a measured turn and not a minute more.

**So this culture's founding problem is not land. It is the queue.** Where water is
scarce and shared, the only thing you must get right is the order - and between
neighbours who will meet again tomorrow at the same channel, the only enforceable
judgement is one that is fast, public, oral and final. The Tribunal is not folklore:
**it is the shape law takes when it is handed down by the people who will have to keep
it.**

The Tension: a people whose founding institution shares out water learns that the
commons is real and that rules about turns are the most serious rules there are - hence
the assembly, hence the síndic the irrigators elect themselves, hence the distrust of
any authority from outside the acequia that thinks it knows more about water.

**And hence the shadow, which is invisible while the system works.** A body built for one
scarce thing, shared among people who know each other, does not scale and cannot speak
about anything else. It has authority over the turn and none over the river, the
reservoir upstream, the aquifer, the coast or the sky. **This culture is admirably
organised for distributing the water that is missing, and has no institution at all for
the water that is too much. The hole has been open for a thousand years and nobody ever
had to look at it.**

## plot_99: El barro

Evening, 29 October 2024, in a huerta town south of Valencia. `Vicent` watches water come
up the street. **It is not the river: it is the barranco, dry eleven months of the year.**
In an hour it is over the cars. **The alert reaches the phone when the water is already
inside the houses.**

The DANA of 29 October 2024 was the worst natural disaster in Spain in decades: **more
than two hundred dead**, almost all in the huerta towns where the ravines meet the plain,
in garages and ground floors and cars, in streets that had not seen water in generations.
The argument about the warning began the next day and has not closed.

Then the other thing. Within days, **tens of thousands of people walked out of Valencia
on foot, crossing the bridges because the roads were gone**, carrying brooms, buckets,
boots and food, to clean houses that were not theirs. **Nobody organised it.** The
`casales` of the fallas commissions - the ordinary neighbourhood clubhouses - became
distribution centres, and paella was cooked in the open for hundreds of volunteers who
did not know each other.

In March the Fallas came. `Carme` went back to the workshop to build cardboard and write
satire, and the city thundered again. **This culture spends a year building an enormous
monument in order to set fire to it on purpose, and that year everyone felt the
difference between the two ways of losing a thing: the one you choose, and the one that
comes down the ravine.**

The Tension: **`plot_00`'s court governs the turn; nobody governs the torrent.** A
culture that solved scarcity a thousand years ago had no institution for excess, and when
it came, what worked was the oldest thing it has - neighbours, on foot, in the same
fields, without being told. **The Tribunal has no jurisdiction over a barranco. The
people who elect it turned up anyway.**

And the uncomfortable part, said plainly: they turned up because not turning up meant
nobody would, **and a society excellent at spontaneous solidarity risks letting that
excellence stand in for what should have worked first.** It is the same account that came
out of `filotimo` in greece two weeks ago in this walk: **what issues no receipt is not
measured, and what is not measured is not repaired.**

The decade closes neither the argument nor the loss. It shows one thing about this
culture and its origin: **it shares out missing water better than almost anywhere in
Europe, it had never once thought about the water that is too much, and in March it went
back to building a monument in order to burn it - because the only loss this people
knows how to administer is the one it decides on itself.**
