**Parks the second axis: cultures with no today.**

The `geo.json` ISO code is not a territorial claim. It is a mental model
answering one question - which culture would you meet if you went there today -
and that is why a country code anchors a culture older than the country.

Two classes break it, and they are not one problem.

**Resolution.** Occitania matches no ISO code; Brittany's excludes its own ducal
capital; Basque and Catalan cross the Pyrenees. These cultures exist today and
would be met today. The anchor is real and only at the wrong grain, or stops at a
frontier the culture does not. `dach` shows the machinery already exists: a
group holds several anchored cultures and says what binds them.

**Time.** The Celts, the Huns, Ancient Greece. Here the question has no meaning -
there is no going there today, and the map is a different shape as well as a
different age: Ancient Greek is the Aegean and Ionia and Magna Graecia and the
Black Sea, a culture held by a language and a list of cities and never by
contiguous territory.

The finding worth recording is that **the map is not the blocker**. The rule that
excludes these cultures is this house's own: `plot_99` is "a record of the decade
still resolving", and a culture whose decade stopped resolving in 146 BC cannot
have that file as defined. Whoever picks this up should start at `plot_99`, not
at the cartography.

Parked, not decided. The living house is 316 cultures and unfinished; a second
axis inside an unfinished first one buys nothing yet.
