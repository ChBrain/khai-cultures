**Two plots.** Four dead entries, and a name that means the opposite of what is

- `plot_00_los_ojos_del_guadiana.md` — _La Mancha_ is Andalusi Arabic for the
  parched land, and the plain floats on one of the largest aquifers in Europe.
  The Guadiana sinks into it and comes back up through holes people have called
  _the eyes_ for centuries. So the question here was never whether there was
  water but who could reach it and by what right, which is why the well — not
  the fuero — is the institution that orders this culture: everyone draws from
  the same bag and nobody can see it. And with no slopes for watermills, you
  grind with the only thing in surplus, which is unobstructed wind.
- `plot_99_el_nombre_y_la_cosa.md` — the pumps beat the rain, the eyes closed,
  and in 2009 the dried wetland burned from below when its peat caught fire
  underground and had to be fought with transferred water. With wells watched
  and irrigation limited the aquifer rose again and in some wet years the eyes
  ran — a truce, not a restoration. Above ground the plain does what it always
  did: white hundred-metre towers on the next ridge from the stone mills of
  Consuegra, harvesting the same wind for the same reason. And the two names:
  the cheese kept its designation because it never stopped being made here, and
  the saffron lost the thing while the name went on circulating without it.
