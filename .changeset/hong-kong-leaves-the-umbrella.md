## plot_00: The Fragrant Harbour

`plot_01` is the cession - the barren rock made a free port by an empire - and it is
the story the city is usually told with. The origin is older, and it is in the name.

Long before any treaty a family is born, married and buried on a boat, working the
estuary where the Pearl River meets the sea, taxed as a people apart and not permitted
to hold land. **Nothing they own is fixed to the ground.**

This coast was worked from the water for centuries before it was worked from the
shore: salt raked from the shallows, pearls dived under the Southern Han, and out of a
small anchorage on the west of the island the cargo that named the place - **incense
wood.** Heung Gong. The fragrant harbour.

**That is what the name records. Not a founder, not a dynasty, not a battle - a
cargo.** The place is named for something that passed through it on its way somewhere
else.

And the water is the whole of it. At the mouth of the river that drains the trade of a
continent lies one of the finest natural anchorages on the coast of China: deep,
sheltered both sides, never frozen. The Qing court looked at the island and called it a
barren rock, because what it counted was land that could be farmed. **The British
looked at the same island and counted the water beside it. Neither of them made the
harbour** - it was already there, already carrying other people's goods, and it had
already given the place the name it still has.

The Tension: a place named for a cargo is defined by what crosses it, and that is the
whole inheritance. **The entrepot instinct was not brought here by an empire; the
empire recognised it and put a flag on it.** From that comes everything the city is
good at - reading a trade, moving early, holding nothing so tightly it cannot be put on
a boat. And from that comes the shadow, older than any of its governments: **what is
valued for its throughput is valued by other people for what it does for them.** A port
is always somebody's asset - the court's, the company's, the crown's, the country's -
and what the people living on it might want for themselves is the last question anyone
asks. **Being useful is what has kept this place alive at every turn, and is exactly
why its own wishes have never once been the point.**

## The middle: Kowloon into plot_02

`place_kowloon` was dead, and its own Shown chapter says where it belongs: _the
peninsula that had to hold everyone the harbour could not, and did_ - resettlement
estates, rooftop schools, sewing-machine workshops. That is `plot_02`, the refugees and
the factories, which staged the industrialist and the Lion Rock but never the ground it
happened on.

Folded in, on the belgium precedent, and it corrects something real: **the factories
were not in an industrial zone, they were upstairs.** A family in one room and a
workshop in the next. The manufacturing city was built on the peninsula that took the
arrivals the Island had no room for.

## plot_99: One Bag Packed

A crew with a cherry-picker cuts a signboard off the bracket it has hung from for fifty
years. **It is not a political act and nobody argues with it**: it is enforcement of
the rules on unauthorised structures, carried out on a form, one sign at a time.

Tens of thousands have come down. **The city the world pictures as a wall of glowing
characters is losing them not to any argument, but to paperwork.**

And the people are going. After 2019 and the national security law of 2020, Britain
opened a route in 2021 to holders of British National (Overseas) status - **a document
the departing sovereign had issued as a formality, meant to confer almost nothing.**
Well over a hundred thousand used it, with more to Canada and Australia. **The exit
turned out to be the colonial passport nobody had expected to need.** The population
fell and then rose again as the government recruited from the mainland: the city is
roughly the size it was and is not made of the same people.

And yet what one might have expected to end has not. The Star Ferry has crossed since
1888 and crosses still, on fewer fares and after asking for help to keep going - while
the Jumbo, the floating restaurant that fed three generations of visitors, was towed
out in 2022 and lost at sea. Happy Valley fills on a Wednesday. And on Sunday morning
yum cha runs long, three generations deep, and **you still tap two fingers on the cloth
to thank whoever poured for you.**

**The rituals are entirely intact. The roll of who is at the table has changed.**

The Tension: the Arc says this city kept **one bag packed and one eye on the harbour**,
and that against every expectation it came to love itself as a home. **This is the
decade the bag was used.** And the inheritance turns out to run both ways - a people
descended from those who arrived with nothing and expected to leave on the next boat is
unusually well equipped to leave. `plot_02`'s refugees arrived; their grandchildren
departed; **both moves are the same instinct, running in opposite directions.**

The decade settles nothing about the fifty years, and nothing written here could. What
it shows is narrower and stranger. **The neon that made the place legible from outside
is being unscrewed by inspectors. The ferry nobody thought would need saving needed
saving. And the tea is still poured for the person beside you before yourself.** The
open question is not whether Hong Kong is changing. **It is which of those three was
the city all along.**
