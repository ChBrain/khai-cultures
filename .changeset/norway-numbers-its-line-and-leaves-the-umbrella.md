**Norway's Arc says nature, levelling and shared work, and only the state events were staged.**

The plot line ran 1030, 1814, 1905, 1969 — a saint, a constitution, a dissolution
and an oil field. All four are state moments, and between them **seven of
seventeen Company elements had no plot at all**: `dugnaden`, `friluftslivet`,
`bunaden`, `brunosten`, Nansen, and both contemporary personas. Everything the
Arc claims the culture _is_ was declared in the play and staged nowhere.

**This one renumbers.** Norway's existing numbering was chronological, unlike
Sweden's, and two of the added plots fall in the middle of it. `order_plot_zero`
puts the origin at `plot_00` precisely so a _prepend_ never forces a renumber; it
says nothing that lets an out-of-sequence middle stand. So `plot_03` becomes
`plot_05` and `plot_04` becomes `plot_07`, byte-identical, with the five inbound
links updated. The line stays a chronology.

**`plot_00` Odelen og tinget, fra 900.** Almost all mountain, so the arable land
sits scattered a day's travel apart, and no magnate can hold it. Two things grow
out of that. The odal right keeps land with the kin and can be used to redeem a
farm sold away, so Norway gets no manorial system and no serfdom — a medieval rule
that is still in the constitution. And the law is not given, it is _spoken_: at the
Gulating and the Frostating, which tradition assigns to the 900s, a lawspeaker
recites it from memory, and a king must be received by each thing separately.
Authority asks upward. **The levelling is not a temperament, it is a condition** —
nobody rose because the ground would not let them, and nobody was helped because
there was no one to help. `dugnaden` is here long before there is a word for it.

**`plot_03` Morgedal og Gudbrandsdalen, 1860-årene.** Two cottars in one decade,
neither of them trying to make a national symbol. Sondre Norheim binds the heel
with birch withies and cuts the ski narrow at the waist, so it turns where others
must brake; in 1868, past forty, he skis to Christiania and shows a city what skis
are for. Anne Hov stirs cream into the whey that was pig feed. **Both are inventions
of scarcity** — the leftover and the way up the hill — and both become Norwegian
only once the town takes them up.

**`plot_04` Bunaden 1903.** Hulda Garborg collects the valley costumes and gives
them back as something wearable. The decisive part is what she does _not_ do:
they are never merged into one national dress. You wear your own valley's, not the
country's. **National romanticism that unifies a country by leaving it a hundred
places.** She writes in landsmål, and that is the same project with the other tool
— which is why Norway ends up with two written norms and refuses to pick.

**`plot_06` Nansenpasset 1922.** The League has neither money nor authority and
needs someone the world will listen to, so it takes the man who crossed Greenland
on ski because he calculated that a party with no retreat would arrive. He
repatriates prisoners, feeds the Volga, and in 1922 gets over fifty states to accept
a paper that is _not_ a passport — a passport needs a country, and these people have
none. **The first time the world gives a person rights they have no state to claim
them with.** `fredsnasjonen` starts here: a small country making itself useful by
having no power.

**`plot_99` Hyttene og elbilene.** In 2004 NRK's listeners voted `dugnad` the
national word — not freedom, not the mountains, a word for unpaid work done
together. Over four hundred thousand holiday cabins in a country of five million,
and `allemannsretten` now grants everyone passage over outfield that more and more
people have built on. `Kari` still turns up; `Sondre` would rather pay his way out.
And the car fleet electrifies faster than anywhere, on exemptions funded by a
sovereign-wealth fund filled by selling oil and gas. **Green at home on money made
doing the opposite abroad.**

Every Cue has a non-state subject: farms too far apart to govern, two cottars with
nothing to waste, costumes in chests out in the parishes, hundreds of thousands of
people with no country, and a nation too rich to need each other.
