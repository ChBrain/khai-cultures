Kölsch and Upper Saxon, the two of Tier 1 that are structurally clean. Kölsch's Has
is not a list of sounds but a shape: it sits inside the Rhenish fan, where the High
German consonant shift crossed not as one front but as a sheaf of separate lines,
so it says ich and maache with High German and dat, wat and et with Low German —
it took from every line what reached it and from none of them everything. Its
Orders and its Loses are one feature seen twice: two tones, so two words can carry
the same letters and differ only in how the voice holds them, and no writing it has
can record that. A tongue that distinguishes words by pitch and cannot write pitch
loses at every transcription exactly what distinguishes it. Upper Saxon carries the
Entrundung and the lenition, both running through every word and both plainly
visible in writing, and its Loses is the one nothing else here has: the Meissen
chancery is part of the foundation of written German, and the region it came from is
now the one most often named as speaking German worst, so the tongue is judged by the
standard it helped build. Both exempt, both flagged for native review, and both
checked against each other for bleeding: five of five own markers, none of the
other's, twenty percent shared tokens. Twelve languages, so 0.10.0 becomes 0.12.0.
The other three of Tier 1 are held back with structural questions rather than written
badly — `wep` is Low German and belongs under `nds`, which forces that anchor split;
`gsw` sits above `swg` in the family tree even though ISO makes them siblings; and
`pfl` and the Untermain are both Rhine Franconian.
