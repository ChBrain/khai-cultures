**Lusophone comes whole, and then becomes a package.**

Unlike Visegrad, this group could not just be moved. `group_coverage` refused it
with **four findings**, and the migration would have failed the ratchet:

```
lusophone: 4 finding(s)
  4 Trigger entries link no plot: A língua portuguesa; A era das navegações; A saudade; A CPLP
  4 plots no Trigger entry chains: plot_01 … plot_04
  no plot_00 … no plot_99
```

That is the exact fault `order_the_group_ratchet.md` was written for: **four good
plots sat in the directory while the Triggers chapter carried four summaries that
linked none of them.** The chapter read as an essay about the group instead of the
chain of its scenes.

## The chain

Triggers is rewritten as **one bold entry per plot, each one a link**, and the four
existing plots are chained under their own declared names — a nação navegadora, a
travessia do Atlântico, a corte e a coroa, a língua compartilhada. Nothing in them
was rewritten; they were already there and already good.

## plot_00: A língua antes dos países

A group's origin cannot be its alliance — `order_the_passport.md` is explicit that
the obvious material for an alliance is the alliance, and here the CPLP was sitting
right there as a Trigger entry. So the origin is **older than either member**.

In a damp corner of the peninsular northwest, a jogral writes a song in the speech
of his own place rather than in Latin. It is in a girl's voice, about the friend
who went away and did not come back, and she asks the sea about him.

Between the twelfth and fourteenth centuries **galego-português** forms in that
corner, and the first great lyric of Iberia is written in it: the _cantigas de
amigo_, sung in the voices of women waiting for someone who left. Kings of Castile
write their religious songs in it, because it is the language one sings in. **None
of this belongs to either country in this group yet** — Brazil does not exist and
Portugal has barely begun.

The Tension is why the group exists at all: it rests on no treaty, and what binds
the two members **predates both of them**. But a tongue inherited and not chosen is
hard to divide, because neither invented it and both changed it. And the _cantiga
de amigo_ already carries, eight centuries before anyone argues over ownership, the
subject both will claim: **the absence of the one who left by sea, sung by the one
who stayed.**

## plot_99: A língua volta pelo ecrã

A mother in Coimbra hears her five-year-old say _você_ to her teacher, then _a
gente vai_ instead of _nós vamos_, and _tá chovendo_ instead of _está a chover_.
The child has never left the country. She learned it on the phone.

**Five centuries after the language left on ships, the current reverses.** Brazil
has over two hundred million speakers and Portugal ten, and what is made in
Portuguese on the internet — the cartoons, the games, the videos children watch —
is overwhelmingly Brazilian. Portuguese children pick up the Brazilian gerund and
the Brazilian forms of address without crossing the Atlantic. Meanwhile the 1990
orthographic agreement, made so the two would write alike, is still contested in
Portugal thirty years on.

There is no blame and no plan in it: nobody in Brazil decided to export their
speech and nobody in Portugal decided to receive it. **It happened because two
hundred million produce more than ten.** What is left open is whether a language
with two centres stays one, and whether a common orthography holds while the speech
diverges.

And it says outright what the group's own Company already admits is missing:
**Angola, Mozambique, Cape Verde and the rest speak this language and are not
staged here**, and in a world where number decides, most of this century's speakers
will come from Africa.
