Berlinisch and Moselle Franconian, and the correction that came with the first of
them. `de/position_language_de_de_be.md` was carrying `ick`, `det`, `wat`, `Kopp`,
`jut`, `ooch` and the dative/accusative merger as things the Berlin *standard* has
— the same fault Bavaria's file had before Bairisch was split out of it, the
better-known name standing in for the actual tongue. Those features are now in
`de/position_language_de_x_brl.md`, written in Berlinisch, and the standard file
keeps what actually survives into it: a vocabulary (Schrippe, Stulle, Pfannkuchen,
Sonnabend), the Schnauze register, the dial between the two, and the
hypercorrection the merger underneath leaves in speakers who reach for the
distinction on purpose.

Berlinisch earns a file on a rule that is unusual for being a list: the second
sound shift is carried out everywhere except in a closed, short set of the
highest-frequency words. `ick mache` here, `ik maak` in Low German, `ich mache` in
the standard — a clean three-way minimal pair, and `machen` is never `maken`. Its
Orders is that the list is a list and not a rule, so it cannot be read up, only
heard; and that dative and accusative fall together, so a sentence needing the
difference has to change language mid-breath.

Moselle Franconian earns one on two words. It is the band of the Rhenish fan where
the shift reached `Dorf` but not `dat`: Ripuarian to the north says `dat Dorp`,
Rhine Franconian to the south says `das Dorf`, and only this band says `dat Dorf`.
Its Orders is the Eifeler Regel, which no other German variety has — word-final
`n` drops before a consonant and stands before a vowel or `d`, `t`, `z`, `h`, `n`,
so the speaker has to know the next word before finishing the current one. Named
honestly as strongest in the north and looser southward. Its Loses is that every
name it has belongs to a region or a Land, and one of them covers two different
tongues, because the `dat`/`das` line runs through what that name spans.

Both carry `de-x-*` private-use tags on the `de-x-hes` precedent and live under
`de/`, so the language count is unchanged and the version stays `0.15.0` at 43
varieties.
