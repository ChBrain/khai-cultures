## plot_00: Езерото и патот

`plot_01` is the Ohrid literary school of Clement and Naum, the cradle of Slavic
letters, and it is already deep. The origin is deeper still, and it is the ground.

Before dawn a fisherman pulls a net on Lake Ohrid. **The fish he takes lives in no
other water on earth.** The lake beneath him is older than any people that ever
reached it, any language spoken on its shore, and any name they gave it.

Lake Ohrid is among the oldest lakes on the planet and **it never dried up.** Because
it never dried, what once entered it stayed: it holds hundreds of species found
nowhere else. **What comes into this water does not pass through it. It settles, and
becomes something that exists only here.**

Above the water ran the road. The Via Egnatia, built by Rome from the Adriatic to
Byzantium, went past this lake, and along it went everything that moved between the
two halves of the world: armies, bishops, merchants, letters, faiths. **Nobody here
lived off to one side. They lived on the crossing.**

So the ground is both at once: a road everyone uses, and a basin nothing leaves. The
peoples arrived along the road, and what remained settled the way the lake's creatures
settled - layer on layer, mixed, and no longer the same as what lives on either side.

The `охридски бисер` is that made into an object: **made from the scales of a fish
that lives only in this water, by a method a few families keep to themselves. It
cannot be made anywhere else and it cannot be copied.**

The Tension, and it is the hardest one in this walk to write honestly. A culture
formed on a crossing has depth and **no clean edges**. Everything it holds came along
the road, so every neighbour can point at a layer and call it his - **which is true,
and equally true of him.** The lake gives the more honest answer: what settles in a
basin becomes something new, and the proof that a thing is yours is not where it came
from but **that it exists nowhere else.** And there is the shadow: **a people that
must permanently prove its own distinctness spends its strength on the proving.** Each
generation is asked again for its papers - the name, the language, the church, the
history - and answering costs precisely what building would have cost.

## plot_99: Рамо до рамо

At a wedding in the west the drum and the zurla strike up and the men take each other
shoulder to shoulder: `Тешкото` begins, slow, step by step, as if hauling a weight.
**Half the line flies back next week** - Germany, Switzerland, Italy. It was always
so. **This dance was always danced by the men who were leaving, and their fathers
danced it before leaving too.**

**Twice this country changed a sign of itself to be let in.** In 1995 the sun on the
flag was changed, after Greece asked for the old one to go. In 2018 the Prespa
Agreement changed the name of the state itself - its geographic adjective - and in
2020 the country entered NATO.

**And the door to the Union did not open.** From 2020 the talks were stopped by
Bulgaria, over language and history: the same dispute `Ленче` lives every day, when
the neighbour understands her every word and calls it his own. **The country paid one
neighbour and was stopped by the other.**

Meanwhile the people go. The 2021 census, the first in nineteen years, counted about
1.8 million residents, roughly a tenth fewer than the last. **And many left on a
second passport from precisely the neighbour that disputes the identity, because that
passport opened the door their own did not.**

Ленче keeps the house, the embroidery and the church by the lake and waits for the
children at the holidays. And at `Водици`, once a year, the cold water fills with
young men diving for the cross - **one of the few days in the year when the young are
all here.**

The Tension: a people whose name, language, church and history every neighbour
disputes has now given up two of its own signs to be let in, and is still outside.
`Да бидеме свои` is the name of a plan and not of a condition. **The hardest part is
not that it paid. The hardest part is that one neighbour took the payment and another
holds the door - which proved that what is being asked for is on neither the flag nor
the name.** The decade does not decide whether the door opens. It only shows why this
dance is called the heavy one: **not slow because it is old, but because the men
dancing it are leaving - and because it is danced shoulder to shoulder, while
everyone is still here.**

**A wall caught the first title.** The plot was called `Тешкото`, after the dance it
stages, and `production-packages` refused it: a display title names one element, so a
plot and a process may not both carry it. That is a rule this session had not met
before and it is a good one - a reader who is handed "Тешкото" must get back one
thing. The plot is now `Рамо до рамо`, taken from the dance's own description of
itself, which is also what its Tension ends on.
