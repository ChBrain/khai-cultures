**Four live references named Yiddish and the house held no file for it.**
`us_new_york/persona_sarah.md` _"wears Yiddish as an overgarment"_ and links it
nowhere; `en_us_ny`, `en_us_nj` and `new_york_culture` each describe what Yiddish
did to their English - _schlep, chutzpah, klutz, kvetch, nosh_ - while pointing at
nothing; and `bar_wien` names _s Beisl und da Hawara ausm Jiddischn_. The same
fault as Navajo at `nv`, as Aragonese, and as the Bijagó that
`order_the_mother_tongue.md` still carries open.

It hangs from the anchor rather than from any culture, because **it belongs to no
country - which is the substance of the file, not an accident of filing.**

## Every chapter

|            |                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Has**    | A fusion, not a borrowing: a Semitic root set into Germanic grammatical machinery (_khasmenen_, _taynen_), plus a Slavic layer acquired later. And an orthography that **shows the seam** - Hebrew-Aramaic words spelled as they stand in the Tanakh and the Gemara, Germanic and Slavic words spelled as they sound - so the layer of every word is legible on the page. Hence three words for a guest that mean three different guests: _gest_, _orkhim_, _ushpizn_. |
| **Orders** | Know where a word came from before you can write it. The loshn-koydesh spellings follow no rule derivable from sound, so writing a note is doing historical philology without meaning to. And a choice among YIVO 1935, the Soviet orthography of 1920, and the old publishers' daytshmerish, because **no state ever ruled between them.**                                                                                                                            |
| **Loses**  | Its speakers rather than its usefulness: about **eleven million** before the war, roughly **five million** of them murdered. That is a killing, not a language death. And the argument about its own standing - fought at Czernowitz in 1908 over whether it was the national language or merely the language of exile, and answered in Palestine with placards reading _Yehudi, daber ivrit_, language patrols, burned kiosks and wrecked presses.                    |
| **Drives** | Ordinary speech carrying quotations nobody is quoting on purpose. And survival in the one direction nobody at Czernowitz planned: the speaker count is rising, and it is rising **among Hasidim**, who keep it precisely as a wall against the outside. The inheritance of people who wanted a modern secular national language now lives among people who want nothing to do with the world.                                                                          |

## Written in Yiddish, and the nv exception was considered and not taken

The house writes a tongue in its tongue - `cu` is Church Slavonic, `lzh` Literary
Chinese, `ar` Arabic. **Two exceptions exist**: `bfi`, because British Sign
Language has no written form, and `nv`, because the author could not write three
hundred words of Diné bizaad to the standard and _"a badly written anchor in a
language with a living revitalisation movement would be a harm and not a
placeholder."_

That second reason was weighed here and rejected: Yiddish has a deep written
literature and a living standard where Navajo's case turned on the author's reach.

**But the provenance names exactly where this is most likely wrong**: the pointing
and the loshn-koydesh spellings - pasekh and komets alef, pey against fey, kof
against khof, tof against sof, and the traditional spelling of each
Hebrew-Aramaic word. **An error there is self-refuting rather than cosmetic**,
because that is precisely what this file's own Has and Orders are about. Flagged
`review: native`, and if a reader judges the standard unmet, the `nv` exception is
the fallback and this file should be rewritten rather than patched.

## What this does not do

**`persona_sarah.md` still links nothing.** Making her Yiddish resolve is one line,
but `us_new_york` carries four declared-and-never-cast members, so touching it
wakes the coverage ratchet and drags the whole culture in. New York is a piece of
work in its own right - no origin, no present, four orphans - and it now has a
tongue to point at when someone does it. The same goes for the two English
positions and `new_york_culture`.

And a silence worth naming rather than fixing quietly: `israel/plot_03_tkhiyat_ha_ivrit.md`
stages the revival of Hebrew as a holy tongue returning to daily speech, and its
Tension is the sacred against the profane. **It does not mention Yiddish**, which
is what most of the people around that revival were speaking, nor the campaign
against it. Whether that belongs in Israel's plot line is not a counter's call and
is not made here.
