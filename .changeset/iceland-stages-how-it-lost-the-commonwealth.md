**Iceland's plot line jumped from the year 1000 to 1944 and never said how the state was lost.**

Four plots — 930, 1000, 1944, 2008 — and a nine-hundred-year gap in the middle
holding the one thing the Arc keeps referring to. **Eight of seventeen Company
elements were dead**: `réttirnar`, `sundlaugin`, `handritin`, `þetta reddast`,
`jafnréttið`, Snorri, and both contemporary personas. The play named Snorri
Sturluson in its cast and had no plot he appears in.

**`plot_00` Landnámið og afrétturinn, frá 874.** The island offers grazing and not
grain, and the lowland grass barely covers the winter, so the sheep have to go
somewhere else in summer and the only place left is the highland nobody can live
on. **That single fact settles how the society is built.** The `afréttur` is owned
by no one, the flocks of dozens of farms mix over unfenced country, and in
September the `göngur` bring everything down to a round pen where each farmer
reads the earmark and draws his own. None of it can be done alone, and none of it
works unless the neighbour draws honestly. The language arrives with the people
and stays put — not by decision, but because for a long time there was nobody to
change it.

**`plot_03` Snorri og Gamli sáttmáli, 1241-1262.** The assembly has no army and
nobody to enforce a verdict; that worked while the chieftain families were many
and stops working when they are few and large. Snorri — the richest of them, twice
lawspeaker, and the one who _writes_ — is killed in his own cellar at Reykholt on
the king's orders, and three words survive: _eigi skal höggva_. Twenty-one years
later the chieftains swear allegiance to Hákon one after another. **The
independence was not taken by a foreign power; it was handed over by its own
chieftains, who could not stop fighting each other.** And the same century that
loses the state writes the manuscripts that are all the country owns for the next
six hundred years.

**`plot_05` Heimaey 1973.** A fissure opens through the field east of town and the
people wake to find fire between them and the sea. The fleet is in harbour by
accident — it had been too rough to sail — and five thousand people leave in one
night on the fishing boats. Then the part nobody would have approved in advance:
seawater pumped onto running lava for months to freeze its edge into a wall, so
the harbour that _is_ the town stays open. It had never been done. It worked, and
the harbour ended better sheltered than before. **`þetta reddast` is not optimism,
it is a working rule** — no reason to decide in advance that it won't.

**`plot_06` Kvennafrídagurinn 1975.** On 24 October about nine in ten women in the
country stop work for a day, and twenty-five thousand gather in Reykjavík out of a
population of two hundred and twenty thousand. The country stops in the places
nobody had written down: banks, because the tellers are women; the telephone
exchange; the newspapers, because the typesetters are not there either. **A
demonstration by absence rather than by demand** — what women did became visible on
the day it was not done. The equality act follows in 1976 and Vigdís Finnbogadóttir
in 1980.

**`plot_99` Potturinn og tvær milljónir gesta.** Two million visitors to four
hundred thousand people. That was the way out of the crash, and it worked by
selling the country itself. The pool holds: a geothermal one in nearly every
settlement, and in the hot tub `Guðrún` and `Birna` sit in the same water without
titles — **the last room in the country where they do**. But `Birna` works in
English all day, not from carelessness but because the tools speak English, so the
state funds Icelandic into language technology. **It is 1262 in another form**:
then the question was who held the power, now it is what tongue it is exercised in.

Every Cue has a non-state subject: people following sheep off a highland nobody
