**Four tongues stop repeating the anchor above them.**

The nine United Kingdom tongues moved last release byte for byte, because
changing one character on the way out would have turned a rename into an
authoring of the culture the file left and charged that culture 19 dead Company
elements and a rename of its id. The move recorded what it found and left the
work here, where the files are the package's own and no culture is touched.

Three of them were spending their four chapters on the language they belong to,
which is the fault the `en_gb` entry named two releases ago and is the reason an
anchor exists at all. A variety with an anchor above it is free to say only what
is its own; these three were not using that freedom.

**`en_gb_eng`** held the `en` anchor entire: the doubled Germanic and Romance
vocabulary, noun-verb conversion, the phrasal verb, do-support, lost gender, the
flat `you`. Nothing in it separated England's English from Scotland's, Wales's or
Ulster's. It now holds the TRAP-BATH split, one vowel on a line running roughly
Severn to Wash, in _bath_, _grass_, _laugh_, _castle_ and _past_, words too
common to be steered around and an internal boundary no other English has;
non-rhoticity together with the linking and intrusive r, so the consonant is
dropped where the letter stands and put in where it does not; and Received
Pronunciation, the only accent in the language named for its reception rather
than for a place, with h-dropping and t-glottalling beside it as the rungs the
rest of the country is measured on.

Its Loses is the chapter the file exists for. Scottish, Welsh and Ulster English
are each taken by outsiders as a nation speaking; this one is taken as a rank, so
a speaker from Sunderland or Bristol cannot present a region without a class
arriving with it, and cannot present the standard without being heard to have
left the region behind. And because the accent has already announced the
standing, the words are free to deny it. That is what understatement is here, and
it is the Drives rather than a national trait.

**`sco_gb_nir`** was three quarters of the Scots anchor now sitting directly above
it. It now holds a standing in law no other Scots has, named in an agreement
between two states and given a funded agency, a translation service and signage,
so the tongue has a budget before it has a classroom; a written practice the
revival built on purpose to mark it off from Lallans rather than into it; and two
continua rather than one, Ulster English on one side and the Gaelic of Ulster on
the other. Its Orders is true nowhere else: the tongue is read as a badge before
it is read as speech, so there is no plain use of it.

**`ga_gb_nir`** repeated the Irish anchor in three chapters of four and had `cha`
for its own. It now holds `cha` and `chan` alive beside `ní` from Donegal to
Rathlin; the analytic first person plural, `tá muid` and `bhí muid` with the
pronoun standing as its own word where the Caighdeán folds it into the verb; and
fixed initial stress, which shortens the long vowels behind it, so the length
written on the page is not the length in the ear. Its Loses is geography rather
than grammar: `cha` and `goidé` are opaque in Connacht and Munster and ordinary
in Islay and Argyll, so this is the one Irish understood better across a sea than
across a land border.

**`cy`** was not thin, only damaged. Its circumflexes had been dropped throughout,
`cyd-destun` had lost its hyphen, two masculine nouns took `yr`, and one verb was
mis-set. Corrected; the prose is otherwise as it arrived.

All three rewrites are the office and not the map, and none of them repeats a
line from the anchor above it. The three non-English files keep `review: native`.

No language and no variety is added, so the package stays at 0.34.0 and no
dependant range moves. `build.mjs --write` re-rendered `README.md` and
`REFERENCES.md` for the new provenance.
