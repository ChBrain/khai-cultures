Aragon gets a plot line and leaves the umbrella. Second of the eight, and the
first to leave with its own mother tongue behind it.

**Two plots.**

- `plot_00_el_nombre_de_un_rio_pequeno.md` — in a high Pyrenean valley runs a
  river you can cross in two strides in summer. It is called Aragón. A few
  valleys with more stone than people took its name, and out of them came a
  crown that ended up ruling Valencia, Mallorca, Sicily, Sardinia, Naples and
  Athens. That disproportion is the question, and the answer is not force —
  Aragon never had people to spare. A place like that does not grow by invading,
  because it cannot afford to. It grows by pact. And it pacted under the rule
  that is the most valuable thing it ever produced: whoever joined kept their own
  laws. The union of 1137 tied two countries through the reigning house and left
  each its cortes, its currency and its law. What was called the Crown of Aragon
  was always a bundle of kingdoms and never a state. _Es la diferencia entre
  sumar y absorber, y aquí se eligió sumar durante cuatrocientos años._ From the
  same root comes the fuero as a condition rather than a privilege — the king's
  power holds while he keeps the bargain — and the office of a judge placed
  between king and subject, which was not a fine idea but a post with a name and
  a desk, and could therefore be cut off in one stroke when a king decided
  otherwise.
- `plot_99_de_quien_es_el_ebro.md` — there is an empty village in a Pyrenean
  valley with its roofs down. No war emptied it and no mine ran out: the people
  were expropriated for a reservoir that was never built. Aragon has the water
  and has spent a century arguing whose it is, because the dams went in the upper
  valleys and the irrigation they won was downstream or outside the region
  entirely — _la parte alta pone el sitio y la parte de abajo pone la firma_. So
  when a national plan proposed piping the Ebro to the southeast in 2001,
  Zaragoza produced some of the largest demonstrations this community has seen,
  the transfer was repealed in 2004, and four years later the city staged an
  international exposition on water. Meanwhile half the community lives in one
  city and the rest is spread across comarcas under ten people per square
  kilometre; in 2019 a single-province citizens' platform with no party behind it
  took a seat in Congress and found itself deciding an investiture, which is the
  most Aragonese way possible to raise a problem — sitting down to negotiate from
  the small side. And now data centres are arriving on cheap, dry, empty land,
  wanting the two things always fought over here: power, and water to cool with.

The two ends meet on the same question. _No es cuánta agua hay, es quién tiene
que dar su conformidad antes de que se mueva._
