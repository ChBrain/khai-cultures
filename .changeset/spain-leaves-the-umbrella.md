Spain carried **nine dead Company elements**, the most of any culture in this walk,

## plot_00: El cruce de los pueblos

`plot_01` **is** 1492, which is both the founding and the passport. The origin has to
be the ground everyone arrived on.

At a southern port they are unloading a ship. The one giving orders speaks a language
nobody here spoke two generations ago, the one carrying speaks the one before it, and
the one buying the one before that. **Nobody finds it strange to change language to
finish the deal.**

The peninsula is the far end of Europe and the door to Africa at once, and Iberians,
Phoenicians, Greeks, Carthaginians, Romans, Visigoths, Jews, Berbers and Arabs all
pass through and stay. **None arrives at an empty land and none quite erases the last
one: they stack.** Out of that stacking come Granada and eight centuries of
al-Andalus, a Romance language loaded with Arabic words, and a music that belongs to
nobody in particular because it belongs to everyone who passed. And in one northern
corner, with nobody able to explain it, a language older than all of them is still
spoken.

Two of the dead were waiting there. **`la guitarra`** says in its own Load Bearing
that it was born of _el cruce de pueblos que pasaron por esta tierra_. And
**`la familia`** says the near bond stands _above the state, work and the distant_ —
which is what a place learns when the state has changed owner that many times.

The Tension sets up the next thousand years: a country made of layers is rich in a
way that cannot be manufactured, and **never finishes agreeing which layer is the
real one.** That argument does not close in 1492, or 1978, or now.

## plot_99: El pueblo en agosto

The village has forty residents in February and four hundred in fiesta week. Carmen
sets the long table in the street for the ones who came back, **and counts the chairs
of the ones who this year did not.**

In two generations the country moved to the coast and the capitals. **Half the
territory is under twelve people per square kilometre, and whole interior provinces
are thinner than Lapland.** Villages do not close all at once: they lose the school,
then the doctor, then the bar, and go on opening one week a year.

It stages the remaining six. Carmen holds the table; **Joxepa** is still on her
caserío speaking the tongue that was here before any other; **Ane**, who has both,
translates the town-hall papers when they arrive in Castilian and decides each year
whether she is coming back next year; **la fiesta** is the whole village at once, one
week, in a village that is not there the rest of the time; **la sobremesa** is
unchanged except in who sits down and how far they drove; **el jamón** comes from a
dehesa that still produces it for a village with nobody left to carve it daily.

The Tension: what is emptying is not the land but **the daily life that made possible
everything this country calls its own** - family close by, the fiesta in the square,
the conversation without a clock. None of it is lost, **and that is the trap: it works
perfectly one week a year, and one week a year is also how a museum works.** The
decade does not ask whether the village disappears - it holds, and it even fills. It
asks whether a culture that defines itself by what is near can hold when what is near
is four hundred kilometres away the rest of the year.

without ceasing to be part.
