## plot_00: Kamen koji niko nije htio

`plot_01` is centuries of resistance - _the land that never fell under the Turks_ -
which is this culture's proudest sentence. The origin is the ground that sentence
stands on, and reading it honestly complicates it.

A man herds among stone on a plateau above the sea. Soil only in the sinkholes, by
the handful. No plain, no navigable river, **no road you could drag a cannon up**. From
the sea below the mountain looks dark, and that is what foreigners will name it.

This mountain is karst. Water falls through rock and vanishes, hay is carried on
backs, and grain feeds you only as far as the winter. So people here always lived on
little, and always went down for the rest.

**And that is why it stayed free.** The Ottomans took the plains, the towns, the ports
and the roads - everything worth holding - and left the stone. Holding this mountain
meant feeding an army where an army cannot be fed. What is remembered afterwards as
_the land that never fell_ was both things at once: **rock that was defended, and rock
it was not worth taking.**

And where there is no lord, a people orders itself. The mountain divides into tribes
and brotherhoods, judgement is given at assembly, and order is kept by unwritten law
and the given word. At a gathering they take up the `oro`: **no instrument at all,
only clapping and sung couplets**, until a young man leaps into the ring, spreads his
arms like wings and flies like an eagle. Nothing there to buy and nobody to pay. All
it needs is people in a circle and a voice.

The Tension: freedom that comes out of poverty is real freedom and a trap at once.
Real, because people here truly did live without a foreign master when everyone around
them did not. A trap, because **the pride of never having fallen becomes the measure
of everything, and a people that measures itself by what it resisted struggles to say
what it is when nobody is attacking.** And there is one more thing: **the same stone
that kept the empire out kept everyone in.** Tribes answerable to nobody were not
answerable to each other either, so what was called heroism toward a stranger was
easily called revenge toward a neighbour. **This mountain learned to defend itself
against the foreign before it learned to agree with its own - and that bill only falls
due once there is no foreigner left.**

## The middle: Marko Miljanov and the poem into plot_02

`plot_02` is Njegoš, the prince-bishop who put the highland code into verse. Two dead
elements belong there and nowhere else, and adding them makes the plot say something
it did not.

**The code is saved from oblivion twice, and the second time by a man who is not a
poet.** Marko Miljanov, chieftain of the Kuči and a veteran of countless fights,
stayed illiterate into old age and then, already old, **learned his letters in order to
write down `Primjeri čojstva i junaštva`.** A sword that became a pen - and the one
definition this people quotes everywhere: _heroism is when you defend yourself from
another; čojstvo is when you defend another from yourself._

`Gorski vijenac` and that book together put the mountain's oral law on paper - **in a
language whose name divided nobody at the time.** Which is the whole of what comes next.

## plot_99: Popis

The enumerator sits in a stone house on the plateau and reads `Milena` the questions.
She answers the one about religion at once. She pauses at the one about nation. **She
is silent longest at the one about language** - because the very words she will answer
in are called something different by her grandchildren than by her.

In 2006 Montenegro voted itself back into independence with 55.5 per cent. Seventeen
years later the 2023 census counted its people and returned the same account, only
more plainly. By nation, about two in five declared themselves Montenegrin and about
a third Serb. **By language, more named their language Serbian than Montenegrin - in
a state called Montenegro whose official language is Montenegrin.** It is the same
speech: the same words, the same decasyllable, the same gusle. The difference is not
in what is spoken but in what it is called, **and that difference does not run between
regions, it runs through houses.**

Before the census came the church. The 2019 religion law brought processions this
country had never seen and brought down the government that had taken it into NATO.
There too it was not one half against the other across a border, but relative against
relative.

And up on the stone, everything goes as it went. Milena keeps the house, the hearth
and the grave and waits for her children at the slava; the children are down on the
coast, where tourists pay better than the mountain ever did, or overseas. At the feast
the `kapa crnogorska` comes out, black band and red crown, the sign of who you are and
where you are from - **and it is worn by the grandson who calls his language something
other than his grandmother does. The cap does not object.**

The Tension: a people that won its freedom on stone and measures itself by what it
resisted now has nothing to resist, and the bill from `plot_00` falls due - **it is
harder to agree with your own than to defend yourself from a stranger.** `Biti svoj` is
the name of a plan, not of a condition. Neither census nor referendum settles the
decade, because the question is not put between two sides of a border but inside one
house, **and what is actually divided in that house is not the speech - the speech is
identical - but only its name. Which is the hardest form of discord there is: the kind
in which both sides understand each other perfectly.**
