## Both are anchors, and nothing is invented

Read against the mnemonic before moving. Neither culture held a variety of a
language; each held the language.

**Polish** carries a seven-case declension that puts a word's role in its own
ending, leaving word order free to serve emphasis rather than grammar; the
perfective/imperfective pair, two separate words where other languages have one
tense for a finished and a continuing action; graded diminutives, _kot_ then
_kotek_ then _koteczek_; and the masculine-personal plural, which will not let the
speaker describe a group without saying whether there are men in it. No article at
all, so definiteness is read from order and context.

**Slovak** carries six rather than seven, the same aspect pairing, the same absent
article, and diminutives that fit a degree of tenderness inside one word. What is
its own is the **Loses**: its closeness to Czech, which a Slovak understands
without being taught, dissolves the difference between the two in ordinary speech
— the speaker reaching for a Czech word without noticing. That is a loss no other
Slavic file in this package records, and it is the reason the two do not read as
one file with the numbers changed.

So `pl` and `sk`, no `pl_pl` and no `sk_sk`, on the `da` and `cs` precedent.
