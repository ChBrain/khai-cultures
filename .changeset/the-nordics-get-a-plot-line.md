**The Nordics had no plots at all.**

Nineteen groups were written with a `## Triggers` chapter of prose blocks that
link no plot, which `management/design/groups-and-references.md` §2.1.1 recorded
as a measured defect, and six of them had zero or one plot on disk. The Nordics
were the worst case: five members, a decent Arc, and **not one plot file**.

The arc had the other problem the design record warns about as well. It said "the
model and the north", which is what the five are famous for rather than anything
they did together, and a group whose arc is its reputation is the alliance-is-the
alliance failure in another coat.

**The spine is reading, and what the north could say to itself once everybody
could.**

- **`plot_00` The Thing and the Danish Tongue, 930 to 1300.** Free men ride to a
  plain and make law in the open by argument, and they do it everywhere in the
  north and in one language whose own speakers call it _dǫnsk tunga_ in Iceland
  and Norway as readily as in Denmark. Everything the world credits to the
  twentieth century here is eight hundred years older: deciding by assembly, in a
  language your neighbour can follow without study.
- **`plot_01` The Household Examination, from 1686.** A church law makes the parish
  priest test every household on its reading once a year and write the result in
  the register. Confirmation requires reading, marriage requires confirmation, and
  the incentive reaches every farm including the ones with no interest in the
  catechism. Within a century the poorest corner of Europe is the one that can
  read, and still cannot write: reading ran a hundred years ahead of writing, so
  what the north built first was not a literate public but an enormous audience.
- **`plot_02` The Movement That Failed and the Understanding That Did Not, 1840 to 1864.** Students find they can each speak their own and be understood, build a
  political movement on it, and Denmark goes to war in 1864 expecting the brothers.
  The brothers do not come. The politics dies in a season; the comprehension does
  not, and a century later becomes a written right.
- **`plot_03` No Passport, from 1952.** Free movement and a common labour market
  five years before the treaty of Rome, presented as housekeeping. The institutions
  built alongside are deliberately powerless, which is precisely what 1864 taught
  the north to design.
- **`plot_99` The Meeting Switches to English, since 1990.** The young of the three
  central countries follow each other worse than their parents did and English
  better, and the room changes language out of courtesy. Meanwhile everything else
  converges, and in 2023 and 2024 the last two holdouts join the same alliance as
  the others.

The group gains its own **position**, `the neighbour tongue` — a language you were
never taught and are expected to follow, with an asymmetry it does not advertise:
a Finn or an Icelander who wants the room learns a Scandinavian language and the
three inside learn nothing, which is the least egalitarian arrangement in the most
egalitarian region on earth. Its **persona** is the Interpreter, the Finn who had
to be taught the thing the others think is free, and who is the only one in the
room who feels the switch to English as a loss. And a pitch.

Every Cue has a non-state subject: men riding to an assembly, a priest at a
kitchen table, students at a meeting, a welder getting on a boat, and four young
people giving up and speaking English.

No play is added and no culture moves, so the registry stays at **0.319.0**, 319
cultures and 21 groups. `references` verified after the build: the five members and
nothing else.
