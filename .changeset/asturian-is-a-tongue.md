So the house had a position about the movement to defend Asturian and no
position for Asturian. Step 3 on its own again, before the culture moves.

**Asturian** is Asturleonese, spoken by perhaps a couple of hundred thousand
people. What it holds that Castilian does not: the **neuter of matter**, where
what is counted and what is weighed do not take the same agreement — _la lleche
ta frío_ — so a speaker must decide in every sentence whether the thing named is
a piece or a quantity. Latin L- gone to ll- (_lluna_, _lleche_, _llobu_). The
feminine plural in `-es` (_les cases_, _les vaques_), audible from a distance and
impossible to hide. Enclitic pronouns in main clauses, _díxome_, as in Portuguese
and Galician. And a past that needs no auxiliary: _fui_, not _he díu_.

What it loses is the part worth stating plainly. It is not official in its own
territory — it has an academy, a grammar and a dictionary, is taught as an
optional school subject, and the attempts to change its status have failed one
after another. The sharpest fact is comparative: **the same language, in its
Mirandese variety, is co-official in Portugal.** It lost its own name, too — many
who speak it say they speak _bable_, or that they do not speak Asturian but "the
way we talk here", because they were taught that what they had was bad Castilian.
And it loses inwardly, since anyone who writes it is choosing between the
academy's norm and what is said in their own parish, and makes that choice not
once but on every page.

Its closing line is the third in a set now. Esperanto: a language nobody was born
into. Aragonese: a mother tongue becoming a chosen one. Asturian: _ye una llingua
que-yos enseñaron a nun contar_ — a language many of its best speakers do not
know they are speaking, because they take what they have for a rough way of
speaking something else.
