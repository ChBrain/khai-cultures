**`npm run next` named two faults and asked one question before either could be fixed.**
`de_saxony_anhalt` sat at the head of the queue on the "wrong" rung: the line ran backwards
somewhere, and it carried 473 years of span with a **408-year hole** between 1517 and 1925.
The question it attached: _is the hole the record being silent, or nobody having written it?_

## The backwards edge

The chemical triangle opens in **1916** and was numbered after the **1945** Land. Not a typo:
its Cue was written from the Land's point of view - _the newly formed Land inherits a
landscape_ - so the plot could only sit after the thing that inherits it. It now opens on its
own date, with the works going up on the Börde to make ammonia and out of it explosive, and
it sits where 1916 belongs.

## The hole, and who was already pointing at it

Not the record being silent. The house itself was pointing into the gap, in the Magdeburger
Reiter's own yearbook:

> der fast vollständigen Zerstörung Magdeburgs im Dreissigjährigen Krieg 1631

Named by a piece, staged by no plot. Four plots now fill it:

| when      | the subject of the Cue                                                 |
| --------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1491      | salters boiling brine over four springs, and the brotherhood they form |
| 1631      | an imperial army storming a city after three refused ultimatums        |
| 1654      | a man pumping the air out of a copper sphere                           |
| from 1765 | a prince laying out a park without a fence, as an instruction          |

1631 and 1654 are one man's life: Otto Guericke sat on Magdeburg's council in 1630/31 and was
one of its four mayors from 1646 to 1676. The person who recorded the dead is the person who
rebuilt the city and then showed that two teams of horses cannot pull apart a sphere with
nothing in it.

## Halle had no place file

In a Land whose Arc names Halle, whose largest city it is, and whose `plot_99` turns on a
door in that city. It has one now, and so does Wörlitz.

## And the spelling

This culture wrote **ss where German takes ß** - `Dreissigjährigen`, `mittelgrosse`,
`Ausstoss` - across 35 files. Corrected on stems where `ss` is never right in standard
orthography; `dass`, `muss`, `Anschluss`, `Fluss` and `Wasser` are untouched. One
over-correction was caught and reverted in the same pass: `geschossen` keeps its `ss`, since
only `Schoß` takes the `ß`.

Every date is checked against a public source and recorded in `REFERENCES.md`, which now also
answers the Cue-subject question per plot so the next hand does not have to ask.
