Westphalian, Alemannic and Palatine — the three held back last time as structural
questions rather than prose problems, and one rule settled all three: the directory
is the ISO code the file declares, the members tree is composition and not descent,
and linguistic descent lives in the prose. One of the three blockers was also simply
wrong — `nds` needed no anchor split, because it was already written at family level
rather than as Schleswig's variety. Westphalian carries the westfälische Briäkunge,
a named categorical change running through the whole language, and its Loses is
doubled: it loses to High German like all Low German, and again to its own family,
because whoever learns Platt learns the northern kind that has no breaking. Alemannic
carries the old monophthongs, Huus and Zit and Lüt, and says in its own text that
Swabian is a branch of it and not its neighbour — the registry makes them siblings,
linguistics does not, and the truth belongs in the prose rather than encoded into the
structure. Palatine shares isch, des and the intervocalic w with the Untermain,
because both are Rhine Franconian and shared features are supposed to overlap; what
separates them is one morpheme, -el against -che, and the file names its neighbour's
ending to mark where it stops. Fifteen languages, so 0.12.0 becomes 0.15.0.
