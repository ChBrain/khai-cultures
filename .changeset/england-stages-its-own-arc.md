**England had a cultural Arc and a constitutional plot line, and this closes the
gap between them.**

The Arc says fair play, the amateur, understatement, the shrug, the green and
pleasant land, and an old dissent from below. The plot line said 1066, 1381,
1534 and the mills: a conquest, a tax revolt, a break with Rome, and an economy.
Six of the seventeen Company elements were the Arc's own material and were staged
nowhere at all, which is the passport failure in its cleanest form and the reason
`company-coverage` had England at six dead.

Five plots are added. The four that existed are unchanged except that plot 4 now
carries its date.

**`plot_00` The Angelcynn, from 450.** Rome's administration goes, Angles, Saxons
and Jutes settle among the people already there, and over three centuries a people
and a tongue become one. Bede writes the history of a single _gens Anglorum_ in
731 with seven kingdoms and no king over them; Alfred writes of the _Angelcynn_
and of _Englisc_ while Wessex is fighting for its life. The kingdom arrives in 927
and takes the name that was already there. England is one of the few countries
whose people and language are older than its state, which is why a conquest could
change who owned the land without changing what the people were called.

**`plot_05` The Laws Written in Taverns, 1863.** Eleven London club secretaries
take a room at the Freemasons' Tavern and argue for six meetings about whether a
man may run with the ball in his hands. The ones who want to walk out and make a
second code. The country codifies its games before it codifies much else, sends
the codes to the world, and gets back the sharpest insult in its language for
conduct having nothing to do with sport. `Gentlemen v Players` runs from 1806 to
1962, amateur against professional, for years through separate gates. This is the
plot that stages [village cricket](../packages/khai-cultures/cultures/gb_england/process_cricket.md),
the pub, fair play and the amateur, and its Tension is that the same act gave
England its best argument for playing fairly and one of its neatest instruments of
class.

**`plot_06` The Mass Trespass on Kinder Scout, 1932.** Four hundred ramblers out
of Manchester walk onto a private grouse moor; five go to prison, one for six
months, sentenced by a jury including two brigadier generals. The Act comes in
1949 and the Peak District is the first national park in 1951. The green and
pleasant land the English keep as a self-image was, for most of them and most of
its history, somebody else's property.

**`plot_07` The Flag Comes Back, 1996.** The crowd at a tournament held in England
picks up the red cross on white instead of the union flag it had waved for
decades, and does not put it down. The year after, Scotland and Wales vote for
parliaments. A flag is not an answer, but it is the first time in living memory
the question is asked out loud, by a crowd rather than by a constitution.

**`plot_99` The Question Nobody Answers, since 1999.** The local shuts, and about
a quarter of the country's pubs have gone since 2000. The other three nations get
bodies of their own; the North East is offered one in 2004 and rejects it by close
to four to one; English votes for English laws goes into the Commons rules in 2015
and out again in 2021. Edith lays on the teas, Liam keeps the round going, and
neither is waiting for a constitution. A record and not a forecast: where it
points is in the Tension and the Stakes and nowhere else.

Every Cue in the five was written against the passport test, _name the subject of
the Cue_, and not one of them is the state or an instrument of it: settlers, club
secretaries, ramblers, a crowd, and a pub closing.
