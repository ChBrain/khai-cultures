**Denmark's play staged the state and left the culture out, and the migration
could not happen until that was fixed.**

The Arc says equality, trust without counting, security and the hyggelige over the
great victory. The plot line said 965, 1849, 1864 and 1943: a baptism, a
constitution, a defeat and an occupation. Six of the eighteen Company elements were
the Arc's own material and were staged nowhere — hygge, pyt, the du-form, rugbrød,
and both contemporary personas.

**And this is the change that discovered why that mattered for packaging.** A
migration adds `package.json`, `LICENSE`, `LICENSE-CODE` and
`playwright_instructions.md`, and an addition is not a relink, so **every migration
authors its culture**. Ireland passed in #574 only because it was already at zero.
Attempting the five Nordic moves as a batch fired `company-coverage` at once. The
two ratchets are composing correctly rather than fighting: a culture cannot be
published as a package while its Company is unstaged. So the order that already
covers this applies, `order_the_migration_ratchet.md` — the pull request that
carries the edit carries the move — and Denmark gets both in one.

Four plots are added; the four that existed are untouched.

**`plot_00` Danevirke og bælterne, fra 500.** A people on a peninsula and four
hundred islands take the boat where others take the road, and have exactly one
place anyone can reach them over land. They close it: thirty kilometres of rampart
across the neck of Jutland, built in phases from the fifth century with one great
phase the tree rings date to 737. No village builds that. **The earthwork is the
evidence of a coordinating authority two centuries before any king is named in the
sources** — authority read off a ditch rather than off a name. The rampart stands
in another country today. It is the same border the play loses in 1864, and it was
built before there was a Denmark to lose it.

**`plot_05` Kvinderne og vuggestuerne, 1960 til 1975.** Joint taxation of spouses'
earnings falls around 1970 and her wage stops being stacked on his; the
municipalities build nurseries at a rate nobody has seen. Inside fifteen years the
two-earner household goes from exception to norm. The Tension is the one the
country does not tell about itself: **a large part of the trust it calls innate was
built, in concrete, by local authorities, over two decades.** That is a more
comfortable story than it sounds, because a thing that was built can be maintained
— and it follows that it can also be defunded.

**`plot_06` Du-reformen, omkring 1970.** A whole speech community drops a pronoun
in about a decade. No law, no decree, no language board, not even a single date to
point at as the Swedes have. Afterwards almost nobody remembers deciding anything.
One exception is left standing, the royal house, and it is now the only thing that
marks it. The cost is that politeness lost its instrument: distance and gravity
have to be carried by tone and word choice, and a language where everyone is `du`
has no easy way to be formal on the rare occasion it needs to be.

**`plot_99` Lyset klokken tre, siden 2015.** It goes dark at three in November and
somebody lights a candle at a table with no guests. Around 2016 the word goes out
into the world in a stack of books and comes back as something Danes are asked to
explain and cannot, because it is not an interior style but an agreement that
nobody makes a scene. `Pyt` acquires a button — a real one, on a board in
classrooms, for a child to hit when something has gone wrong and cannot be undone:
a technique for putting a thing down, taught rather than inherited. The country
stays at the top of every trust measure and has tightened its immigration policy
comprehensively and across the middle of its own parliament, and the two facts are
discussed separately far more often than together.

Every Cue has a non-state subject: people who take the boat, a married woman doing
the arithmetic on her own wage, a shop assistant being addressed, and a candle lit
at three.
