The persona-wiring ratchet: the two edges of the wiring triangle that a script
can actually hold.

- **A grip needs a tongue.** A Projection that says how well someone speaks and
  never says what has named a width with nothing under it. Ten open, in five
  cultures.
- **A tongue nobody acquires first is nobody's mother tongue.** `de_ch` says in
  its own text that it is written and read aloud and hardly spoken; the flag
  `khai.wiring.noMotherTongue` says it where a script can read it.
- **A width must be one the engine ships.** Invented widths fail.

Neither rule is typed in the gate. The widths come from the language engine's
manifest (18 leaves of its members tree) and the tongues from the tongues
package's `khai.wiring`, because a rule written in two places will disagree with
itself. A vitest guard asserts only that both readings still find something — a
rule read as an empty set is a gate that has gone quiet without going red.

What is deliberately absent: whether a Projection repeats what its tongue says.
That was tried and cannot be decided by a script, because no check separates a
persona naming its tongue from one describing it. It lives in the packages'
playwright instructions, applied while the prose is written.
