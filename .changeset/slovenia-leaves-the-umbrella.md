## plot_00: Voda, ki gre pod zemljo

`plot_01` is Trubar's books of 1550, and 1991 is `plot_04`. The origin is older
than both and is the ground.

Slav peoples settle in the sixth and seventh centuries at the **lowest gap through
the Alpine wall**, between the Alps and the Dinarides, where the Roman roads ran
and where every army afterwards would pass. The ground there is limestone, and the
water on it behaves in a way they have not seen: a river reaches the edge of the
plateau, sinks into the rock, and is gone.

**That ground gave the science its word.** _Kras_, the plateau between Ljubljana
and the gulf of Trieste, is why the world says karst - and _dolina_, _polje_ and
_ponor_ went with it. A small nation's one universal export is a Slovene word for
ground that hides its water, used daily by geologists who do not know whose it is.

And the ground does what it says. The river at Škocjan goes under and comes back
up in another country under another name. The water reaching Ljubljana arrives
with several names because it sinks and returns more than once, and it is the same
water. Cerknica is a lake for half the year and a field for the other half.

So the people on the crossing road learn the first thing, long before they have a
lord or a state: **on a road everybody marches down, you do not last by standing
across it - you go under and come back up.** In those same centuries Slovene
separates from its neighbours and keeps the dual, the smallest thing in a language
and the easiest to lose.

The Tension: a people that settles on a corridor learns to endure and not to rule,
and everything later follows - a thousand years of foreign lords without one
dynasty of its own, a book before a state, and in 1991 an exit almost without
blood, because this nation never counted on surviving by fighting. **The price is
that whoever learns to hide and wait finds it hard to stand in daylight and say
what it wants - and while it is under, nobody, itself included, can see whether it
is still there.**

## plot_99: Voda, ki se vrne

The origin and the present are the same fact, once each way round.

The first weekend of August 2023 drops a month of rain in two days. **Marija stands
in front of her house and looks at a stream she has never once had to look at.** It
is not in its bed; it is in the garden, the cellar, the road. **The ground that
swallowed water all her life cannot take it.**

The floods are the worst natural disaster of independent Slovenia: two-thirds of
the municipalities, bridges and roads gone, valleys in the Savinja and Koroška cut
off, houses filled with gravel. Slovenia asks the EU for civil protection
assistance and gets it. In many places what is left standing on a homestead is
**the kozolec - a wooden thing built so something in a rainy country would stay
dry, standing in the water.**

Then the thing the weeks are remembered for. Thousands of people drive into the
valleys with shovels, tractors, diggers and vans, unasked and unpaid, mostly from
places the water never touched. **Luka does not go up to the mountains that
weekend; he goes down, into a valley he does not know, and digs mud out of the
cellars of people he has never met.** Nobody calls it heroism: `pridnost`, which
normally works quietly in the garden and the hive, is for a few weeks entirely out
on the road and visible. The state introduces a solidarity levy later; the people
were there first.

The decade also runs elsewhere and more quietly - the young crossing the Austrian
and Italian borders for work and coming home for holidays, villages ageing, the
lakes and Ljubljana filling with more visitors each year, English falling into the
Slovene of the young and the dual thinning at the edges. **None of that is a
disaster, which is exactly why none of it is seen the way the water was.**

The Tension: the nation that survived for centuries by going under cannot hide
this time. For a few weeks it shows that it holds together without being ordered
to, which is precisely what `plan_majhni_a_svoji` wagers on. But **the rebuilding
takes years and not a weekend**, and it is not known whether a people excellent at
the surge of mutual aid can also persist in the long haul. The decade does not
settle whether smallness is an advantage or a fear: only that there were more
people on the road that August than ever before, and that a few months later they
were each back in their own garden.
