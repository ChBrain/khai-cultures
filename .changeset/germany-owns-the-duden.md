**Germany owns the Duden.**

A group needs what a group needs, and DACH's arc is the making of a shared
written standard - which cannot be staged without the dictionary it was built
on. The dictionary is not the group's: Konrad Duden was a schoolmaster in
Schleiz and the book is German. The ownership rule in
`management/design/groups-and-references.md` says a group adds a file only when
no member already owns it, so the fix is here rather than there, and DACH will
cast it by reference.

It lands in `plot_03_reichsgruendung`, where it belongs on its own merits and
not only because a group asked. That plot already collects the Reich ordering
its interior by binding things to a proof: the Meisterbrief of 1897 tying craft
to a certificate, the railway time of 1893 tying punctuality to an agreement.
The Duden is the same move applied to writing, and it arrives from the opposite
direction - not decreed, but assembled by a schoolmaster tired of every school
correcting differently, and adopted afterwards.

That also answers a standing criticism of this culture in
`order_the_defining_question.md`: Germany's cast was two churchmen and
statesmen, two chancellors and two contemporaries, staging every chancellor and
no craft, against an Arc that rests on `Können statt Rang`. Ships no released
content change.
