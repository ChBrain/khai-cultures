**The archipelago becomes a group, and the group is named for the fact that it has
no name.**

Ireland and the four nations, two states and five cultures on one archipelago.
This is the group Ireland was lifted to a package for, and it is the last piece of
the sequence that ran tongues, culture, migration, group.

**The name is the hard part and the group is named for it rather than around it.**
_The British Isles_ is the ordinary term in Britain and is avoided by the Irish
state, so a group named that would be a file arguing with one of its own five
members. _The Atlantic Archipelago_ is a coinage nobody says. Naming it after the
British-Irish Council would name an institution, which is the failure
`order_the_passport.md` describes. **_These islands_** is what both governments
actually reach for when they need a word for the whole, and it is the register the
1998 Agreement used. The group takes the workaround as its title and stages the
need for one: it is the only group in the house whose members do not share a word
for the group.

**The spine is movement, not the Council.** The obvious material here is Strand
Three and the Agreement, and the Agreement is already staged where it belongs, in
`gb_northern_ireland`. What the five actually did together is move among each
other and then decline to stop.

- **`plot_00` One Written Language, 1200 to 1650.** The learned classes of Ireland
  and Gaelic Scotland keep one literary standard across the water for four
  centuries, taught in schools that take years and carried by hereditary families
  who cross as their patrons require. A poem composed in Munster is read in Argyll
  without translation. Both states end it within two years of each other, after
  1607 in Ireland and by statute in Scotland from 1609, for the same reason: a
  learned class that answers across a sea answers to nobody in particular.
- **`plot_01` Liverpool and the Clyde, 1845 to 1852.** The crossing that got the
  songs went to America; the crossing that made these islands cost a few shillings
  and took a night. In the worst year the nearest port receives more arrivals than
  it has residents.
- **`plot_02` The Navvies and the Nurses, 1945 to 1970.** A generation leaves for
  the building sites and the wards of a country that is advertising for them in
  Irish newspapers and specifying in its windows who need not apply, and does both
  on the same street.
- **`plot_03` The Arrangement Nobody Signed, from 1923.** Two states that have just
  finished fighting agree not to check anybody's papers and write almost none of it
  down. It survives a neutrality, a departure from the Commonwealth, a joint
  accession and a Brexit, and it grants what almost no two sovereign states grant:
  citizens of each vote in the other's elections.
- **`plot_99` A Border in the Sea, since 2016.** The frontier goes into the water
  rather than onto the road, and the movement of people is never seriously on the
  table. In 2019 the two governments write the arrangement down for the first time
  in ninety-six years, having discovered they could not point to the document.

Its own position, **the neighbour**, is a status with no other example: not a
foreigner, not a fellow citizen, settled in the law of both states rather than in a
treaty between them. Its Orders is to hold a grievance and a familiarity at once
without letting either cancel the other, and to choose a word for the whole every
single time, which nobody does consciously and everybody does. Its persona is
**the Cousin**, who has two passports in the same bag and takes whichever queue is
shorter.

It shares four members with `the_four_nations` and no material: that group stages
what the four built with each other inside one state, this one stages what five
cultures did across a sea.

Every Cue has a non-state subject: a poet taking service across the water, a
family selling up for a deck passage, a woman answering a newspaper advertisement,
a man walking off a boat at Holyhead, and a queue for passports that is not at a
border.
