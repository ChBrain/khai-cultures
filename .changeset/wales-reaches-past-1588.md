**Wales stopped in 1588, and seven of its eighteen Company elements were staged
nowhere.**

The play ran Llywelyn, Glyndwr, the Welsh Bible and Coal and Bevan. Everything
the Arc rests on after the Bible was in the Company and in none of the plots:
Gwen, Rhys, Y Ddraig Goch, solidariaeth, y capel, the eisteddfod and rugby. Five
plots are added; the four that existed are unchanged except that plot 4 now
carries its span.

**`plot_00` Cymry and Wealas, from 550.** The Britons of the west begin calling
one another by a word meaning _the people who belong together_ at the same time
as the newcomers begin calling them a word meaning _the strangers_, and both names
stick for fifteen hundred years. The country is known to the world by a name
meaning _not us_ and to itself by a name meaning _each other_. In the same century
the tongue separates out of British, and the oldest poetry in any living tongue of
these islands is an elegy for a war-band out of Din Eidyn cut down at Catraeth.
_Hiraeth_ is not something this culture picked up from its defeats: it is in the
first verse it has, when the loss being mourned was still the Old North.

**`plot_05` Brad y Llyfrau Gleision, 1847.** Three English barristers who speak no
Welsh report that the Welsh are ignorant, dirty and immoral and name the cause as
the language and the chapel. Wales answers not with a rebellion but with
respectability: the Sunday schools teach a population to read, the chapels swell,
and the eisteddfod is rebuilt into a national institution so the nation can be
seen doing in public the exact opposite of what the blue volumes said of it. The
cost is written into the culture, and the argument that English is the road out
was never actually defeated; the parents heard it and acted on it.

**`plot_06` The Match and the Anthem, 1905.** The crowd answers the haka by
singing, taken to be the first national anthem sung before a sporting fixture
anywhere, and Wales beat the New Zealanders by a single try. A nation with no
state finds a body to be a nation in, and it is an eighty-minute one. The badge
says the whole thing: the crowd flies the dragon of a people who were never
anyone's possession, and the team runs out wearing the feathers of the prince who
took the country.

**`plot_07` Aberfan, 1966.** The tip comes down on Pantglas Junior School; a
hundred and forty-four die and a hundred and sixteen are children. The tribunal
blames the Coal Board in terms nobody expected and not one person is prosecuted,
dismissed or demoted. Then the Board takes a hundred and fifty thousand pounds out
of the fund the public had given the village, towards clearing the tips. Repaid in
1997, and made good again by Cardiff in 2007. Solidarity underground was a
bargain, and this is the day the industry's half of it was shown to be nothing,
thirty years before the pits closed.

**`plot_99` The Count and the Congregation, since 2011.** The Senedd gets its
law-making powers by nearly two to one. Then the country counts itself: 538,000
Welsh speakers in 2021, 17.8 per cent, down from 19, against a declared national
aim of a million by 2050. Wales is the only one of the four with a target for its
own language written down, which means it is the only one that will be able to say
precisely how it did.

Every Cue was written against the passport test, and the subjects are a people
being named, a child with a piece of wood on a string, a crowd in a stand, a
classroom of children under a tip, and a chapel with a notice in the window. Not
one is the state.

`position_culture_cymreictod.md` gains the conformance links and they carry their
weight: Wales is the way of being British that was annexed rather than joined,
made part of the realm by statute two centuries before there was a union to enter,
so it has no treaty to point at, and the flag of that union carries no part of it
at all.
