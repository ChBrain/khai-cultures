**Latvia's Arc ends at 1991, and the largest fact about the country since then is that a third of it left.**

Four plots — the German centuries, the Awakening, the occupations, the Singing
Revolution — and nothing after. **Five of seventeen Company elements were dead**:
`Roberts`, the `Lielvārdes josta`, `rudzu maize`, the `Dziesmu svētki` and `Jāņi`.
The song festival, in a play whose Arc says the people sang themselves free, was
declared and staged nowhere.

Three plots added and one existing plot extended.

## `plot_00` Dainas un ugunskurs, pirms raksta

A woman reaps and sings. She cannot read, she does not own the ground she is
standing on, and her son will belong to the same lord she does. The song is four
lines long and she did not learn it — **she knows it, the way she knows when to
sow.**

That is a daina, and there are a million of them. Four lines, one rhythm, and
everything in them: work, marriage, birth, orphans, the sun, death. Almost no
lords and almost no church — a world older than both. Nobody writes them down for
seven centuries **and nobody needs to**, because four lines in a fixed metre stay
in the head without paper.

That world holds three things that are still here: dark sourdough `rudzu maize`,
**not festival food but the everyday kind, which is exactly why the family sits
round it**; `Jāņi` at midsummer, older than Christianity and never having yielded
to it; and the `Lielvārdes josta`, red and white, covered in signs whose meaning
has long been argued over — _and the argument is itself the proof: something is
written there in a language that is not letters, and it is older than everyone who
has ruled this place._

## `plot_02`, extended: the first Song Festival, 1873

Staged where it belongs, inside the Awakening. It is permitted because a choir is
not politics, and that is exactly why it works: **the festival asks for nothing
and passes nothing — it counts.** When a thousand peasants from across the land
stand in one field and sing in their own tongue loudly enough for the barons who
own them to hear it, everything has been said and nobody has said anything.

## `plot_05` Aizbraukšana, no 2004

A coach runs from Riga to Dublin. Two days, cheap, and the people on it are not
refugees and nobody deported them. Accession opens the one thing nobody here has
had for two hundred years: the right to leave and work anywhere, as of right. **It
is exactly what the nation spent decades wanting.**

Then the number. Around 2.6 million people in 1990; under two million thirty years
later. No other country in Europe has lost so large a share of itself in peace.
And the thing nobody wants to say aloud: **the same freedom the nation won by
singing is the one emptying it.** Nobody took it, and nobody will, because it is
the one thing everything else was done for.

## `plot_99` Pusotrs miljons un divas valodas

Two neighbours in a Riga stairwell greet each other each in her own language and
both understand. Latvia is the one of the three where this is not a minority
question by the numbers: about a third speak Russian at home, in Riga nearly half.
The 2012 referendum settled that there is one state language; the school
transition to Latvian completed recently. **To one person that is a justice
arriving late; to another it is the language of home leaving the classroom. Both
are true, and it is the same language they are both talking about.**

And every five years the festival still fills the stand — some of the singers
flying in for it.
