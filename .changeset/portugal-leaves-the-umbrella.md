## plot_00: Onde a terra acaba

The kingdom dates from 1143, which would be the passport `order_plot_zero.md` warns
against. The origin that is not a founding is **the shape of the ground**.

A man reaches the cape and sees the floor run out. Behind him a narrow strip of
land and then a larger kingdom speaking another language; ahead, nothing that can
be ploughed. Portugal sits against the Atlantic with **Spain closing its back**, on
a sliver that never fed everyone. That is not a choice: whoever has little land and
much sea learns early that the road is water. Centuries before there were caravels
and charts, people already fished far out, emigrated and came back, and families
were used to counting who was missing from the table. **When the ships later leave
Sagres they do not invent that habit — they give it scale.**

It stages `position_o_atlantico`, whose own text says a small land turned to the sea
kept _the long habit of seeking abroad what the narrow strip did not give_.

The Tension is why Portugal exists separately from Spain at all: **what could not
grow east grew west.** But a vocation that makes leaving the normal way to settle a
life leaves the country permanently half elsewhere — so `saudade` here is not a
loose sentiment, it is **the fixed price of this geography**.

## plot_99: A fachada fica

`plot_04`'s Tension ended on the emigration that followed 1974. The present had to
move past it, and what actually happened is the reverse and stranger.

The Cue: Amália passes the street she lived on for forty years and sees her own
window open, with people in it she does not know. **The building has been taken down
from the inside; the front wall, with its early-century azulejo, is left standing,
propped up, waiting for whatever comes behind it.**

Tourism and foreign money fill Lisbon and Porto, rents outrun wages, the old
neighbourhoods empty of the people born in them, the tasca closes and reopens with
another menu, the fado house does two ticketed sittings a night. **None of it is
destruction — almost everything is conserved, restored and put on display. What
leaves the neighbourhood is the people.**

It stages the other six, each from its own text: **Amália**, who kept the memory of
those who left and is now on the list of those who left; **Tiago**, whose Atlantic
vocation is settled this time not by ambition but by the price per square metre;
**the azulejo**, memory put on a wall, and the wall is what stays when everything
else comes down; **fado**, a sung confession that is also now a show with a start
time, both in the same room; **the table**, the slow company and the bica at the
counter, at a counter that changed hands and price; and **the pastel de nata**, the
little ambassador, never so sold and never so seldom eaten by the person living
next door.

The Tension: a people that always answered a shortage of land by leaving is now
leaving its own street, and this time the sea is not to blame. **It is the first
time departure is not a vocation but an arithmetic.** And the country does what
`position_o_brando` prescribes — it does not oppose head-on, it goes round, it
manages — and meanwhile the city grows beautiful and empty. The decade does not
settle whether **portugalidade is something lived or something conserved**, because
Portugal has never been so easy to look at nor so expensive to live in.
