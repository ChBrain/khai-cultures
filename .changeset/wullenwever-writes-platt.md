**Jürgen Wullenwever's file was written in German and his mother tongue is Low
German.** His Projection already said so and wired it: `dat Plattdüütsch`, held at
`process_writing_mother_tongue`. The file around it was `de`.

That is the first of the four findings `order_the_mother_tongue.md` opened, and it
is the cleanest of them: **the wiring was already right, so only the prose was
wrong.** No grip changed, no link target changed, no tongue was added.

## The register is the culture's own, not a new one

`de_schleswig_holstein` already holds two personas in `nds` — `persona_soenke.md`
and `persona_wiebke.md` — so this follows them rather than inventing a house Platt.
From them: link text takes the Low German form while the target stays
(`[de Koopmann](position_kaufmann.md)` against a position declaring _"der
Kaufmann"_, as Sönke writes `[Flensborg](place_flensburg.md)`), the culture reads
_"schleswig-holsteinsche Kultur"_, and `de Reet` is already this culture's word for
the tear Wullenwever's German called _"der Riss der Zeit"_.

He keeps `language:` of his own now, where before he inherited the play's `de` —
which is the only reason the counter could see him at all.

## What this does not settle

**Nothing in this house can check that the Platt is good Platt.** No wall reads
Low German, and `order_the_mother_tongue.md` says these rewrites each want a native
reader. This one wants one.

And one tension it surfaced rather than resolved: `nds/position_language_nds.md`
declares under **Orders** that Low German holds the verb in second place _"ok wo't
Hoochdüütsche in'n Bisatz dat Verb noch trüchhöllt"_ — even where High German keeps
it back in a subordinate clause. The two personas already in this culture do not
write that way; they put the verb last in subclauses throughout. This file follows
**them**, for consistency inside the culture, and the disagreement between the
tongue's stated Orders and the prose written under it is left standing where it can
be seen.
