**One move that is both of the two good choices, which is why it was taken before
the one that is only one of them.**

`ro` into the package, byte for byte. **All three remaining Visegrad members now
plan clean** — poland, slovakia and hungary — which they did not before this.

## The care point, met head on

This is the case the working order's one reservation was written for. Hungary's
`persona_bence` links Romanian, because a Hungarian in Transylvania grows up
inside it, and a published production carries no `../`. **But `romania` is not a
member of visegrad.** The permission to edit a tongue during its move rests on the
group's cultures being authored at step 4 anyway; romania is not in the group and
will not be. So the edit that is free elsewhere in this walk is not free here, and
the file lands unchanged — title still `"the Romanian Language"`, corrected when
romania itself migrates.

**Renamed to the anchor** on the `ar` precedent, and for a harder reason than
usual: `build.mjs` holds a directory as a language **only if that directory holds
its anchor**, so landing it as `ro_ro` would have made a `ro/` that is not a
language at all. The rename is content-identical and `romania` stays relink-only.

What it holds could not be a national variety: grammatical case still carried on
the noun — the only Romance language still keeping genitive-dative apart from
nominative-accusative, a trace of Latin its western sisters lost long ago; the
neuter as a third gender, masculine in the singular and feminine in the plural, an
ambiguity no other Romance language knows; and the definite article stuck to the
tail of the word, _lup_ then _lupul_, inherited from Slavic and Balkan neighbours
rather than standing in front as with its Latin brothers.

`moldova`'s `ro_md` stays where it is. It is a genuine variety and now has an
anchor to hang from; it moves when moldova does.

**47 languages, 104 varieties, 0.47.0.** 319 cultures at 0.319.0, unchanged.
Eleven files linked it, ten inside romania and one in hungary.

## Why this one, measured

The umbrella is **254 cultures, 240 of them blocked by at least one tongue**, and
245 distinct tongues stand in the way. Asked which single move frees the most
cultures outright, the answer is nearly flat — **the best frees two**, and
`romania/ro_ro` is one of only four that do. Asked which group is closest to being
a package:

| group     | members under the umbrella | tongue moves needed |
| --------- | -------------------------- | ------------------- |
| visegrad  | 3                          | **1**               |
| lusophone | 4                          | 2                   |
| iberia    | 4                          | 4                   |
| benelux   | 6                          | 7                   |
| eu        | 16                         | 18                  |
| nato      | 20                         | 22                  |

So this move is the shortest path to a group package **and** joint-first on
leverage. The two do not compete here, and a move that is both should be taken
before either is traded against the other.

## Where they do compete, for the record

Rolled up by language rather than by file, the leverage is not flat at all:

| language | files waiting | anchor already in the package |
| -------- | ------------- | ----------------------------- |
| `en`     | **78**        | yes                           |
| `es`     | 30            | yes                           |
| `ar`     | 19            | yes                           |
| `fr`     | 18            | yes                           |
| `pt`     | 8             | **no**                        |

**145 of the 245 tongues are varieties of four languages whose anchors are already
here** — the same mechanical move, repeated. That is the maximum-choices play, and
it is real: the `en` family alone touches anglosphere, caricom and the_americas.

It also has a catch worth naming before anyone starts it. **Roughly 48 of those 78
`en` files are US states**, and every one of them is among the **59 sub-national
cultures that do not conform** (#607) — they must be renamed `us_<name>` before
they can migrate at all. So the honest immediate reach of the `en` batch is about
**30 cultures, not 78**, with the rest standing behind a permanent-id rename that
is its own decision.

`pt` is the cleaner version of the same play: 8 files, no anchor yet, and it closes
`lusophone` while serving mercosur, latin_america and the_americas later.
