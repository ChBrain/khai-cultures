## plot_00: Ο χαλκός

Cyprus is unusual in that its floor was already deep - `plot_01` reaches antiquity.
But it reaches it as a **sweep**: the island passed from hand to hand of empires. A
summary is not a scene, and it never says why they all came.

In the middle of the second millennium BC, in a workshop near the sea, men break ore
brought down from the mountain and melt it. What comes out is reddish and soft, and
mixed with tin it is hard enough for a blade. On the shore a ship is waiting.

From the third and second millennia BC the island is one of the great copper
suppliers of the eastern Mediterranean, its ingots cast in the shape of an oxhide so
they can be carried, travelling to Crete, Egypt, the Levantine coast and Anatolia. In
the Amarna letters a king of Alashiya writes to Pharaoh about consignments of copper:
**the first time this island appears in anybody else's writing, it appears in a
letter about a shipment.**

And the name. Latin `cuprum` comes from `aes Cyprium`, the metal of Cyprus. **The
island did not take its name from a people or a founder - a metal and an island
exchanged names, and the metal then carried it into nearly every language in Europe.**

The ore is why they all come. The position explains why ships pass; **the copper
explains why they stop.** Phoenicians, Greeks, Assyrians, Egyptians, Persians,
Ptolemies, Romans - the crossroads becomes layer upon layer, and every layer starts
the same way, with somebody who wants what is here.

The Tension: an island wanted for what lies under it learns early that its master
will always be a foreigner, and that what can be kept is not power but whatever
cannot be loaded onto a ship - the speech, the table, the saint's day, the village.
That is the island's endurance, which changed eight rulers without losing its speech.
**And it is the shadow. A place that always belonged to somebody else learns to ask
whose it is rather than what it is; and when it finally becomes its own, in 1960, the
question does not go away. It only turns inward. It stops being "whose is the island"
and becomes "which of the two".**

## plot_99: Χαλλούμι και hellim

One spring morning in 2021 the same news reaches both sides of the line: the island's
cheese has been registered in Europe with a protected designation of origin, and **the
name registered is double - "Χαλλούμι/Hellim".** `Αντρέας` reads it in the south in
Greek, `Mehmet` hears it in the north in Turkish, and for once they are talking about
the same thing on the strength of the same piece of paper.

The registration followed an application of more than ten years, and it does not cover
the south alone: the cheese is protected as a product of the **whole island**. For that
to hold, cheese made in the north had to be inspected by the same recognised body,
which both sides had to accept. **It is one of the very few things the two communities
have actually completed together since 1974.**

Politically nothing moved. The last substantive talks collapsed in 2017 and were not
replaced. In 2020 the crossings shut for the first time since 2003 - not for a war but
for a virus - and for months the island learned what it had while it had it; then they
reopened. Nicosia is still the last divided capital in Europe. The bicommunal committee
still digs and still returns bones to families on both sides: **it is the one thing
that has never stopped.** `Αντρούλα` still keeps the key and waits for the telephone
call.

And underneath, the daily truth. In the south the `καφενείο` opens on the square and
in the north the kahvehane: same coffee, same backgammon, same table, two names for
one word. In the south the `πανηγύρι`, in the north the panayır. And halloumi is
hellim. **The two communities share the words for everything they eat, drink and
celebrate, and do not share the language in which they would say them to each other.**

The Tension: half a century of separation, and the only thing the two communities
finished together this decade is a registration for a cheese. That is very little, and
it is not nothing: to do it they had to agree on something that holds across the whole
island, and nothing of the kind had happened since 1974. `Η επανένωση` is the name of a
plan and not of a condition. The decade does not decide whether the island becomes one
again; it says something smaller and harder. **The cheese was registered under two
names on one piece of paper. The people who make it still meet, if they meet, in
English.**

## The tr_cy repair

The fault was recorded as the Has chapter carrying the anchor's material - pan-Turkish
agglutination and vowel harmony under an island's name. Measuring it properly found it
had spread further, and the worse case was **Orders**, which commanded the evidential
`-mIş` and a sentence-final verb. **Cypriot Turkish is known for doing neither.** The
file was not merely generic; in its central chapter it asserted the opposite of the
variety's two best-known features.

All four chapters are now the variety's own: the question asked by intonation with no
`mI` particle, so a written question does not look like one; `-dI` standing in for
`-mIş` in daily speech, so the speaker is not forced to mark where knowledge came from;
the verb leaving the end of the sentence under centuries of contact with the island's
Greek; consonant voicing unlike the standard; and a vocabulary of its own - old
Anatolian words the mainland has dropped since 1571, English from the British decades,
and island words shared with Cypriot Greek. Its Loses now names the quieter of the two
losses: **the words shared with the island's Greek are the first to be dropped when the
variety is defended as Turkish rather than as Cypriot - the words still joining the two
sides of the line are discarded precisely because they join it.**

**Only cyprus links this file.** The repair charged one culture, and that culture was
being authored in the same change anyway: the #608 condition met exactly, rather than
argued around. Flagged for native review, and the review matters more here than
anywhere else in the package.

**Two of the four recorded faults now stand repaired** - `pt_br` at #619 and `tr_cy`
here. `sv_fi` itself and `ar_dz` remain, and `ar_dz` belongs to algeria's migration.
