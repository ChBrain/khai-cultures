Ceuta gets a plot line and leaves the umbrella. Seventh of the eight, and the
first culture in this walk that arrived already well written.

**A note on what was already here.** This play has a real Arc, real Tensions and
a persona with two mother tongues both correctly linked — a standard the other
seven did not meet. The Arc already carries the end of the porteo and the long
silence of the border, so plot_99 had to go past it rather than restate it. What
was missing was only the floor and the ceiling.

**Two plots.**

- `plot_00_catorce_kilometros.md` — a skipper crosses from one shore to the other
  in a quiet morning without losing sight of either, Africa astern and Europe
  ahead, and under the keel the whole Atlantic is entering the Mediterranean
  through a funnel fourteen kilometres wide. That funnel is why there is a city
  here and not something else. Whoever holds a point of land with a harbour and
  height over that passage does not hold a stretch of coast, they hold a key —
  _el estrecho es lo que se disputa y la ciudad es solo donde se disputa._ The
  Romans called it the Seven Brothers for the seven hills a ship sees, which
  became Sebta and then the name it has now: **this city is named after how it
  looks from a boat.** And what follows is not a succession of owners but an
  accumulation of layers, none of which erased the one before — the caliphal
  gate lies under the floor of the centre, and whoever crosses the square
  crosses it without seeing it. The four communities follow from the same fact:
  a harbour in the only door between two seas is not used by those who pass, it
  is used by those who stop.
- `plot_99_la_raya_ya_no_da_de_comer.md` — for decades, every dawn, thousands of
  people, mostly women, walked through the Tarajal crossing under enormous
  bundles strapped to their backs, because what rides on a person's body pays no
  duty. That trade fed half the city and half the district opposite, and then it
  simply stopped existing. It was not banned in a debate, it went out: the
  commercial customs on the other side closed in 2019, the border shut entirely
  for the pandemic and stayed shut more than two years, and when it reopened the
  porteo did not come back. Half the city discovered at once that its economy was
  an anomaly — _vivía de que la raya existiera y de que a la vez no funcionara del
  todo._ Then in May 2021 the line was crossed another way, thousands of people
  in some forty-eight hours, many swimming round the breakwater, a large share of
  them minors. And then came the strangest thing of all, which was a normality:
  from 2023 lorries began crossing with papers, for the first time in the city's
  history — there had never been a real commercial customs post here, under any
  crown, in six hundred years.

The two ends meet on the same asymmetry. _En cinco años la misma raya ha sido
tres cosas distintas: un oficio, una avalancha y, por fin, una aduana. Ninguno de
los tres cambios lo decidió esta ciudad._ What is the city's own — four calendars
that fit in four streets — is the one thing that never depended on what is agreed
elsewhere.

Written from inside, and the sovereignty question is left where the house leaves
it: named as the daily fact that decisions about this border are taken in two
capitals that are not here, and not adjudicated.
