## plot_00: Svoja slova

Around 1100, in the church of St Lucy at Jurandvor on Krk, a priest cuts a record
of a land gift into a stone slab. Not in Latin, the language of the church he
belongs to, and **not in the Latin alphabet either** - in Glagolitic, the rounded
letters assembled for the Slavs two centuries earlier. He does it inside a church
in full communion with Rome, and nobody reproves him for it.

Glagolitic was made in the ninth century for the Slavonic liturgy of Cyril and
Methodius, and everywhere else in the West it died or gave way to Latin letters.
On the Croatian coast and islands it did not. The _glagoljaši_, village priests
saying mass in Church Slavonic and keeping their books in Glagolitic, lasted for
centuries, and Rome did not stop them: in **1248 Innocent IV expressly permitted
the bishop of Senj** Glagolitic and the Slavonic liturgy, an exception without
parallel in the Latin church. In **1483**, under thirty years after Gutenberg, the
_Misal po zakonu rimskoga dvora_ was printed - the first Croatian printed book and
the first European missal not printed in Latin script.

So a people on the seam of two churches got **a third alphabet**: not Rome's Latin
and not the East's Cyrillic, but its own, and with it its own word in the most
sacred thing it had. `hrvatstvo` begins as a belonging to the West that does not
coincide with the West's language.

The Tension is where this stops flattering. A people that belongs by permission
rather than by language learns that belonging is proved, and it will spend
centuries proving it in blood as the `predziđe kršćanstva`. What it holds is not
power but a script and a word - so the script later becomes the border: when
Croatian stays all but indistinguishable from its neighbour's, the difference gets
looked for in the letters. **The first Croatian writing was in a third alphabet,
and the culture that now keeps its latinica as proof of where it belongs can
easily forget that the oldest thing it wrote is written in other letters.**

## plot_99: Sezona

At the end of August `Ivan` closes the konoba where he worked the
season and packs for Ireland. `Kata` stays. **There were more
people in the village that summer than ever, and fewer will winter in it than in
any year before.**

Croatia entered the Union on 1 July 2013 and the euro and Schengen on 1 January 2023. The northern border went, the kuna went, and with them the last obstacle to
leaving. The 2021 census counted about 3.9 million people, **nearly a tenth fewer
than ten years earlier - the largest fall since counting began, and no war caused
it.**

Meanwhile the coast has never been fuller. The Adriatic takes several times the
country's population in guests each summer, and Dubrovnik, shelled in the Homeland
War, now has to cap how many ships and how many visitors come through the gate in
a day. **Inside the walls there are fewer Dubrovnik people every year and more
passers-through.** Houses in the stone towns become apartments, schools close, and
the klapa is sung to guests as often as to its own.

The Tension: a people that defended its ground for centuries and paid dearly for
it in 1991 is now emptying it itself, with no enemy at all. `svoj na svome` has
been achieved completely - the state is its own, the border open, the money shared

- and the people do not stay on their own. **The coast is fuller than ever, with
  people who do not live there.** The decade does not decide whether Croatia becomes
  a country of return or of departure; it shows something harder. This is the first
  time the leaving is free, and **a free departure is much harder to name than a
  siege, because there is nobody to accuse and nothing to defend.**
