## `plot_00` Gintaras ir Rasos, iki krikšto

After a storm people walk the shore picking up what the sea has thrown out. Some
of the stones have an insect in them that stopped moving forty million years ago.
They do not know what it is. They know that in the south they will give anything
for it.

**The first line the world ever writes about these people is about a stone the sea
throws up** — Roman notices of northern tribes who gather amber, and that is all.
Not their kings, not their wars, not their gods.

And the baptism comes last in all of Europe: 1387, and in Samogitia a quarter of a
century later, by which time Paris has had a cathedral for two hundred years.
**Which means what was here before the baptism was alive the day before yesterday**
— not a memory, not a reconstruction, but something the baptism covered and did
not have time to erase. That is why `Rasos` still burns under the Catholicism, and
why the language kept what its neighbours lost: nobody was here long enough to
change it.

## `plot_05` Barselona 1992

A national team with no money and no kit. The state is two years old. An American
rock band nobody had asked sends money and tie-dyed shirts with a skeleton dunking
a basketball. Four of those men had won Olympic gold four years earlier — **for the
Soviet Union**, in the same kit as the people who deported their grandparents. Now
they play under their own flag, carrying the `Vytis`, a sign that was banned for
fifty years and that people drew anyway. The bronze game is against the former
Union's team. They win, and they take the medals in the tie-dye.

**It is not sport.** Independence was declared on paper in 1990 and for two years
much of the world was unsure what that state was. Here it stands on a podium under
its own name, against that opponent, and wins. `Krepšinis` is called the second
religion after this, and not only as a joke: it is the one thing this nation does
better than everyone, and the one place where being small counts for nothing.

## `plot_99` Grįžimas

At the next table in a Vilnius café people are speaking Belarusian, not quietly.
The border is thirty kilometres away and they cannot go home.

Lithuania emigrated like its neighbours after 2004 — and then, **alone of the
three, it stopped.** Net inward migration from around 2019, more after 2022. _The
country turned from a place people leave into a place people arrive, and not
because it got better here but because it got worse around it._

And one thing was always different: **in 1991 citizenship was given to everyone
resident, unconditionally.** So the question that will not close in Estonia and
Latvia does not arise here. Three states, the same occupier, the same dates — and
one decision taken in one year still separates one from two.
