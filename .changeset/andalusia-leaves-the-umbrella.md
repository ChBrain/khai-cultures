Andalusia gets a plot line and leaves the umbrella. First of the eight.

**A correction first.** In #661 I called these eight "already authored, already
at zero, no plots to write" and "near-free migrations". That was wrong, and the
counter I was reading does not say what I said it said. All eight measure `0
blocking, 0 advisory` and `0 dead`, and **none of them has a plot_00 or a
plot_99**. `plot-zero` is a ratchet that fires only when a change touches a
culture's plots, so a bare migration would have passed every wall and shipped
eight headless plot lines as npm packages — which is precisely the invisible
failure `order_plot_zero.md` was written about: nothing looks missing below the
first plot, because there is nothing below it to look at.

They are cheaper than the eleven — no tongue to move, no id to rename, no dead
to clear — but the expensive half was never the mechanics. It was the research.

**Two plots.**

- `plot_00_el_monte_de_las_anforas.md` — there is a hill in Rome that is not a
  hill. It is made of millions of broken oil amphorae, nearly all from one
  valley in southern Hispania. Rome ate the oil; what stayed in Rome was the
  container, and what stayed in the valley was the work of filling it. This is a
  fertile triangle with one door, and the door faces the Atlantic, with the only
  large navigable river on the peninsula running through it. A rich valley at
  the end of a navigable river is not a larder, it is a warehouse with a quay:
  silver before Rome, oil in quantities that raised a hill in the imperial
  capital, and later every ship from the Americas funnelled through one office
  on this river. _Por este valle han pasado cantidades de riqueza que ningún
  sitio de su tamaño ha visto, y han pasado._ A warehouse with a quay does not
  need people who own; it needs people who load — which is where the enormous
  estates and the landless jornalero come from, and why plot_02 happens.
- `plot_99_el_mar_de_plastico.md` — the same shape, a different crop. Tens of
  thousands of hectares of greenhouse under a continuous white sheet visible
  from orbit, supplying half of Europe with tomatoes in January, worked at forty
  degrees by people who own none of it and whose surnames come from further away
  than Manuel's — the identical relation to land the jornalero has described for
  centuries. One province holds tens of millions of olive trees and out-produces
  almost every country, and the dry years of this decade made oil expensive in
  the house that makes it. Underneath is the same river: the 2023 bill to
  legalise irrigation beside Doñana, where the Guadalquivir ends, drew European
  Commission objections and UNESCO warnings and was withdrawn for an investment
  agreement. The contest was postponed, not settled.

The two ends close on the same ledger. The one thing this valley makes that
cannot be extracted without the person making it is flamenco — inscribed by
UNESCO in 2010, filling the tablao nightly, and still happening at three in the
morning in a peña for twelve people who know when to be quiet. _No hay manera de
embarcarlo y dejar aquí el trabajo, porque el trabajo es la cosa._
