**Benelux, member one of three.** Belgium out, at zero dead Company, with both ends
of its plot line written.

Belgium carried **eight dead Company elements** - the most of any culture in this
walk - and a plot line running 1830 to the federal reforms with no origin and no
present. They did **not** split two ways like the others: one of them belongs in the
middle of the chronology, and putting it in a new plot would have been tidier and
wrong.

## plot_00: Le pays des autres

1830 is already `plot_01`, and it is the passport `order_plot_zero.md` warns about
anyway. The origin is **older than every owner this ground has had**.

A cloth merchant in a Flemish town pays tax to a duke who lives in Dijon and keeps
his accounts in French, while his hall, his workshop and his wife speak Dutch. Two
villages south, nobody understands him - and it has been that way for as long as
anyone can say.

**The line between Germanic and Romance speech has crossed this soil since the end
of the Roman Empire and has barely moved in fifteen centuries. It is older than any
state laid over it.** Across it come the Burgundians, then the Habsburgs, Spain,
Austria, France, the Netherlands - **never a master from here**. So this country
learns, before it is a country, what can be kept when sovereignty always belongs to
somebody else: not a national epic, but a way of living. One eats well and long,
one laughs at power without confronting it, and once a year the town turns upside
down with the prince's permission.

That is where two of the dead were waiting. **`la table`** is what the Burgundians
left behind - the language here still calls it _bourgondisch_. **`le carnaval`** is
the licensed inversion, tolerated under the Habsburgs, where you mock the world at
the permitted hour.

The Tension: a land that never had a master of its own gets rare at lasting under
anyone, keeping what cannot be confiscated. That is why it will one day exist
without ever having been a nation - and why it will find becoming one so hard. **It
never had to decide together what it is, only to come to an arrangement with what
others had decided.**

## plot_99: On se parle en anglais

In a corridor in Brussels, **Wout** passes **Julien**. Wout learned French at school
and does not use it; Julien never learned Dutch. **They greet each other in English,
and nobody nearby finds it strange.**

Each community lives a complete life in its own language - parties, media, schools —
and they meet mostly in a third that belongs to neither. The country sets records
for months without a government and keeps working, because the regions govern and
the federal state has little left to block. Brussels, the bilingual enclave in
Flemish ground, is now majority foreign-origin and works in English; **Naïma** moves
from the counter in French to a colleague in Dutch to a client in English without
thinking about it.

The Tension is the one I did not expect to find and could not avoid writing. A state
that holds without a government has proved something and nobody knows what: that
`le compromis à la belge` is sturdier than the people negotiating it, **or that
there is no longer enough in common for the deadlock to be felt.** The two halves
barely quarrel any more, which looks like peace and might be indifference - _one
does not quarrel with someone one expects nothing from._ The decade does not decide
whether Belgium comes apart; it dissolves far too slowly for that, and the Crown,
the capital and the social security still hold it together. It asks only **what is
left to share when the common language is nobody's**, and the honest answer, for
now, is a cone of chips and a joke about a pipe.

## The one that went in the middle

**Hendrik Conscience** is 1838, not 1830 and not now. He belongs to `plot_03`, the
language border of 1962, as its cause: the man who a century earlier gave Flemish a
written language to be proud of, **and without whom there is no movement to demand
that line.** He is staged there, in the plot that already existed, rather than
bent into a new one.
