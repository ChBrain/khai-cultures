## plot_00: El Consulado del Mar

`plot_01` is the Renaixença of 1859, which is where this culture's plot line began -
and a nineteenth-century revival is a strange place for a thousand-year-old people to
start. The origin is five centuries earlier and it is not a nation, it is **a court**.

In fourteenth-century Barcelona two merchants who have never met, from different
cities and under no common lord, argue over a cargo lost at sea. **They do not go to
the king.** They go to a tribunal of merchants elected by merchants, judging from a
code written for the sea.

That is the Consolat de Mar, and the book it applied became the commercial law of the
western Mediterranean, adopted in ports where no Catalan count ruled anything, **because
it worked.** This country's contribution to the world is not an empire. **It is a
procedure.**

The same century sets the political habit beside it. The count-king of the Crown of
Aragon governs with the Corts, and the principle is that the sovereign is bound by what
he has agreed - one of Europe's earliest constitutional traditions, and at bottom **a
merchant's idea: whoever rules is a party to a contract.**

So the whole vocabulary this culture describes itself with comes out of a counting
house. `Seny` is the judgement of someone who keeps accounts. `Feina ben feta` is the
obligation of someone answerable to whoever pays. `Pactisme` is simply the habit that
things are agreed. **All of it is written on a trading floor long before it is written
on any flag.**

The Tension: a people whose founding instrument is an agreement becomes excellent at
negotiating and constitutionally allergic to being governed by decree, and will spend
six centuries inside a larger state trying to settle by pact what that state prefers to
settle by law from the centre. **And the shadow is the method's own. Pactisme assumes
a counterparty willing to bargain. Where there is none, a culture built on the pact has
no second move: it can negotiate and it can refuse, and the space between those two is
exactly where it keeps getting stuck.**

## plot_99: La pinya

A village square, festa major. Before anyone climbs, hundreds pack into the base -
neighbours, outsiders, whoever turned up. **That is the pinya, and without it the tower
does not go up.** `Montserrat` is in there somewhere, shoulder to shoulder, **and
nobody is asked who they vote for.**

The decade was the division. After the referendum of 2017 came the trials, the pardons
of 2021 and the amnesty law of 2024 - and whatever anyone makes of any of it, one thing
is measurable: **the country divided from itself, at the table and inside families.**

The split reached the wine. In 2019 a group of Penedès producers left the cava
denomination to found their own, **so one method and one soil are now shared between two
names.** Montserrat's cooperative lives inside that arithmetic.

It reached the school. The immersion plan that brought Catalan back to the classroom
after 1979 met court rulings fixing a share of teaching in Spanish, **and what had been
an agreement became a quota.**

And meanwhile the craft went on. `Jordi` works glass in a city whose largest export is
its own architecture, and the Sagrada Família nears its finish - **a tower begun in 1882
by a man who knew he would not see it done, still going up a hundred and forty years
later.** And the castells went on: UNESCO since 2010, colles larger than ever, and the
same rule as always - **anyone may join the pinya.**

The Tension: a culture whose politics is the pact spent this decade finding out what
happens when there is nobody to pact with, and what happened is that it divided
inwardly - `plot_00`'s method left without its second move, stuck exactly where it
always sticks. **And yet ten times a year the same society raises in the square the
answer it cannot produce in the chamber.** A castell does not stand by whoever is on
top; it stands on the pinya, **and in the pinya nobody is asked where they come from or
what they think, because the tower falls if people are missing.** The decade settles
nothing political. It leaves something more uncomfortable in view: **this country knows
perfectly well how it is done, and demonstrates it in public, to music, at a village
festival, standing on the shoulders of neighbours it does not agree with.**
