# Position: grannspråket

## Taxonomy

Parent group: positions

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Has

A language you were never taught and are expected to follow. Danish, Swedish and
Norwegian are close enough that a speaker of one can read the others almost at
once and, with a little exposure, hear them, and the north treats this not as a
happy accident but as a competence: schools teach the neighbour languages as a
subject, broadcasters subtitle sparingly or not at all, and a Nordic meeting is
supposed to run with each person speaking their own.

It has a written right behind it. A convention among these states gives a citizen
of any of them the right to use their own Nordic language in another's public
offices and courts, which is a thing almost no group of sovereign states has ever
granted one another and is the direct descendant of a student movement that failed
at everything else it tried.

And it has an asymmetry it does not advertise. Norwegian speakers understand both
their neighbours best, because the written form leans to Danish and the sound
leans to Swedish. Danish is the easiest of the three to read and the hardest to
hear. And two of the five are not in it at all.

## Orders

It orders effort that looks like none. Following a neighbour language is work, and
the etiquette of the north is that the work is not mentioned: you do not switch,
you do not ask for repetition more than once, and you certainly do not say that
Danish is difficult, though everybody thinks it.

It orders the two outside to come in on their own. Finnish is not related to any
of it and Icelandic has drifted too far to be followed, so a Finn or an Icelander
who wants the room learns a Scandinavian language, and the three inside do not
learn theirs. That obligation runs one way and nobody has ever proposed
correcting it, which is the least egalitarian arrangement in the most egalitarian
region on earth.

## Loses

It loses precision at exactly the moments precision matters. Comprehension that is
good enough for a conversation is not good enough for a contract, so the serious
business has always been done in writing or in a third language, and the office
therefore covers the sociable half of a relationship and quietly excuses itself
from the other half.

And it is losing its holders. The young of the three central countries test worse
at the neighbour languages than their parents and better at English, so the
competence is thinning generation by generation while the institutions built on it
carry on as though it were not.

## Drives

It teaches that understanding and speaking are separable, and that a room can
work with five people speaking three languages if everyone accepts partial
comprehension and nobody makes a performance of it. Most of the world regards that
as a failed conversation. The north regards it as a normal Tuesday.

And it drives an unusual relation to one's own language: something you speak
outward and expect to be met halfway in, rather than something you switch out of
to be understood. That is the habit English is now taking, and the north has not
yet noticed that it is the habit rather than the language that was the rare thing.
