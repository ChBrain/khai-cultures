# Plot: No Passport, from 1952

## Taxonomy

[play_nordics](play_nordics.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

A Finnish welder gets on a boat to Stockholm with no passport and no permit, walks into a yard, and starts on Monday, and there is no office anywhere that needs to be told.

## Action

The five agree to let their citizens cross without papers, and then go further and agree a common labour market, so that a Nordic citizen may take work in any of them on the same terms as a national. This is 1952 and 1954. The treaty of Rome is 1957, and the equivalent freedom inside it takes another decade to bite. Nobody in the north makes much of the achievement, partly because it is presented as housekeeping and partly because these are not countries that make much of things.

What it produces is a generation of movement that shows up in the demography rather than the diplomacy: Finns to the Swedish factories in numbers large enough to change both countries, Icelanders and Faroese to Copenhagen for university, Swedes to Norway when the oil comes. The institutions built alongside it, a council of parliamentarians and later a council of ministers, are deliberately weak, with no power to bind anybody, which is exactly what 1864 taught the north to design.

## Stage

- [Finland](@chbrain/khai-cultures-finland/play_finland.md): the country the movement mostly went out of, into Swedish industry, in numbers that reshaped its own west.
- [Sweden](@chbrain/khai-cultures-sweden/play_sweden.md): the one it mostly went into, which is why the largest minority language in Sweden's recent history was Finnish.
- [Iceland](@chbrain/khai-cultures-iceland/play_iceland.md): the far end of the arrangement, with a whole professional class educated in Copenhagen.
- [Norway](@chbrain/khai-cultures-norway/play_norway.md): the destination once the oil arrives, and the demonstration that the flow reverses when the money moves.

## Tension

The north got the thing Europe spent fifty years negotiating, got it early, and got it by not writing very much down and not asking anyone to give anything up. That is the Nordic method and this is its best result. Its cost is the same as its method: the arrangement binds nobody, so it holds only while all five find it convenient, and when two of them joined a larger union and one did not, and one joined the currency and the others did not, nothing in the Nordic machinery could do anything about the divergence except note it.
