# Plot: the order of the cuts

## Taxonomy

[play_arizona](play_arizona.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

There is a white band of mineral around the rock walls of the reservoir, a bathtub ring a hundred feet high, marking where the water used to be. It is not a surprise and it is not a dispute. Everyone downstream has known for decades roughly when this would happen, and - more to the point - in what order.

## Action

In 1968 Arizona got the Central Arizona Project authorised, the canal that carries Colorado River water uphill across the desert to Phoenix and Tucson: the third attempt at the problem in plot 0, and the largest. The price of authorisation was priority. California would not agree otherwise, so Arizona's canal water was made junior - meaning that when the river ran short, this state would be cut first, and deepest, by contract. **The canal exists because Arizona agreed in writing to be the first to lose it.**

The shortage arrived on schedule. In August 2021 the federal government declared the first-ever official shortage on the Colorado, and Arizona took the deepest reductions of any state, exactly as the 1968 bargain specified. The following year the reservoirs hit record lows. Nothing about this was a natural disaster or a failure of foresight; it was a clause.

So the state turned to what it had left, which was the ground. But [the groundwater plan](plan_groundwater_management.md) only governs the designated management areas, and in June 2023 the state stopped certifying new subdivisions in parts of the Phoenix area because the hundred-year assured supply could no longer be demonstrated - the growth model that has run this valley since the war meeting an arithmetic it cannot argue with. Outside those areas the pumping is barely metered at all, which is how leases in the western valleys came to be growing alfalfa on unmeasured groundwater for export, until they were cancelled in 2023.

And the heat has stopped being weather. In the summer of 2023 Phoenix ran thirty-one consecutive days at or above a hundred and ten degrees, and the county recorded its worst year for heat deaths, most of them outdoors and most of them people without housing. A city that exists because of air conditioning is discovering the cost of the building's other half.

The old industry is having the opposite experience. Arizona still produces most of the copper mined in the United States, and copper has quietly become the metal the whole energy transition runs on - grid, motors, panels. [Ramón](persona_ramon.md) has spent his career on ore bodies that were a commodity and are now strategic. But [the smelting](process_copper_smelting.md) largely left: American smelting capacity collapsed over decades, so concentrate from these mines is shipped across an ocean to be smelted and comes back as metal. The state digs the thing and no longer finishes it. And the next great ore body sits under a site the San Carlos Apache hold sacred, which has been in the courts through this whole decade - the same question as the canals, asked about rock instead of water: whether what a place already is counts against what can be taken out of it.

The hardest fact belongs to [Yazhi](persona_yazhi.md)'s side of the state. Somewhere around a third of homes on the Navajo Nation have no running water, in a nation whose border is the river. In June 2023 the Supreme Court held that the United States, under the treaty of 1868, has no affirmative duty to secure water for the Navajo Nation. [The language](@chbrain/khai-cultures-tongues/nv/position_language_nv.md) this country once used because no enemy could read it belongs to people it has told, in court, that it does not owe a pipe.

## Tension

The Hohokam were stopped by salt and flood - physical facts that arrived without anyone's signature. This time the limit is a clause. Arizona bought its canal by agreeing to be cut first, and the cut came in the order the document specified, on time. That is a harder thing to be angry at and a harder thing to fix, because there is no flood to rebuild after: the water is going where the paper says it goes. The third attempt at the same canals is the first one to fail by contract rather than by nature.
