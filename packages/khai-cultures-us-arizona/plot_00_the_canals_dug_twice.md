# Plot: the canals, dug twice

## Taxonomy

[play_arizona](play_arizona.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

A man in the 1860s is clearing silt out of a ditch in the Salt River valley, and the ditch is already there. He did not survey it. It runs where water has to run, at the gradient water needs, for miles, and it was cut by hand with stone and wood by people who left no one behind to tell him so. He widens it, puts his water in it, and farms.

## Action

Between roughly the middle of the first millennium and the fifteenth century, the people archaeology calls the Hohokam built the largest irrigation system in pre-contact North America: hundreds of miles of main canals off the Salt and the Gila, hand-dug across desert, carrying river water far enough from the river to feed tens of thousands. It is the single most impressive piece of engineering anyone attempted on this continent before the arrival of iron tools, and it worked for something like eight hundred years.

Then it ended. Not in one blow: great floods that tore out the headworks and had to be rebuilt, a long drought, and - quietly, underneath - salt. Irrigate a desert for centuries with river water and the water leaves its minerals in the soil every time it evaporates, until the ground that the canals made productive stops producing. The system that made the place habitable is also what made it, in the end, uninhabitable. By around 1450 the valley's population had dispersed.

Four hundred years later the next arrivals did not solve the problem again. They cleared the old alignments and used them, because the hard part - where, exactly, water can be persuaded to go across this ground - had been worked out by somebody else a thousand years earlier. And they named the settlement Phoenix, after the bird that rises from the ashes of the thing that burned. The city's name is a confession that they knew what they were building on.

Meanwhile the state's greatest river is unreachable. The Colorado crosses this ground at the bottom of a trench a mile deep, cut through nearly two billion years of rock - [the most complete exposed geological record on earth](place_grand_canyon.md), and water you cannot drink without engineering on a scale no one had. So Arizona holds an enormous quantity of water it cannot touch, above a desert that has to be irrigated to hold anyone at all, and the whole of its history is arrangements to close that gap.

Everything else follows from the same arithmetic. [Living here](position_desert_resilience.md) means two rain seasons, a monsoon that either comes or does not, and a saguaro that takes fifty years to grow its first arm - a place where patience is not a virtue but a measurement. And [the other way of answering](position_southwest_frontier.md) - dig it out, pipe it in, build the thing - is equally native to the ground, because the ground rewards engineering and punishes assumption.

## Tension

The first people to make this desert carry a city were ended by the very thing that let them do it, and they left the evidence lying in the ground in the form of the ditches themselves. The second people found those ditches, understood enough to reuse them, named their town for rising out of the ashes - and did not read the rest of the sentence. A warning and an omen of rebirth are the same object seen from two angles, and which one you are looking at is not decided until later.
