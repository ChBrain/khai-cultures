# Obwalden: a khai culture

Obwalden staged as a full khai play, ISO CH-OW (a Swiss half-canton; the website derives its parent, `CH`, from the subdivision code), language de. The half above the wood, which gave a country its founding story and kept the file copy: its history as the plot line, its people as the cast, the office of being a half, its places, its practices, and its defining objects, all tuned by a single pitch.

## Estate

[khai-cultures](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-cultures): the Cultures house that holds this culture and answers for it. This culture logs the house as its Estate; a culture with no Estate is not yet a production.

## The production

- **Anchor:** [play_obwalden.md](play_obwalden.md), the culture itself.
- **Pitch:** [pitch_obwalden.md](pitch_obwalden.md), written from Switzerland's profile inflected by a very small polity whose influence always depended on being come to, and naming no dimension.
- **Plots (history):** the Kernwald that made one landscape into two, the 1291 charter sealed under a name this half does not use, the White Book of Sarnen of around 1470, Brother Klaus and the counsel of 1481, the acquisition of Engelberg in 1815, the flood of 2005, and the tax decision of 2006 that turned the poorest canton into the cheapest.
- **Personas:** Agnes (who keeps the place open without farming it) and Reto (whose profession came out of a referendum).
- **Positions:** die Obwaldner Kultur (a way of living Swiss culture, nested on the position and not on every persona), ob dem Wald (the office of being the upper half of a thing that never was whole) and der billige Kanton (the office of undercutting the neighbours you are in a confederation with).
- **Places:** Sarnen (the capital and the archive), der Ranft (the gorge three hundred metres from a farm), Engelberg (the exclave behind the mountain).
- **Processes:** der Ranftgang (the night walk, while the coaches come by day).
- **Pieces:** das Weisse Buch (a nation's founding story filed between two certified copies), das Radbild (the only piece of a hermit's thought that is not second-hand).
