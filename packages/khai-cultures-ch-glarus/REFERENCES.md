# References

This is a creative staging; sources are generic historical encyclopedias (e.g. HLS).

## The defining question

**What defines Glarus, and does the play stage it?**

The version this package replaces began in 1861 and ran three plots. Its origin
was `plot_00_das_fridolinsfeuer_1861`: a name joining the canton's patron saint,
Fridolin, who stands on its flag, to a town fire he has nothing to do with. No
such term exists. And a town fire, however consequential for the street plan, is
not where a canton comes from.

Glarus comes from **Näfels**. On 9 April 1388 a few hundred Glarners met a far
larger Habsburg force on their own slope, behind their own Letzi wall, and won.
They stopped belonging to anyone and became a full member of the Confederacy,
and they have walked to the battlefield on the first Thursday of April every
year since 1389 - reading the battle at eleven memorial stones, without a break,
through the two centuries when the canton could not even hold one Landsgemeinde
together. That is `plot_00`, and the Näfelser Fahrt is a process rather than a
memory because the canton performs it.

The plot line then carries what the earlier version left out: the two separate
Landsgemeinden, Catholic and Reformed, that ran side by side from 1623 to 1836
in a canton of a few tens of thousands; the cotton printing that sold to three
continents while financing an organised emigration of about two hundred of its
poorest to Wisconsin in 1845; the 1864 factory act, which the branch already had
and which is kept because it is Glarus's strongest claim - workers using a ring
where every man has one vote to impose Europe's first such law on their own mill
owners; and the 2011 Landsgemeinde that merged 25 municipalities into 3 in a
single afternoon, going further than its own government proposed.

`plot_99` is renamed. The branch called it `die_letzte_landsgemeinde` while its
own text says, correctly, that Glarus keeps its Landsgemeinde. It is not the
last, and it is not the only one remaining either - Appenzell Innerrhoden holds
one too. It is now `der_ring_haelt`.

The Stakes turn on the one thing all six plots share: a ring that is capable of
breaking its own ruling class and of erasing twenty-two municipalities that were
never asked, using exactly the same mechanism.

**What is deliberately not staged.** The 1861 fire and the grid rebuilt after it
are held in `place_der_zaunplatz` rather than given a plot. The rebuilding is a
town-planning fact; the factory act three years later carries the industrial turn
better, and a sixth plot from the same decade would crowd the arc. A later hand
may judge that wrong; this is the argument to answer.

**On the package.** It was regenerated from `tests/new_culture.mjs` and the
written prose moved across. What it replaces used `## Grip` / `## Value` on its
positions and `## Input` / `## Method` / `## Output` on its process, none of them
canon chapters; carried no parent nesting; had no `type:` on either persona; and
was CRLF in all 17 files.
