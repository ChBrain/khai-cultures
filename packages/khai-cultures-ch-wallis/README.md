# Wallis: a khai culture

Valais staged as a full khai play, ISO CH-VS (a Swiss canton; the website derives its parent, `CH`, from the subdivision code), languages de and fr. A valley too dry to farm that hung its water off the cliffs: its history as the plot line, its people as the cast, the office of holding the water, its places, its practices, and its defining objects, all tuned by a single pitch.

## Estate

[khai-cultures](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-cultures): the Cultures house that holds this culture and answers for it. This culture logs the house as its Estate; a culture with no Estate is not yet a production.

## The production

- **Anchor:** [play_wallis.md](play_wallis.md), the culture itself.
- **Pitch:** [pitch_wallis.md](pitch_wallis.md), written from Switzerland's profile inflected by a community that has counted water by the hour for six hundred years, and naming no dimension.
- **Plots (history):** the suonen that made a dry valley farmable, the Walser migration that settled the high valleys of half the Alps, the conquest of the Lower Valais in 1475, the seventeen years from subjection to equality between 1798 and 1815, the straightening of the Rhône, the Grande Dixence of 1961, and the glacier collapse at Blatten in 2025.
- **Personas:** Gaby (who keeps what was agreed, and expects the same) and Céline (who farms ground a dam and a channel made possible).
- **Positions:** die Walliser Kultur (a way of living Swiss culture, nested on the position and not on every persona), das Wasser (the office of a country with too much water above it and too little falling on it) and die zwei Hälften (the office of a language border that was once a border of rule).
- **Places:** Sitten (where both halves must meet), das Lötschental (where the old forms lasted longest), die Grande Dixence (the largest object this canton ever built).
- **Processes:** die Wasserkehr (the turn at the sluice, which holds at two in the morning).
- **Pieces:** die Suone (a constitution in stone and wood), die Tschäggättä (the masks one carves oneself).
