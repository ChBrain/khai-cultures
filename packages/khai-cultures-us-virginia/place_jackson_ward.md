# Place: Jackson Ward

## Taxonomy

Parent group: places

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Shown

A Richmond neighborhood of brick row houses, churches, shops, theaters, offices, and meeting halls where Black residence and enterprise concentrated under segregation. Maggie Walker's home and the institutions of the Independent Order of St. Luke belong to this urban fabric.

## Holds

- [put our money together](plot_03_put_our_money_together.md): a bank, newspaper, mutual-aid network, and business district built to make Black economic action possible.

## Offers

Proximity between household, borrower, reader, organizer, congregation, and storefront. [Maggie Walker](persona_maggie_walker.md) could build institutions whose users met one another in the neighborhood; [Aya](persona_aya.md) can still ask what development preserves besides a facade.

## Withheld

A simple triumphal story. Segregation helped force Black life into a bounded district, and later highway construction and redevelopment cut through its continuity. Celebrating the institutions without naming those constraints mistakes adaptation to exclusion for consent to it.
