# Persona: Maggie Walker

## Taxonomy

[Black self-possession](position_black_self_possession.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Projection

The Richmond organizer who turned the Independent Order of St. Luke from a struggling burial society into a network of mutual aid, publishing, employment, and finance. [Virginian English](@chbrain/khai-cultures-tongues/en/position_language_en_us_va.md) was the mother tongue in which she [spoke, read, thought, and wrote](process_speaking_mother_tongue.md), moving between the register of a schoolteacher, a newspaper editor, a fraternal officer, and a bank president without asking a segregated city which voice it preferred. She belongs to [the Old Dominion](position_culture_old_dominion.md) as a maker of institutions in a place whose inherited institutions were designed to exclude her.

## Action

In [put our money together](plot_03_put_our_money_together.md), she establishes [the St. Luke Herald](piece_st_luke_herald.md) in 1902 and the St. Luke Penny Savings Bank in 1903. Through [mutual-aid banking](process_mutual_aid_banking.md), she turns small deposits into mortgages, business credit, wages, and a durable claim on [Jackson Ward](place_jackson_ward.md).

## Shadow

The discipline required to keep Black institutions solvent under hostile conditions could harden into respectability: a demand that members demonstrate thrift and order even when poverty was produced by forces no household budget could overcome.

## Tell

She answers a closed door by drawing an organization chart, finding ten small sums where no large sum exists, and making the next door belong to the people who need to pass through it.
