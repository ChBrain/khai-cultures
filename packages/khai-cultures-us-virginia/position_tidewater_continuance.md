# Position: tidewater continuance

## Taxonomy

Parent group: positions

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Has

A relation to the tidal rivers older than the name Virginia and still lived by the tribal nations of the coastal plain. It has [the Pamunkey River](place_pamunkey_river.md) as homeland and working water, [Pamunkey pottery](process_pamunkey_pottery.md) as knowledge made adaptable rather than frozen, and the authority of survival through colonial seizure, racial classification, and official stories that kept announcing an ending which did not occur.

## Orders

That continuity be measured in repeated work: governing, fishing, making, teaching, restoring, and raising the next generation in relation to a place. That an outsider distinguish respect from nostalgia and living practice from a demand to reproduce an imagined past. That environmental planning consult the people whose knowledge of the river was never merely recreational.

## Loses

When public history treats Indigenous presence as a chapter closed by Jamestown, and when recognition by a later government is mistaken for the moment a nation began to exist. It also loses when the need to demonstrate authenticity rewards what looks oldest to a buyer and hides the adaptation that made survival possible.

## Drives

[Lena](persona_lena.md) to make a pot that answers present clay, present hands, and inherited knowledge without performing timelessness. In [the rivers already had names](plot_00_the_rivers_already_had_names.md), it moves Virginia's origin from an English arrival to peoples whose continuing relation to the rivers makes every later founding date partial.
