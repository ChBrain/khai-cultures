# Plot: the river the sea entered

## Taxonomy

[play_virginia](play_virginia.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

A boat coming up the James can ride the tide inland until rock breaks the river at Richmond. The water below that point is broad, slow, and salted by the Atlantic; above it, the river narrows into the Piedmont. The same stop repeats at the heads of Virginia's other tidal rivers. Sea and stone have drawn a line across the ground.

## Action

About ten thousand years ago, rising seas entered the lower valley of the Susquehanna and drowned it. The water also backed into the Potomac, Rappahannock, York, and James, widening their mouths into long tidal reaches. The Chesapeake is therefore a river system the sea moved into, not a harbor opened by a colony.

Those reaches end at the fall line, where the hard rock of the Piedmont meets the softer sediments of the coastal plain. Boats coming from the bay could go no farther. Much later, towns grew at the stops: Alexandria near the Potomac's head of navigation, Fredericksburg on the Rappahannock, and Richmond on the James. Hampton Roads held deep water below them. The ground arranged an inland edge, a coastal plain, and a maritime road before any of those places had their names.

The same water joins country that looks divided. Rain in [the Shenandoah Valley](place_shenandoah_valley.md) enters the Potomac and reaches the bay; water leaving farms and streets farther south runs through the other tidal rivers. The watershed crosses mountains, cities, and later borders without noticing them. What happens upstream eventually enters the estuary.

That physical arrangement is the first meaning beneath [the Old Dominion](position_culture_old_dominion.md). Jamestown in plot 1 could be supplied from the Atlantic because the James carried the sea inland. Tobacco could leave from river landings. Shipyards could fill Hampton Roads. Virginia's claim to be old begins on a coast that was already a network of passages and limits, made by water moving against rock.

## Stage

- [the Chesapeake Bay plan](plan_chesapeake_bay_cleanup.md): the later attempt to manage one connected watershed across every jurisdiction laid over it.

## Tension

The drowned valleys gave Virginia deep water, river landings, and a road to the Atlantic. They also made the bay the receiver of everything sent down from the watershed. The fall line appears to separate inland from coast, but the water refuses the separation. A culture can call the ground its dominion; it cannot make the river stop at its border.
