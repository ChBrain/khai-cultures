# Position: the neighbour

## Taxonomy

Parent group: positions

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Has

A status with no other example: not a foreigner, not a fellow citizen, and settled in the law of both states rather than in a treaty between them. A person from either may live in the other indefinitely, work without a permit, claim the same welfare, use the same health service, and vote in the other's elections, and none of it depends on membership of any wider union, because it predates every union either of them has joined or left.

It has a very long memory on one side and a very short one on the other, which is itself part of the office. The smaller state's history of the larger is detailed, continuous and taught; the larger state's history of the smaller is episodic and mostly absent from its schools. Both of those facts shape every conversation between them and only one party knows it.

And it has family. Two centuries of movement have produced a population on each island descended from the other in numbers that make the relationship domestic rather than diplomatic: this is not two peoples who trade, it is two peoples who are related and know which cousins they mean.

## Orders

It orders you to hold a grievance and a familiarity at once, without letting either cancel the other. The record is real and is not in dispute in any serious quarter; the intimacy is also real, and daily, and neither of them is allowed to be the whole answer. The office is unavailable to anyone who wants to settle on one.

It orders a choice of words every time the whole is mentioned. There is no neutral noun for this archipelago, so every reference is a small position taken, and the speaker either picks a side, picks the workaround, or talks around it. Nobody living here does this consciously and everybody does it.

## Loses

It loses the ability to be a simple relation of any kind. Two states this entangled cannot be allies in the ordinary sense, because allies are separable, and cannot be one country, because one of them left. What is left has no name in the vocabulary of states, which is why it has to be described in the language of family and why the description always sounds sentimental to whoever is not in it.

And it loses its own protections to their own invisibility. The arrangement holds because nobody examines it, and a thing nobody examines is a thing nobody is defending when it comes up. The one time in a century it was written down was the one time it looked like it might go.

## Drives

It teaches that the state and the border are not the same instrument, and that people can be left alone while goods are counted, which most of the world's frontiers assume is impossible and which these islands have been demonstrating for a hundred years.

It teaches its holder to hear a national conversation from outside and inside at once. Someone with a grandmother from the other island watches both countries' news as a participant, and is never fully surprised by either.

And it drives a habit of not naming the whole. Anyone raised in this can carry on an entire conversation about the archipelago without ever saying what it is, and does not notice they are doing it until somebody from elsewhere asks the obvious question.
