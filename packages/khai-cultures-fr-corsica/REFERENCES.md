# Corsica: References

The provenance behind this culture: the source data that tunes the pitch (kept
here, never in the prose), the public historical record behind the plots,
personas, places, pieces and processes, and the note on the language the package
is written in.

## The language this package is written in

Every file here is written in Corsican, against
[`position_language_co.md`](@chbrain/khai-cultures-tongues/co/position_language_co.md).
That is the house rule and it is the right rule: a culture whose central open
question is the legal standing of its tongue cannot be staged in the tongue that
displaced it.

**The Corsican in this package has not been read by a speaker.** It was written
by the agent that staged the culture, from the tongue position and from written
Corsican, and it should be treated as staged prose awaiting native review, not
as attested usage. The likely faults are the ones a non-speaker makes: the
initial-consonant lenition the tongue position names as its Orders is a
pronunciation rule and does not surface in spelling, so nothing here proves it
was respected; the polynomic question - which variety a given form belongs to - is answered here by defaulting to the northern written norm rather than by
choice; and the French administrative vocabulary the tongue position lists under
Loses is worked around in prose (`statutu particulare`, `Cullettività`) where a
speaker might simply use the French. A native reader should expect to change
words, not the content.

## The defining question, asked of Corsica

**This section replaces an earlier one, and the replacement is the point.**

The first staging answered the defining question with a constitutional line: six
plots, five of them a state acting on a people, an Arc built on four erasures
(1769, 1991, 2003, 2021), and Stakes asking what becomes of a country that had a
state and lost it. It ran the tell that `order_the_defining_question.md`
prescribes and passed, because the Arc, Name and Stakes were themselves
constitutional. **A play whose self-description is a passport passes that tell
perfectly.** The order names the failure - "a play that stages only the statehood
has described a passport" - and the first staging cited the order by name while
committing it. Worse than in Brittany or Alsace, it also argued the point at
length: it defended the constitutional shape as a virtue, calling the arc "a legal
argument, not a security file".

The line now opens on the land. Malaria held the plain, so the island lived at a
thousand metres in two villages a year; Genoa ordered chestnuts planted and the
interior ate flour for three centuries; and with no justice within reach the
villages ran their own, with the voceru as its public instrument. Then Paoli,
Ponte Novu and the emigration, all kept. Then DDT, which is the plot that
reorganises everything around it: five years of spraying made two thousand years
of uninhabitable plain into the island's best farmland, and from 1962 it was
settled by repatriates from Algeria. **That is what makes Aleria legible.** The
1975 siege was at a repatriate's wine cellar on that plain, and the first staging
had the siege without the plain - which is to say, it had the politics of the
event and not its ground.

Three of nine plots are a state acting, which is where the house sits.

The Stakes changed with the line. They no longer ask about belonging to France.
They ask what becomes of a country where every improvement has arrived for
somebody else: the plain became healthy and went to others, the flour came back
as a luxury bought by people who never lived on it, and the language is not
forbidden - it is simply worth nothing, so giving it up costs nothing. That is
one grievance with four instances, and the constitutional version could only see
the last one.

Four things are deliberately not staged:

- **Napoleon.** Born three months after Ponte Novu and present in
  [plot_04](plot_04_ponte_novu_1769.md) as exactly that: the timing. He belongs
  to France's play.
- **1991 as its own plot.** It is in [plot_99](plot_99_una_lingua_senza_statutu.md)
  with 2003 and 2021, where the three court defeats belong together. Giving each
  its own scene is how six plots became five state acts.
- **The armed campaign.** The FLNC years, the Érignac assassination and the
  Colonna affair are held inside plots 07 and 99. Aleria is staged because that
  is where the ground and the claim meet, and nowhere else.
- **Bonifaziu and Calvi.** Ligurian and Genoese remnants inside the same ISO
  code; a variety gets a file in the tongues package or it gets nothing.
  Gallurese in Sardinia is the same question with a border through it.
