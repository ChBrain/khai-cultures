# Plot: The Question Asked and Left Open, since 1999

## Taxonomy

## Owner

## Cue

At the bells the doors go open, the hands cross for a song a ploughman sent to a collector in 1788, and the first-foot comes over the threshold with something in his hand; the country keeps the largest new year in Europe partly because the Kirk spent three centuries disapproving of Christmas, which was not even a holiday here until 1958.

## Action

The Parliament comes back to [Edinburgh](place_edinburgh.md) in 1999 after two hundred and ninety-two years, and in September 2014 the country is asked the question outright and answers it: fifty-five to forty-five against, on a turnout of nearly eighty-five per cent, the highest for any vote Scotland has ever held. Two years later it votes sixty-two to thirty-eight to remain in the European Union, and does so in every single one of its thirty-two council areas, and the union it stayed in leaves anyway. The Saltire is on the flagpoles of both campaigns throughout and belongs to neither. [Eilidh](persona_eilidh.md) argues all of it in the pub, takes nobody's rank on trust, is proud of the country and impatient with its grievances in the same sentence, and has barely caught a word of the oldest of its three tongues.

## Stage

- [Hogmanay](process_hogmanay.md): the year's largest act of fellowship, and the country's answer to a Kirk that would not let it have Christmas.
- [the Saltire](piece_saltire.md): flown by both sides of the argument, which is what makes it the nation's flag rather than a party's.
- [Eilidh](persona_eilidh.md): the levelling temper lived without argument, and the third tongue not inherited.
- [the Scots tongue](@chbrain/khai-cultures-tongues/sco/position_language_sco.md): warm and kent on the street, buttoned away for the interview, in the same mouth on the same day.

## Tension

Scotland has now asked itself the question it spent three centuries not being allowed to ask, and got an answer it did not settle on. What the decade actually records is a country that turns out in numbers nobody else in these islands manages, argues at a pitch that would be rude anywhere else, and is entirely capable of holding a grievance and a self-mockery about the grievance at once. Whether a nation can keep that temper without the question to sharpen it, and keep three tongues when one of them reaches its young by festival rather than by mother, is what the Stakes are for.
