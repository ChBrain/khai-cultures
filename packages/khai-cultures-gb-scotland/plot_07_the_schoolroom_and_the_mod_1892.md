# Plot: The Schoolroom and the Mòd, 1872 to 1892

## Taxonomy

[play_scotland](play_scotland.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

A child in a Hebridean township who has never heard a word of English at home is sent to a new compulsory school where the teaching is in English, the Act that built it makes no provision at all for the language the child actually speaks, and the child is punished for using it in the playground.

## Action

The parish school had been the country's pride for three centuries, the instrument of the levelling learning it boasted about, and in the west it is now the instrument taking the language out. A generation of Gaelic-speaking parents draw the obvious conclusion and speak English to their children for their children's sake, which does more damage than any punishment. Then, twenty years on, the same country that legislated the language out of the classroom founds a society for it in Oban and holds a festival, with adjudicators, medals and a competition, for singing and reciting in Gaelic. What the Mòd formalises is the thing the ceilidh house had been doing on its own for centuries without any medals: the room passing the song round.

## Stage

- [Scottish Gaelic](@chbrain/khai-cultures-tongues/gd/position_language_gd.md): taken out of the schoolroom by one hand and put on a stage by the other, inside twenty years.
- [the Highlands](place_the_highlands.md): the ground where both things happen, already emptying.

## Tension

Scotland is proudest of its schools and this is what its schools did, which is the hardest single fact in the play and the one the national self-image has least room for. And the rescue has a shape that is easier to admire than to live in: a language kept alive in the competition, on the stage and in the medal is being kept, but it is being kept as a performance, and a tongue that survives as something you go and watch is not yet a tongue anybody has to speak.
