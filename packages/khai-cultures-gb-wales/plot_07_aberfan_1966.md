# Plot: Aberfan, 1966

## Taxonomy

[play_wales](play_wales.md)

## Owner

## Cue

The children of Pantglas Junior School have come in on the last morning before half term and are in their classrooms just after a quarter past nine, under a tip of colliery waste that has been built on a mountainside spring and has been moving for years, and which the village had complained about in writing and been told not to worry.

## Action

The tip comes down onto the school and the houses below it. A hundred and forty-four people are killed and a hundred and sixteen of them are children, most of the village's generation in one morning. The men come up out of the pit and dig with their hands beside everyone else. The tribunal reports the following year and puts the blame on the National Coal Board in terms nobody expected from a tribunal, naming ignorance, ineptitude and a failure in communications, and not one person is prosecuted, dismissed or demoted. Then the Board takes a hundred and fifty thousand pounds out of the disaster fund the public had given the village, to help pay for removing the tips that were still there. It is repaid in 1997, and in 2007 the government in Cardiff pays the fund again with what the years had taken off it.

## Tension

The industry the Valleys had given three generations to killed their children by leaving its own rubbish above a school, was found to have done it, and answered by charging the village for the clearing up. Solidarity underground had always been a bargain: you carry the man beside you and the work carries you all. Aberfan is the day the second half of the bargain was shown to be nothing, thirty years before the pits closed, and the Valleys have carried both facts since without either cancelling the other.
