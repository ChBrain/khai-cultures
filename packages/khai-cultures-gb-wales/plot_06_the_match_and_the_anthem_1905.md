# Plot: The Match and the Anthem, 1905

## Taxonomy

[play_wales](play_wales.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

A crowd packs into Cardiff Arms Park in December to watch the touring New Zealanders, who have come through the whole of Britain and Ireland without losing, and who begin by performing a challenge in their own language at the men standing opposite.

## Action

The crowd answers it by singing. What they sing is _Hen Wlad Fy Nhadau_, written fifty years earlier by a weaver and his son in Pontypridd, and it is taken to be the first time a crowd has sung a national anthem before a sporting fixture anywhere. Wales win by a single try to nothing, in a match still argued over a century later, and something is settled that afternoon that no vote had settled: that this nation now assembles itself, in public and by the tens of thousands, around a game and a song rather than around a parliament, a crown or a church. The chapel taught the country four-part harmony and the terraces put it to use, which is why a Welsh crowd sings hymns at a rugby match and means them.

## Tension

A country with no state found a body to be a nation in, and it is an eighty-minute one. That has carried Wales further than anyone expected and it has a floor: the singing is real and it ends at the whistle, and a people whose fullest public expression of itself is a fixture list is placing a great deal of weight on results. The badge says it plainly. The crowd flies the dragon of a people who were never anyone's possession, and the team runs out wearing the feathers of the prince who took the country.
