# Plot: The Delta

## Taxonomy

[play_benelux](play_benelux.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

A bargeman ties up where the river braids into its last channels. His cargo came down from the Alps and the Ruhr and will go onto a sea-going hull tomorrow morning. He has changed language twice on the way down and paid toll to three different lords, and none of that has changed the direction of the water.

## Action

The Rhine, the Meuse and the Scheldt all reach the sea in one low, wet corner of Europe, and everything that half a continent makes has to pass through it to get to the ocean. That single fact builds the medieval cloth towns of Flanders, the Hanseatic and then Dutch shipping trade, and Antwerp and Rotterdam after them, and it makes this ground the richest per square mile in Europe for most of a thousand years. It also makes it the place where every larger neighbour's war goes: whoever holds the delta holds the back door to France, to Germany and to England at once. So the three peoples who live on it become one economy centuries before they are three countries, and someone else's battlefield just as long.

## Stage

- [Belgium](@chbrain/khai-cultures-belgium/play_belgium.md): the ground where the Germanic and Romance halves of the continent meet, and where the wars stop.
- [the Netherlands](@chbrain/khai-cultures-netherlands/play_netherlands.md): the mouth itself, and the people who learned to make land out of the water.
- [Luxembourg](@chbrain/khai-cultures-luxembourg/play_luxembourg.md): the high ground at the delta's back, fortified by everyone who wanted to hold the rest of it.

## Tension

The water makes these three one market and one battlefield in the same act, and neither half of that is chosen. Each of them, separately, has a founding story about living under somebody else's decision - a Burgundian duke, a Spanish garrison, the sea itself - and each learned the same trades from it: accommodate, negotiate, keep what cannot be occupied. That is a real inheritance and it is the only thing they held in common before 1944. But a unity that comes from a river system rather than from a choice explains why they act together and says nothing about what they are to each other. The delta made them partners. It did not make them a we.
