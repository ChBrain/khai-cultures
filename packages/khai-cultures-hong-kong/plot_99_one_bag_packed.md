# Plot: One Bag Packed

## Taxonomy

[play_hong_kong](play_hong_kong.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

A crew with a cherry-picker works a street in Mong Kok, cutting a signboard off the iron bracket it has hung from for fifty years. The tubes go into a skip. It is not a political act and nobody argues with it: it is enforcement of the rules on unauthorised structures, carried out on a form, one sign at a time.

## Action

[The neon is going](piece_the_neon_signs.md). Tens of thousands of signboards have come down across [Kowloon](place_kowloon.md) and the Island under that enforcement, and the street that once lit itself by its own advertising is lit now by lamps like anywhere else. **The city the world pictures as a wall of glowing characters is losing them not to any argument, but to paperwork.**

And the people are going. After the protests of 2019 and the national security law of 2020, Britain opened a route in 2021 to holders of the British National (Overseas) status - a document the departing sovereign had issued as a formality, meant to confer almost nothing. Well over a hundred thousand people used it, with more going to Canada and Australia. **The exit turned out to be the colonial passport nobody had expected to need.** The population fell, and then rose again as the government recruited talent from the mainland. The city is roughly the size it was and is not made of the same people.

And yet the things one might have expected to end have not ended. [The Star Ferry](piece_the_star_ferry.md) has crossed the harbour since 1888 and crosses it still, on fewer fares and after asking for help to keep going - while the Jumbo, the floating restaurant that fed three generations of visitors, was towed out in 2022 and lost at sea. Happy Valley fills on a Wednesday night and [the races](process_the_races.md) are called as they always were. And on Sunday morning [yum cha](process_yumcha.md) runs long, three generations deep, the pot refilled again and again, and you still tap two fingers on the cloth to thank the person who poured for you.

**The rituals are entirely intact. The roll of who is at the table has changed.**

## Tension

The Arc of this play says the city kept **one bag packed and one eye on the harbour**, and that against every expectation it came to love itself as a home. This is the decade the bag was used.

The inheritance turns out to run both ways. A people descended from those who arrived with nothing and expected to leave on the next boat is unusually well equipped to leave - the papers ready, the money moveable, the English fluent, the instinct for the exit older than the colony and older than the border, reaching all the way back to a harbour named for a cargo. `plot_02`'s refugees arrived; their grandchildren departed; **and both moves are the same instinct, running in opposite directions.**

The decade does not settle what this city will be before the fifty years are out, and nothing written here can. What it shows is narrower and stranger. **The neon that made the place legible from the outside is being unscrewed by inspectors. The ferry nobody thought would ever need saving needed saving. And the tea is still poured for the person beside you before yourself.** The open question is not whether Hong Kong is changing. It is which of those three was the city all along.
