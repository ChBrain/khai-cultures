# Position: the four teams

## Taxonomy

Parent group: positions

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Has

One state and four national sides, which no other state on earth has, and four separate governing bodies to go with them, each older than any devolved assembly and none of them created by any government. It has four seats on the board that writes the laws of world football, held continuously since 1886, and a share of the votes out of all proportion to anything except the fact of having got there first.

It has a calendar that says four rather than one, in a way nothing else in public life does. There is no British team, no British anthem sung at a Six Nations match, no single crowd. A person in this state is asked several times a year which of the four they are, by an institution rather than by a pollster, and answers without it being a political act.

## Orders

It orders a choice, and the choice is not the one a passport asks. Supporting a side is not the same as claiming a nationality and the two come apart constantly: a Scot in London, a Welsh speaker in Shrewsbury, a Catholic in Belfast who follows one island's rugby and another island's football. The arrangement obliges everybody to hold a position on a question the state prefers not to put.

It orders the four to keep faith with each other, because each of them holds their standing only as long as the other three hold theirs. No one of these four could defend a seat at that board alone; four can, and have, for a hundred and forty years. It is the one thing in this archipelago that is genuinely a mutual guarantee rather than a hierarchy.

## Loses

It loses the whole. There is no occasion on which this state assembles as itself and feels like one people, because the occasions on which peoples assemble are exactly the ones this arrangement divides into four. The Olympic side exists and is treated by the associations as a hazard rather than a celebration.

It loses England, oddly. Three of the four came out of the arrangement with a team, a flag and an anthem of their own; the fourth has the team and the flag and sings the state's song, because when the arrangement was made England did not think of itself as one of four but as the thing the other three were attached to, and never wrote itself the words.

## Drives

It teaches everybody raised in it that being from somewhere and being governed from somewhere are two different questions, and that the second one is the less interesting. Nobody has to be taught this. It is simply what a fixture list has been demonstrating every season since before their grandparents were born.

And it makes a habit of separate administration feel ordinary rather than separatist. When four governments took four different decisions in the same week and the country called it the four nations approach, it borrowed the phrase from the sport, because the sport had been the only place the idea was already normal.
