# Plot: The ewe

## Taxonomy

Parent group: plot of [the Diné](play_navajo.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

A churro ewe, small, long-wooled and hard to kill, loose on ground that grows almost nothing a person can eat.

## Action

She came up from the Spanish settlements, by trade and by raid, and she changed everything she touched. Land that could not be farmed could be walked, so the whole country became usable at once. The wool became the weaving, and the weaving became the one thing the outside world would reliably pay for. The flock became the way a family counted itself: not what you earned but what you had, standing in a corral, countable by a stranger at a glance. [Running them](process_running_sheep.md) put a child out on the land every day of their childhood, which is where the country and the weather got taught. Within a few generations a people who had arrived as newcomers held the largest herds in the Southwest, and the wool off them went up on [the loom](process_weaving.md) and came down as [something a trader would buy](piece_the_rug.md).

## Stage

- `process`: [running sheep](process_running_sheep.md), the day that shaped every childhood
- `persona`: [Asdzáá Tsosie](persona_asdzaa_tsosie.md), who still keeps what is left of a flock
- `process`: [weaving at the upright loom](process_weaving.md), which is what the wool became

## Tension

The animal that made this people wealthy also made them legible. A flock can be counted from a distance by someone who has not asked permission, and wealth you can count is wealth somebody else can decide there is too much of. Nothing about that was visible for two hundred years.
