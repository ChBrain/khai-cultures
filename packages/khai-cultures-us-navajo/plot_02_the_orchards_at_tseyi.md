# Plot: The orchards at Tséyi'

## Taxonomy

Parent group: plot of [the Diné](play_navajo.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

Peach orchards on the canyon floor in 1864, thousands of trees, planted by people who expected to eat from them for the rest of their lives.

## Action

The soldiers did not come down into [the canyon](place_tseyi.md). They did not need to. They cut the orchards, burned the cornfields, took the sheep and waited, because a place that feeds you can be starved from above. What followed is the march: between eight and nine thousand people walked three hundred miles east under escort, in winter, and those who could not keep up were left. The Diné call the whole of it by the name of the place they were walked to, and use it the way other people use a date.

## Stage

- `place`: [Tséyi'](place_tseyi.md), the shelter that became a trap
- `place`: [Hwééldi](place_hweeldi.md), four hundred miles from any of the four mountains

## Tension

The trees are the part people still name first, ahead of the march and ahead of the dead. An orchard is a statement that you intend to be somewhere in ten years, and cutting one says what the campaign was actually for. It was not a battle and it was not lost; it was a harvest destroyed by men who understood exactly what they were destroying.
