# Piece: Naaltsoos Sání

## Taxonomy

Parent group: piece of [the Diné](play_navajo.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Place

The original is in Washington. Copies hang in chapter houses, in the tribal museum, and framed in a fair number of front rooms.

## Load Bearing

It is the treaty of 1868, and it is the only one of its kind: an agreement in which a people who had been removed were sent back to the land they were removed from. Every legal thing this nation is rests on it, and so does the argument it keeps having with the United States, because a document that gave the land back is also the document that fixed how much.

## Apparent

Handwriting on aged paper, with marks rather than signatures for most of the men on the Diné side, because they were not writers of English and the clerk recorded them as X.

## Yearbook

Signed at Hwééldi in 1868 by men who had spent four years there. It sent between eight and nine thousand people home on foot. It promised sheep, which arrived, and schools, which arrived in a form nobody had agreed to. It has been read in court more often than any other paper this nation holds, and in 2023 the Supreme Court read it and said it obliges the United States to secure no water.
