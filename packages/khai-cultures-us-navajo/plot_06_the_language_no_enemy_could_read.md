# Plot: The language no enemy could read

## Taxonomy

Parent group: plot of [the Diné](play_navajo.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

A language with no written form in general use, unrelated to anything spoken across an ocean, in the mouths of young men in 1942 who had been beaten for it in school.

## Action

The Marine Corps recruited them for exactly the property the schools had tried to destroy. They built a code on top of the language rather than merely speaking it, so that even a Diné speaker who was not trained could not read the traffic, and it was used through the Pacific and never broken. They came home to a country that had classified the work, so they could not say what they had done for twenty-three years, and to a nation where the schools were still running.

## Stage

- `position`: [hózhǫ́](position_hozho.md), and the sings held for men coming back
- `persona`: [Harrison Begay](persona_harrison_begay.md), who still arranges one for a nephew

## Tension

The same government ran both programmes at the same time. One paid these men for the language and the other was still punishing their younger brothers and sisters for it, and nobody at the time appears to have noticed the contradiction, because the two things were filed in different departments. That is not hypocrisy exactly. It is what happens when a state is large enough to do two opposite things without either hand reading the other's paperwork.
