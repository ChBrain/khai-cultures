# Position: k'é

## Taxonomy

Parent group: positions

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Has

A body who holds this has four clans and can say them in order: born to the mother's, born for the father's, and the two grandfathers' behind those. It has the arithmetic that follows from them, which decides at a glance who is a relative, who is a stranger, and who cannot be married.

## Orders

It orders that the relation comes before the business. You give the clans first, the other person gives theirs, and only then does anybody say what they came about. It also orders the hard half: two people who share a clan are brother and sister however far apart they were raised, and that is a prohibition, not a sentiment.

## Loses

It loses its grip where the clans are not taught. A young person raised off the land may have the four and not know what they mean, or have two and be missing the others, and there is no office that can issue the rest.

## Drives

It makes obligation automatic and refusal expensive. If the person in front of you is kin by the arithmetic, the help is not a favour you may decline, and a great deal of what looks from outside like generosity is closer to accounting.
