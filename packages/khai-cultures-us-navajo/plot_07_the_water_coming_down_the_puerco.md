# Plot: The water coming down the Puerco

## Taxonomy

Parent group: plot of [the Diné](play_navajo.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

Water coming down the Puerco on a July morning in 1979, more than ninety million gallons of it, with eleven hundred tons of solids in it, past sheep that are drinking.

## Action

A tailings dam at Church Rock had failed, and it remains the largest accidental release of radioactive material in the country's history, larger by volume than Three Mile Island four months earlier. There were no evacuations and very little coverage. It was the end of a run that had started in the nineteen-forties: hundreds of mines on this land, Diné men working them without ventilation or masks or a word of warning in a language they read, and the tailings left where they lay. Some of the houses were built out of them. Compensation came decades later, through a scheme whose paperwork defeats the people it was written for, and hundreds of mines are still not cleaned up.

## Stage

## Tension

This nation supplied the ore for the weapons and the coal for the cities, and the reservation is where the leftovers stayed. A people whose word for the right order of things is the centre of its religion has a landscape it cannot put back, and no ceremony addresses a tailings pile.
