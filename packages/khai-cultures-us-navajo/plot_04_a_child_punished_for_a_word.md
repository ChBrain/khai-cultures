# Plot: A child punished for a word

## Taxonomy

Parent group: plot of [the Diné](play_navajo.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

A child of seven, hundreds of miles from home in 1887, who has said something in the only language they have and is being punished for it.

## Action

The treaty had promised schools. What arrived was a system whose written purpose was to replace the language and the religion, and which took children far enough away that a parent could not reach them. Hair cut, clothes burned, English enforced by the strap, and the punishment applied for the specific act of speaking. It ran for eighty years. It worked in the way such things work, which is not that it killed the language but that it taught a generation that speaking it to a child was doing the child harm, and that lesson outlasted every school that taught it.

## Stage

- `persona`: [Asdzáá Tsosie](persona_asdzaa_tsosie.md), who was taught that and is still doing it
- `position`: [k'é](position_ke.md), which cannot be taught to a child who is not there

## Tension

This is the only injury in this plot line that the nation inflicts on itself now. The soldiers went home and the agents were reassigned, but the belief they installed stayed in the kitchens, and a grandmother switching to English to spare a grandchild is finishing a job that began in a dormitory in the eighteen-eighties.
