# Plot: One Grid

## Taxonomy

[play_iberia](play_iberia.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

At the same second on an April afternoon, a traffic light dies in Lisbon and a traffic light dies in Madrid. Neither driver knows yet that the other one has stopped too, and neither has any habit of thinking about the other at all.

## Action

In 2025 almost the whole peninsula loses power in seconds - [Spain](@chbrain/khai-cultures-spain/play_spain.md), [Portugal](@chbrain/khai-cultures-portugal/play_portugal.md) and Andorra together, some fifty-five million people - and it becomes very plain, for one afternoon, that they are a single electrical island joined to the rest of Europe by a thin link over the Pyrenees. That is the pattern of everything here. They share the longest land border in the European Union and hardly any rail across it; the rivers that reach the Portuguese sea all rise in Spanish rain, and are argued about only in drought years; the interior empties on both sides of the same line, village by village, in the same decade and for the same reasons. Meanwhile the coasts fill with the rest of the continent moving south for the sun and the rent. The two countries look north, at Europe, and past each other.

## Stage

- [Spain](@chbrain/khai-cultures-spain/play_spain.md): the larger half, whose interior is emptying while its coasts fill with people from further north.
- [Portugal](@chbrain/khai-cultures-portugal/play_portugal.md): the smaller half, doing the same thing on the other side of the line, and priced out of its own cities by the same movement.

## Tension

Seven centuries of not looking, and the peninsula keeps demonstrating that it is one system anyway: one grid, one drought, one set of rivers, one hollowing interior, one arrival of everyone else's remote workers. **None of that has produced a single shared institution beyond the ones Brussels built for them.** The proverb still current in Portugal - from Spain, neither a good wind nor a good marriage - is not hostility; it is the settled form of indifference, which is harder to argue with. This decade does not ask whether the two will merge, because they will not and neither wants to. It asks whether being one system by physics obliges anything at all, or whether the oldest border in Europe has simply taught both sides that the important direction is north.
