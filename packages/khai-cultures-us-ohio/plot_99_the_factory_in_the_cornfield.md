# Plot: the factory in the cornfield

## Taxonomy

[play_ohio](play_ohio.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

Black topsoil east of Columbus lies in spoil ridges beside a concrete basement large enough to read as a district. The field roads have been widened for loads they were not built to carry, and a crane turns above ground that held corn two seasons before. The factory is not making anything yet. The construction site already has.

## Action

In January 2022 Intel announced that two semiconductor factories would be built on nearly a thousand acres in Licking County. The proposal was described as the beginning of a new manufacturing center, but its ground was not a silent mill in Cleveland, Akron, Youngstown or Dayton. It was farmland outside New Albany. Ohio's next industrial map began by drawing roads, substations and a vast excavation across the rural center of the state.

Work started. By early 2025 Intel reported that it had spent $3.7 billion in Ohio, completed the basement level of the first fabrication plant and begun building above it. More than six million hours of construction were in the ground. Those are records. The opening date was not. Chip production had first been attached to 2025; in 2025 the company moved completion of the first plant to 2030 and its start of operations to 2030 or 2031. The promised year arrived with concrete on the site and no chips leaving it.

Northeast Ohio carried a second version of the same industrial turn. General Motors stopped assembling cars at Lordstown in 2019. In 2022 the Ultium Cells battery plant nearby began production, putting a new product and a new joint venture into a region that already knew the shift whistle. Then demand fell. Battery production stopped in January 2026, 850 workers went on temporary layoff and 480 were laid off indefinitely. In August most of the workers returned, and the plant resumed making cells with about 1,400 people expected at work. Within one year the new industrial heartland had already repeated the old one's stop, layoff and recall.

[Harper](persona_harper.md) reads the two sites against [the brownfield redevelopment plan](plan_brownfield_redevelopment.md). The plan's completed cleanups are real, but the largest new factory did not wait for poisoned ground to be assessed, capped and reused. It chose a field where construction could begin at full scale. Her map of vacant industrial land and the map of incoming investment no longer lie on top of each other.

[Samuel](persona_samuel.md) reads the same change from the other side. In the counties around the chip site, farmland became industrial acreage before the first product existed. A farmer does not need to oppose manufacturing to know that soil covered by a fabrication plant will not be planted again. The old bargain between field and factory has become a competition for the same ground.

That makes the present more than a return of manufacturing. It is a relocation inside Ohio: from the old northern valleys toward a greenfield corridor in the center, and from assembly lines that could run for decades to projects whose schedules move with demand before the first shift begins. The [industrial heartland](position_industrial_heartland.md) is active again, but it does not occupy the same heart.

## Tension

Ohio has the construction, the restarted battery line and the returned workers; it does not yet have the chip production that gave the cornfield its meaning. The wager is whether this becomes a bridge between the factory hand and the farmer, and between the old industrial valleys and the new central corridor, or whether each new plant simply moves the state's dividing line. The Portage Path joined two waters by carrying across the ground between them. The present has put a factory on that ground and has not yet shown what, or whom, it can carry across.
