# Plot: The rent on our own land

## Taxonomy

Parent group: plot of [the Catawba](play_catawba.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

The leases, hundreds of them, held by white farmers across a hundred and forty-four thousand acres of Catawba land, paying a rent that had gone from a living to a nuisance as the money moved and the nation did not.

## Action

The nation had leased its land because it was too small to work it and the rents were what it had instead of a crop. Then the tenants outnumbered the landlords by a hundred to one, the state was disinclined to collect for them, and a landlord who cannot collect is a tenant with extra steps. At [Nation Ford](place_nation_ford.md) in 1840 the nation signed it away: the whole tract, in exchange for a sum and a promise that the state would buy them land in the mountains, which the mountain counties refused to sell. The state bought a few hundred acres back near the river instead. The treaty was made with South Carolina and never ratified by the Senate, which nobody present thought mattered.

## Stage

- `place`: [Nation Ford](place_nation_ford.md)
- `position`: [river people](position_river_people.md), holding a river they now rent beside

## Tension

They were dispossessed by a contract they had signed, at a table, with counsel of a sort, for consideration. That is a harder thing to carry than a removal at gunpoint and a harder thing to explain, and it is also the flaw that a much later generation would pick the lock with.
