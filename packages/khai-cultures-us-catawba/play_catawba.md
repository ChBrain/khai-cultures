# Play: the Catawba

## Estate

The Catawba River where it runs through the Carolina Piedmont, red clay and hardwood, an upcountry of rolling ground nothing like the marsh a hundred miles east. A reservation of a few hundred acres near Rock Hill, and before that a hundred and forty-four thousand.

## Name

Yap Ye Iswa, the people of the river. Catawba is the name the neighbours used and the one the treaties wrote down, and the Nation carries it in its own legal title today. The river is in the older name and not in the newer one, which is most of what happened.

## Arc

They were the town that took the others in, and then the smallpox came home with the war party and there was nobody left to take. What survived is the clay: the pots never stopped, not one generation. What did not survive is the mouth. The language sits in a linguist's notebooks, complete enough to teach and too late to inherit.

## Stakes

Whether a people is the thing it can still do or the thing it can no longer say. The Catawba can still make the pot and cannot hold the conversation, and both of those are true on the same afternoon in the same building.
