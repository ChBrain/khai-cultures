## Cue

A footpath leaves the forks of the Susquehanna, follows the river's west branch, finds the ford at the place now called Lock Haven, and turns into the Appalachian ridges. It is only a few feet wide. The ground rises, creek after creek crosses it, and still the line continues west until it reaches Kittanning on the Allegheny. The line is older than either name at its ends.

## Action

For thousands of years, the Great Shamokin Path joined the Indigenous communities of the northern Susquehanna to those of the Allegheny valley. Its eastern end was Shamokin, at the meeting of the Susquehanna's north and west branches; its western end was the Lenape town of Kittanning. Trade, news, diplomacy, families, and war crossed the mountains on foot along the same narrow trace.

That crossing is the first physical fact beneath Pennsylvania. The Appalachians divide the waters: east of the crest they run toward the Chesapeake, and west of it they gather into the Allegheny and flow past [Pittsburgh Point](place_pittsburgh_point.md) toward the Ohio. The path made one lived country across that divide before a colony put a border around it. Later roads and railways followed pieces of its valleys because the old route had already answered the hard question: where a person could get through.

The route did not carry one people in one direction. By the eighteenth century, colonial settlement had pushed Lenape communities from the Delaware and Lehigh valleys into the northern Susquehanna. When settlers pressed them there too, the path became a road west again. In 1772, two hundred Christian Lenape traveled it with the Moravian missionary John Ettwein and a herd of cattle, leaving the Susquehanna for new homes in the Ohio Country. An ancient line of exchange had become a line of displacement.

Penn's charter in plot 1 therefore begins after the crossing and inside it. The colony's eastern archive and western mills, its arguments over land and its claim to hold an arch together, all occupy ground already tied east to west by other feet. [Keystone culture](position_culture_keystone_culture.md) starts here as geography before it becomes a nickname: not a stone placed by a government, but a passage kept open across a range.

## Tension

The same path can prove connection and dispossession. It joined river peoples across the ridges for thousands of years, then carried Lenape families west when settlement took away one home after another. Pennsylvania's keystone is not innocent masonry laid at a founding. It is an older crossing whose usefulness survived every change in who controlled the ground, even when passage meant exile.
