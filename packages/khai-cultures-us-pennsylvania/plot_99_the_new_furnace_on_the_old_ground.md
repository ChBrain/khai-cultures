# Plot: the new furnace on the old ground

## Cue

A furnace flare turns the Ohio River clouds orange on ground where zinc furnaces stood for a century. Across the water, windows reflect the light. The old smelter is gone, the soil beneath it was remediated, and a different industrial complex now fills the same riverbank with pipe racks, cracking furnaces, rail lines, and pellets.

## Action

In November 2022, Shell Polymers Monaca began operating in Potter Township, Beaver County, about thirty miles northwest of Pittsburgh. The 384-acre complex replaced a century-old zinc-smelter site. At the height of construction, about nine thousand workers had been on the project; when the work ended, western Pennsylvania had one of the largest new industrial plants built in the United States in decades.

The new feedstock is ethane from shale gas produced in the Marcellus and Utica basins. Seven furnaces crack it at high temperature into ethylene, and three production lines turn that into polyethylene pellets. The plant was designed to make 1.6 million tonnes a year for packaging, household goods, pipes, and other products. Barges, trains, pipelines, and the Ohio River place it inside the same geography that once brought ore, coal, and steel together around [Pittsburgh Point](place_pittsburgh_point.md).

This is an [industrial transition](plan_industrial_transition.md) that has already happened in concrete and steel. A retired smelter was cleared; a brownfield became a new plant; thousands built it and hundreds operate it. But the material now coming out is plastic, and the material going in comes from shale gas. The valley changed its furnace without leaving extraction behind.

On June 4, 2025, flammable cracked gas flowed backward into Furnace 5 and met lit pilot flames. The explosion ruptured the firebox wall, damaged the furnace, and started a fire. Fifteen workers evacuated. No one was killed or seriously injured, but Shell estimated about ninety-five million dollars in property damage, and about 5,100 pounds of ethylene and combustion products were released. The furnace returned to service about seven months later.

The Chemical Safety Board's final report in September 2026 found that two isolation valves had been opened together while the furnace was being returned to service. A control engineer assigned to the task had not performed it before; the screen showed three nearly identical valves; and safeguards that could have prevented the backflow had not been configured for that operating state. The finding belongs to [industrial grit](position_industrial_grit.md) as much as the construction does: the work is real, the skill has consequences, and endurance cannot substitute for a system designed to keep the people doing it alive.

## Tension

The valley did not refuse a new industry. It cleared poisoned ground, built at enormous scale, trained a workforce, and lit new furnaces. The unresolved question is what counts as transition: replacing a zinc smelter with a plastics complex, or changing the dependence that keeps both the river towns and the extraction economy bound to another furnace. If clean technology is the destination, Monaca is either a bridge built with the skills Pennsylvania already has or another turn of the same road. The record cannot decide which. The next work will.
