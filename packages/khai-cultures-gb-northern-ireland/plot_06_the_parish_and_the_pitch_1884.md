# Plot: The Parish and the Pitch, 1884 to 2001

## Taxonomy

[play_northern_ireland](play_northern_ireland.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

A handful of men take a room in a hotel in Thurles to stop the old field games from dying out, and write rules for hurling and football that tie a club to a parish and a parish to a county, with nobody paid and nobody transferred.

## Action

The thing they build turns out to be the most durable civil institution the island has, and in the northern counties it becomes the parish's own ground in a place where a community has few grounds of its own: the pitch, the Sunday match, the county colours, the young brought up in the games and often in the language along with them. It is also, for most of a century, explicitly a body of one tradition, with a rule barring members of the British forces from membership. That rule is deleted in 2001, three years after the Agreement, by the clubs voting; four years after that the association opens its own stadium to the games it had been founded to keep out, and in 2007 the British anthem is played at Croke Park, on the ground where soldiers shot dead fourteen people at a match in 1920. Nothing about any of those votes was easy and all of them carried.

## Stage

## Tension

An institution that carried one community through a century when it had little else is asked, once the shooting stops, whether it can stop being a fortress without stopping being itself. It answered twice, by vote, and gave up two of the rules that had defined it. The harder question it did not settle is the one it shares with the lodges across the road: whether a body that is genuinely a culture and genuinely a marker of one side can ever be read by the other side as the first thing rather than the second.
