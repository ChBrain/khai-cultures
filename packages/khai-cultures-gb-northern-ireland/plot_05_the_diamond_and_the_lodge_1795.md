# Plot: The Diamond and the Lodge, 1795

## Owner

## Cue

In County Armagh, where the linen trade has put weavers of both religions into the same townlands competing for the same holdings and the same work, two armed factions who have been raiding one another's houses for years meet at a crossroads called the Diamond.

## Action

The fight is short and the consequence is not. The Protestant side comes off the field and founds a society of lodges that same day, with a warrant system, a set of degrees and an annual parade, and the parade is set on the twelfth of July for a battle fought a century earlier on the first, before the calendar was changed. What the Order does is turn a scattering of local factions into an institution with a rulebook, a calendar and a national shape, and what the calendar does is give a community one day a year on which it is unmistakably itself in public, with the banners out, the bands playing, and the bonfires the night before. Two hundred and thirty years later it is still the largest thing that happens here, and the other tradition has long had its own August dates in answer.

## Tension

A tradition that expresses itself by walking has to walk somewhere, and in a place this small the route runs past somebody. That is the whole difficulty in one sentence, and it is not solved by deciding that one side is only being provocative or that the other is only being obstructive, because both the walking and the objecting are genuine. The Twelfth is the clearest case in the play of something that is a real culture to those who hold it and a real imposition to those who do not, with no version available in which it is only one of those.
