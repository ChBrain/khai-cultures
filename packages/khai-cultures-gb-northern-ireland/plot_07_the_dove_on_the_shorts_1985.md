# Plot: The Dove on the Shorts, 1985

## Taxonomy

## Owner

## Cue

A featherweight from Clones, an Ulster town that ended up on the southern side of the line, a Catholic married to a Protestant and living in County Down, is told he must come into the ring under one flag or the other, and declines both.

## Action

He fights under the dove of the United Nations instead, and where the anthem would be his father stands up and sings _Danny Boy_. On a June night in London he takes the world title off a champion who had held it for seven years, and the reaction at home is the thing this plot is for: they came out in the Falls and they came out on the Shankill, in the middle of the worst decade this place has had, for the same man on the same night. He is claimed by everybody and belongs to nobody's side, which is exactly what he arranged, and it works because he settled the flag question by refusing to answer it rather than by finding a compromise answer.

## Stage

## Tension

For one night the place proved it could do the thing everybody said it could not, and the proof was worth exactly one night. That is not a small finding and it is not a large one. What it establishes, and what the Agreement thirteen years later had to build on, is that the appetite for a shared thing was always there and was never the difficulty; the difficulty was that almost nothing in public life was arranged so that both communities could hold it at once, and a man had to invent a flag to get around it.
