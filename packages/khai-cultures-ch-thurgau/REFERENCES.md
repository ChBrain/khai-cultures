## The defining question

**What defines Thurgau, and does the play stage it?**

The version this package replaces answered "apple juice". It had two plots,
`plot_00_mostindien` and `plot_99_der_pendlerkanton`, and between them a blank
where the history is. Its `Name` chapter read `Kanton Thurgau.` and nothing more.

Thurgau's answer is not the orchards. In 1460 the Confederates took the Thurgau
from the Habsburgs and kept it - not as a member and not as an associate, but as
a **Gemeine Herrschaft**, a shared subject territory administered by bailiffs the
ruling cantons supplied in rotation from the castle at Frauenfeld. That lasted
338 years. It ended in 1798 with the old Confederation itself, and Thurgau became
an equal canton in 1803 by Napoleon's Act of Mediation - by decree from Paris,
not by revolt.

That is where the canton comes from, and it explains what the earlier version
could only describe: the mildness, the reluctance to press a case, the readiness
to accept a nickname coined elsewhere and hang it over the door. `plot_00` and
`plot_01` now carry it, `plot_02` keeps Mostindien as what it actually is - an
outside description accepted rather than a self-description - and `plot_99` asks
whether a canton receiving its purpose from outside for the third time will do
something different with it.

**What is deliberately not staged.** The Reformation reached Thurgau from
Konstanz and left it confessionally mixed under the 1531 settlement, which is
genuinely formative and is held in `place_der_bodensee` rather than given a plot,
because the subject-territory arc already carries the same lesson and a fifth
plot would dilute it. A later hand may judge that wrong; this is the argument to
answer.

**On the package.** It was regenerated from `tests/new_culture.mjs` and the
written prose moved across. The version replaced here used `## Grip` / `## Value`
on its positions and `## Input` / `## Method` / `## Output` on its process, none
of which are canon chapters, carried no parent nesting, and was CRLF throughout.
