## The defining question

**What defines Solothurn, and does the play stage it?**

The version this package replaces had the right two anchors - the ambassadors'
city and the Olten junction - and was missing the thing that ties them together
and the moment that made the junction matter nationally.

Solothurn has never had a meaning of its own. It has had three that came from
outside, and it made something first-rate of each before losing it when the
circumstances moved:

- **The number.** Admitted as the **eleventh** canton in 1481 under the Stanser
  Verkommnis, and the ordinal stuck: eleven churches and chapels, eleven towers
  and gates, eleven fountains, and a cathedral with eleven altars, eleven bells
  and three flights of eleven steps. The local beer is called Öufi. Four hundred
  years of building on a position in a list. `plot_00` and `position_die_elf`
  carry it; the branch had none of it.
- **The ambassador.** From 1530 to 1792 the French king's envoy to the
  Confederacy resided here, and with him came the pensions that paid for the
  baroque. `plot_01`, kept from the branch and rewritten to say where the money
  came from.
- **The junction.** Olten is where the lines cross and therefore where the
  country meets when it is in a hurry. `plot_99` had this. What it lacked is
  `plot_02`: in November 1918 the **Oltener Aktionskomitee** ran the national
  general strike from here - a quarter of a million strikers, the army deployed,
  and three dead in Grenchen, in this canton. That is the one time Solothurn's
  through-position made national history, and it belongs in the play.

`plot_03` keeps the branch's Von Roll collapse, which is correct and well
observed, and sets it as the second borrowed identity to expire.

The Stakes ask what the arc actually poses: whether a canton whose significance
has three times been supplied by circumstance has a story of its own, or only a
location.

## One persona replaced

`persona_der_ambassador` cast the French envoy as a Solothurn persona. He is the
foreign party in this relationship, not a member of the culture. `persona_der_patrizier`
stages the same era from the side that belongs here - the councillor who took the
French pension, married into the right houses, paid for an altar and had it
recorded, and who called France "the crown" and the other cantons "the country
people" without hearing the symmetry.

**What is deliberately not staged.** The canton's fragmented shape - exclaves
like Kleinlützel, a border that wanders - is the most visible oddity on the map
and takes no plot. It is a consequence of the patrician city-state buying and
inheriting territory piecemeal, which `plot_01` already carries, and a plot about
a border would describe the map rather than the culture. A later hand may
disagree; this is the argument to answer.

**On the package.** Regenerated from `tests/new_culture.mjs` and the written
prose moved across. What it replaces used `## Grip` / `## Value` on its positions
and `## Input` / `## Method` / `## Output` on its process, none of them canon
chapters; carried no parent nesting; had no `type:` on either persona; and was
CRLF throughout.
