# Position: plantation resistance

## Taxonomy

Parent group: positions

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Has

The long refusal of the people the code was written against, held from the first decades of the colony to the present. It has a march with drums on the Stono road, knowledge of the dye vats that the record credited to someone else, a stolen steamer and a captain's straw hat, and twenty signatures on a petition for equal schools. It holds the habit of asking in exactly the form the other side cannot refuse without showing what it is.

## Orders

That one refuses in whatever form is available: by marching when there is a road, by bluffing when there is a boat, by asking for a bus when only a bus can be asked for; and that one pays for it, in a head on a milepost, a lost job, a burned house, and asks again.

## Loses

Its footing whenever the state answers the request with the law rather than the thing: the Negro Act after Stono, the constitution of 1895 after Smalls sat in Congress, a private academy after Brown. Each refusal won something and was then written around, and the record of the writing-around is always better kept than the record of the refusal.
