## What changed, and why

This culture left the umbrella in the same pull request that rewrote it, and the reasons are on the record here so the next hand does not have to reconstruct them.

**The plots ran backwards and two of three were state acts.** The old line was Fort Sumter 1861, then indigo 1745, then the Gullah Geechee Cultural Heritage Act of 2006: out of order, and with its first and last plots staged from a battery firing and an act of Congress. Every Cue in the line now is a person doing something, and the state appears in each plot as what answers them: the Negro Act after Stono, the 1895 constitution after Smalls, the legislature after Emanuel.

**The old plots put modern archetypes into the past.** Charlotte, a present-day architect, designed restoration strategies in the 1740s and audited Fort Sumter in 1861. The archetypes now appear only in the present, in the wharf plot, and the history is carried by the people who made it.

**Gullah Geechee is its own culture now.** Sweetgrass weaving, the sweetgrass basket and a plot about the 2006 corridor were staged here before `us_gullah_geechee` existed. They are Gullah rather than South Carolinian, and that culture holds its own coiling and its own baskets, so the two files were removed rather than kept as a second copy. Elijah's belonging now points at the Gullah Geechee way, as Yazhi's points at the Diné in Arizona; he stays, because a South Carolina without a Gullah person in it would be false in the other direction.

**The tongue held two languages.** The language position this culture wrote described Charleston English and Gullah grammar as one variety. It moved to the tongues package with the Gullah grammar cut, since `gul` holds it, and with the Charleston vowel corrected; the reasoning is in that package's provenance.

**The indigo plot keeps Eliza Lucas's name on it.** It could have been folded into the others. It stands alone because it is the plot where the record of who knew what gets written down under one name, and that is this culture's habit in a single scene.
