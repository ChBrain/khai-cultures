# Plot: the colony of a colony

## Taxonomy

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

In April 1670 a ship called the Carolina comes up the Ashley River to a bluff the settlers name Albemarle Point, after calling at Barbados on the way. A younger son of a Barbados sugar family walks up the bank. There is no land left for him on an island already carved into plantations, and he has come to a coast where there is nothing but land. What he brings with him is not a plan. It is a habit: he already knows how a plantation is run, and by whom.

## Action

Within the first two years enslaved Africans arrive from Barbados with the men who own them, and the colony is laid out on the pattern of the island. The proprietors in London, in the circle of Lord Ashley and his secretary John Locke, have already written into its Fundamental Constitutions that every freeman shall have absolute power and authority over his negro slaves, so the document a philosopher of liberty helped draft begins by settling who shall have none. In 1680 the town moves down the river to the point between the Ashley and the Cooper, where [the Charleston Historic District](place_charleston_historic_district.md) now stands, and in 1696 the assembly passes a slave code built on Jamaica's, which was built on the Barbados code of 1661. By the first decade of the next century the enslaved outnumber the free.

Every other English colony on the mainland became a slave society over time, by drift and by profit. This one arrived as one. [The code](piece_the_slave_code.md) was not invented here to answer a problem; it was unpacked on arrival, like the tools.

## Stage

- [the Palmetto State](position_culture_palmetto_state.md): a state whose first settlers came from a sugar island and brought its order with them before they had built a house.
- [the slave code](piece_the_slave_code.md): written in Barbados, copied through Jamaica, enacted here in 1696 while the enslaved were still a minority.
- [the Charleston Historic District](place_charleston_historic_district.md): the point between two rivers where the town moved in 1680 and has stood since.

## Tension

Most places ask where they come from and find a founding. South Carolina finds a copy. Its origin is not a frontier and not a refuge; it is an island's surplus younger sons and an island's law, carried nearly two thousand miles north to where land was cheap. Everything in this play, the rice, the indigo, the first shot of the war, the long refusal by the people the code was written against, comes out of the fact that the colony did not have to become what it was. It was what it was when the boat landed.
