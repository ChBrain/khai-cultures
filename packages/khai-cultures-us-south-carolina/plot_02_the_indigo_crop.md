# Plot: The Indigo Crop 1744

## Owner

## Cue

In the summer of 1744 a young woman of about twenty-one walks the vats at Wappoo, the plantation on the creek west of Charles Town that she has been running since she was sixteen, while her father serves as lieutenant governor of Antigua. The leaf has been cut, steeped and beaten, and for the first time in five years of trying the blue settles out of the water and dries into a cake that will sell.

## Action

Eliza Lucas had planted indigo seed her father sent from the West Indies in 1739 and lost it to frost, to worms and to bad seed. He sent her a maker from Montserrat, Nicholas Cromwell, who spoiled the dye on purpose with too much lime, because a Carolina crop would compete with his own island's. The vats she finally made work were built by Quash, a carpenter and builder enslaved on a neighbouring plantation, whom her father asked to have put to making an indigo work, and the leaf was cut, steeped and beaten by enslaved people, some of whom had known the dye in West Africa or the islands before they knew her.

She kept the 1744 crop for seed and gave it out to other planters. The colony exported about five thousand pounds of dye in 1745 and more than a hundred thousand by 1748, the year Parliament put a bounty on it. Indigo grew on the higher ground rice could not use, and its work fell in the gaps of the rice year, so it added a second crop to what an enslaved workforce could be made to produce. Her letter book survives, and so her name is on the crop.

## Tension

The story South Carolina told for two centuries is a true one with the labour taken out: a teenage girl, left in charge, outwits a saboteur and makes the colony rich. All of it happened, and she was remarkable. What the telling leaves out is that the skill she is credited with was partly held by people who could not sign a letter, and that the crop she gave away made slavery here more profitable rather than less. Both are in the family's own papers. The question was never whether the record knew; it was which half of it got read aloud.
