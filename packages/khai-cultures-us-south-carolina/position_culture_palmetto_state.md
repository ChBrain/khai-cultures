# Position: the Palmetto State

## Taxonomy

Parent group: positions

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Has

Belonging to a state that arrived as a slave society rather than becoming one, founded by planters from Barbados who brought the island's code with them, and whose people were a Black majority within forty years of the first landing. It carries two inheritances on the same ground: [historic stewardship](position_historic_stewardship.md), the keeping of Charleston's brick and manners, and [plantation resistance](position_plantation_resistance.md), the refusal that ran from Stono through the Planter to a school bus in Clarendon County. It speaks [South Carolina English](@chbrain/khai-cultures-tongues/en/position_language_en_us_sc.md), split by the fall line between Lowcountry and Upcountry. It lives beside [the Gullah Geechee way](@chbrain/khai-cultures-us-gullah-geechee/position_culture_gullah_geechee.md), which is its own people and not a flavour of this state. It is a way of being [American](@chbrain/khai-cultures-usa/position_culture_american_way.md), not a second passport.

## Orders

That a story is told before a fact is stated, in a register formal enough to pass for manners; that what was built is kept, the brick, the mortar, the street, the name on the crop; and that the past is spoken of with pride. It orders, more quietly and for most of its history, that the half of the past which built the brick is spoken of less.

## Loses

Ground every time the two halves of its record are read side by side: when the founder's name on the indigo turns out to have shared the skill with people it does not name, when the fort where it began the war is the fort a man it held as property sailed past, when the flag it kept up for fifty-four years comes down only after nine people are killed at a Bible study. It loses ground too to the tide, which is coming into the low streets of the city it preserved.

## Drives

Drives South Carolina to keep what it made with unusual care, and is beginning to drive it to keep where it landed people as carefully as where it housed them: a museum on the wharf, a flag in a museum, an apology passed by seven votes to five. Whether that is a reckoning or a second preserved site beside the first is the question it now carries.
