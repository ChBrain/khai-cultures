# Plot: The Planter 1862

## Taxonomy

[play_south_carolina](play_south_carolina.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

Before dawn on 13 May 1862 the white captain and both mates of the Confederate steamer Planter are ashore in Charleston for the night. The pilot, who is enslaved and twenty-three, puts on the captain's coat and his wide straw hat, and orders steam.

## Action

[Robert Smalls](persona_robert_smalls.md) takes the Planter off the wharf with the rest of the enslaved crew, stops at another wharf up the Cooper River for their wives and children, and comes down the harbour at the speed the boat always makes. He gives the whistle signals at each fort as the captain gives them, and stands at the wheelhouse window with his arms crossed the way the captain stands. The last fort is [Fort Sumter](place_fort_sumter.md), where the war began a year earlier, and the Planter passes it close enough for the sentries to call to her and be answered. Out of range, he runs up a white bedsheet and steers for the Union blockade.

Sixteen people are aboard, and the boat carries guns and the Confederate code book. In the North he is a celebrity within a week. He serves the rest of the war piloting for the Union, and in 1863 he buys, at a tax sale, the house in Beaufort where he had been born enslaved, and later, when the widow of the man who had owned him is old and confused and believes the house is still hers, he lets her move back in.

He sits five terms in Congress. In 1895, as one of six Black delegates to the convention that rewrites the state constitution to take the vote away from men like him, he tells it that his race needs no special defence, only an equal chance in the battle of life. The convention takes the vote away.

## Stage

## Tension

South Carolina began the war at Fort Sumter to keep people like the Planter's crew as property, and within thirteen months a member of that crew had sailed past the fort in the captain's hat. The scene does not need improving. What follows it does: the man who took the boat sat in Congress and bought his master's house, and the same state spent the rest of his life and a constitutional convention undoing what he had done. The steamer escaped; the state caught up with everyone else on land.
