# Position: historic stewardship

## Taxonomy

Parent group: positions

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Has

The habit of keeping what was built exactly as it was built: Charleston wrote the first historic zoning in the country in 1931, and the soft lime mortar, the single houses side-on to the street, the piazzas and the cobbles are kept by ordinance and by a trade that still knows how. It has the pride of a city that saved itself before anyone else thought to, and a tourist economy that lives on the result.

## Orders

That the old be repaired with the old: lime and not cement, the original brick and not a match, the house kept on the plan it was built to; that a change to a facade go before a board; and that the visitor be shown the city as it was made.

## Loses

Its balance when the question is not how to keep a thing but whether to: a flag on the State House grounds, a statue over Marion Square, a city that preserved the houses of the people who sold human beings and for most of a century let the wharf where they were sold go unmarked. And when the cost of preserving a district prices out the people whose families lived in it.

## Drives

Drives the keeping of [the Charleston Historic District](place_charleston_historic_district.md) and the craft of [historic masonry](process_historic_masonry.md), is tested over the flag in [Mother Emanuel 2015](plot_05_mother_emanuel.md), and is asked in [the wharf](plot_99_the_wharf.md) to keep the place where people were landed as carefully as it kept the houses.
