# Piece: the slave code

## Taxonomy

Parent group: pieces

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Place

In the statute books of the colonial assembly at Charles Town, enacted in 1696 and rewritten in 1740, and read aloud on plantations and enforced by patrols on every road between them.

## Load Bearing

Without it the plantation is a farm with a labour problem. The code is what made a person into property that the whole colony, not only the owner, was bound to keep in place: it set the patrols, the passes, the punishments, and what an enslaved person could never own, learn or do. It arrived from Barbados by way of Jamaica before the colony needed it, and the economy was built to fit it rather than the other way round.

## Apparent

Pages of clauses in a clerk's hand and then in print, each a single prohibition or a penalty, the 1740 version long enough to forbid drums, horns, gatherings, money, gardens and writing one after another, with the fine for the white man who breaks it set lower than the punishment for the Black one.

## Yearbook

- Copied from the Barbados code of 1661 through Jamaica's and enacted here in 1696, in [the colony of a colony](plot_00_the_colony_of_a_colony.md).
- Rewritten in 1740 as the Negro Act after [Stono 1739](plot_01_stono.md), to take away the drum and the pen.
