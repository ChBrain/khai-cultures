# Plot: Mother Emanuel 2015

## Cue

On the evening of Wednesday, 17 June 2015, twelve people are at Bible study in the fellowship hall of Emanuel African Methodist Episcopal Church on Calhoun Street in Charleston, and they make room for a young white stranger who asks to join them. He sits with them for most of an hour.

## Action

Then he kills nine of them, among them the pastor, Clementa Pinckney, who is also a state senator. The church is the oldest AME congregation in the South; one of its founders was Denmark Vesey, and the building was burned in 1822 after the plot he was hanged for. The killer had posed for photographs with the Confederate battle flag, and said he wanted to start a race war.

Two days later, at his bond hearing, relatives of the dead speak to him over a video link. A daughter of Ethel Lance tells him that she forgives him. Others say the same, or that they are trying to. The city does not riot. It walks, thousands of people, across the bridge over the Cooper River holding hands.

In Columbia, [the flag](piece_the_flag.md) has flown on the State House grounds since 1961, first on the dome for the centennial of the war and since 2000 on a pole beside the Confederate monument, where a compromise put it. Within three weeks the legislature votes to take it down, and on 10 July a guard lowers it and carries it away to a museum.

## Stage

- [the Palmetto State](position_culture_palmetto_state.md): a state that took down in three weeks a flag it had kept up for fifty-four years, and needed nine funerals to do it.
- [the flag](piece_the_flag.md): raised for a centennial, moved for a compromise, lowered after a massacre.
- [historic stewardship](position_historic_stewardship.md): the long habit of keeping what was made, tested by the question of whether a flag is a thing to preserve or a thing to retire.
- [South Carolina English](@chbrain/khai-cultures-tongues/en/position_language_en_us_sc.md): the plain words a daughter used at a bond hearing, which went round the world faster than the flag came down.

## Tension

The forgiveness was real and it was also put to work. Some of the families have said since that the speed with which the state praised their grace let it skip what grace was answering: the street the church stands on is named for John C. Calhoun, whose statue towered over the square nearby until 2020. The flag came down, which no one had been able to make happen for decades, and the cost of it was nine people at a Bible study. Whether the state learned anything it could not have learned for free is the question the families are entitled to keep asking.
