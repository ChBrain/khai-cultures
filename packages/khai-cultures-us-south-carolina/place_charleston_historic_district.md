# Place: the Charleston Historic District

## Taxonomy

Parent group: places

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Shown

The lower peninsula between the Ashley and the Cooper: single houses turned side-on to the street with their piazzas to the sea breeze, cobbles, church spires, the Battery, and soft lime-mortared brick kept by the oldest historic zoning in the country.

## Holds

- the point the colony moved to in 1680, in [the colony of a colony](plot_00_the_colony_of_a_colony.md): the ground between two rivers the town has stood on since.
- the preserved city beside the wharf in [the wharf](plot_99_the_wharf.md): the half of Charleston that was kept, a few streets from the half that was landed.

## Offers

Carriage tours, house museums, garden weekends and the most-praised streets in the South, and a trade in restoration that keeps people like [Charlotte](persona_charlotte.md) in work.

## Withheld

Where the money came from and who was sold in these streets. The slave auctions ran here, in sheds like Ryan's Mart on Chalmers Street, which is now a museum, and most of the ships came in at a wharf the district's tours did not visit for a century. The houses were preserved first, and the story of who built and served them is still being added to the tour.
