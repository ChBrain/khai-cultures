# References: Zürich

## The pitch layer

The pitch is written from Switzerland's national Hofstede profile, inflected by what two thousand years of earning a percentage on other people's goods does to a place: the same low power distance and high individualism, read through a city that translates everything into a comparable quantity. No dimension is named in the prose.

Source: Geert Hofstede, Hofstede Insights (the Country Comparison tool and the six-dimensions model of national culture), the national profile for Switzerland. No separate sub-scores for the cantons are claimed.

## Two decisions, recorded

**The origin is the toll post, not the guild revolution.** The obvious `plot_00` is 1336, and it is the wrong answer for the reason the house order on the defining question (`management/orders/order_the_defining_question.md`) names: a constitutional act read as the culture. What explains this place, and what makes everything after it legible, is older and less flattering - it began as an office that counted other people's goods and took a share, and it has never done anything else. The Reformation's work ethic then lands on a book-keeping that was already there, and Escher's bank is the toll post in its penultimate form.

**Zwingli and Escher are staged and not cast.** Both are national figures with lives far larger than this canton's Company, and both are already known from the outside; a Projection would have to invent the interior neither man's sources supply. What this city owns is the empty church, the platz and the consequences, and those are plots and pieces. A Company is a city's own people.
