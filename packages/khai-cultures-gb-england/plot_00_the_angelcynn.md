# Plot: The Angelcynn, from 450

## Taxonomy

[play_england](play_england.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

Rome's administration is gone from the province and the coin and the garrison with it, and small boatloads of Angles, Saxons and Jutes are settling the eastern river valleys among the people already there, farming, marrying, burying their dead in their own way, and speaking a Germanic tongue that nobody yet calls anything.

## Action

Over three centuries the settlers and the settled become one people, and the tongue becomes one tongue, before any of it becomes one country. By the time Bede sits down at Jarrow in 731 he can write the history of a single _gens Anglorum_ though there are seven kingdoms and no king over them; a hundred and fifty years later Alfred writes of the _Angelcynn_, the English kin, and of _Englisc_, the speech they hold in common, while Wessex is fighting for its life and England as a state does not exist. The word for the people and the word for the language arrive first. The kingdom arrives in 927, four hundred and seventy years after the boats, and it takes the name that was already there.

## Tension

England is one of the few countries whose people and language are older than its state, and it has never quite decided whether that is a boast or a difficulty. Everything the play stages afterwards is done to a country that already knew what to call itself, which is why a conquest could change the ownership of the land without changing the name of the people on it, and why the English have always found it easier to say what they speak than what they are.
