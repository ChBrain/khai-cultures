# References: Nidwalden

## The pitch layer

The pitch is written from Switzerland's national Hofstede profile, inflected by what it does to a community of forty-five thousand that once found out, in a single day, what standing by its own decision costs: the same low power distance and high individualism, read through a place that does not narrate its worst day. No dimension is named in the prose.

Source: Geert Hofstede, Hofstede Insights (the Country Comparison tool and the six-dimensions model of national culture), the national profile for Switzerland. No separate sub-scores for the cantons are claimed.

## Three decisions, recorded

**The catastrophe is staged and not narrated.** The house order on the defining question (`management/orders/order_the_defining_question.md`) values a culture that names its catastrophe, and this one has an unmistakable candidate: 9 September 1798. It is `plot_04`, in the plainest prose in this package. What is deliberately not done is turn it into an identity: there is no museum of that day in Stans and no festival play, and the play says so. Not narrating is itself the form in which this landscape holds it, and staging a commemoration it does not hold would have been an invention.

**Pestalozzi is staged and not cast.** The most important thing that ever left this place is not from this place and would not exist without the worst thing that happened here. He is a Zürcher and belongs to no canton's Company; what belongs here is the town that supplied the occasion and the letter that came out of it. Casting him would let this canton take credit for something it caused rather than made.

**Winkelried is staged and not cast, and the doubt is kept.** The play does not treat the legend as history: the narrative is first attested a century after the battle and is absent from the earliest accounts. What this canton owns is the monument of 1865 and what putting it up says about 1865, which is the more honest inventory - the same decision Uri's play makes about Tell.
