# References: Luzern

## The pitch layer

The pitch is written from Switzerland's national Hofstede profile, inflected by what a hundred and fifty years of living on being looked at does to a place: the same low power distance and high individualism, read through a city that knows exactly how it appears from outside. No dimension is named in the prose.

Source: Geert Hofstede, Hofstede Insights (the Country Comparison tool and the six-dimensions model of national culture), the national profile for Switzerland. No separate sub-scores for the cantons are claimed.

## Three decisions, recorded

**The origin is a change of transport and not a battle.** Sempach is the obvious `plot_00` and was where an earlier draft put it. It is the wrong answer: it is a constitutional moment - the transfer from Habsburg to the Confederation - and it explains a status rather than a culture. What explains Lucerne is that it exists where boat becomes road, and that its first and greatest building is therefore a bridge with its own history hanging in the roof. Everything since is the same arrangement with different travellers: pack trains, pilgrims, tourists.

**The Lion Monument is not staged.** It is the most photographed object in the city and it commemorates Swiss mercenaries killed in Paris in 1792 - a story that belongs to the mercenary trade and to France, not to what this city does. Staging it because it is famous would be casting for the counter rather than for the play; the pieces here are the bridge, which is the city's own reason for existing.

**Tell and the Rütli are not reached for.** The lake's founding story belongs to the neighbouring cantons and is staged in their plays; a culture may reach another's positions and never its cast. What this canton owns is the view of it and the fare for the crossing, which is the more honest inventory and is what `place_vierwaldstaettersee.md` withholds.
