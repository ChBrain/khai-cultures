# Plot: A name at the edge of the map

## Taxonomy

Parent group: plots

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

Somewhere in the second century of the common era, in Alexandria, a geographer
who has never been north of the Mediterranean writes the Kimbroi onto the top of
the Jutland peninsula, and that sentence becomes the oldest thing anyone has
about where they were from.

## Action

It is not a founding and it is not a memory. It is a placement, made three
hundred years after the people it places were destroyed, by a man working from
reports of reports.

Everything upstream of it is inference. The name may be older than Grimm's law,
which would make it Germanic; it may have come through Celtic mouths, which is
why Latin writes a `c` where a Germanic word would later carry an `h`. It may
mean the people of the rim, the coast-dwellers. It may name a body of water.

Those are readings of a word, not records of a people.

## Stage

A library in Alexandria. A peninsula the librarian will never see.

## Tension

The origin of a sunken people is the earliest place somebody else wrote them
down, and that is not the same thing.

The archaeology has looked for the departure this placement implies and has not
found it: no clear trace of a mass migration out of Jutland in the early Iron
Age. So the line at the top of Ptolemy's map may record where the Cimbri came
from, or where a name like theirs stuck to a coast, or nothing.

The house holds it as the origin because it is what there is, and says plainly
that what there is is a sentence in Greek.
