# Play: Cimbri

## Estate

A people that ended. Held here because the living are holding their remains and
saying something about them: Denmark keeps the objects, and four hundred people
in an Alpine village keep the name.

This is a sunken production. It carries no ISO code, because no state recognises
the Cimbri and none ever will, and it declares `khai.sunken` and never
`khai.production`, so the house's culture count cannot move by its existing. See
`management/orders/order_the_sunken.md`.

## Name

Cimbri, in Latin. Kimbroi, in Ptolemy's Greek. Nothing survives of what they
called themselves, because nothing survives of their language beyond a handful of
names written down by other people.

The prose here is in English for the reason `nato` and `the_four_nations` are:
there is no single tongue to perform. In their case a group spans several. In this
one there is none. A play written in Latin would be written in the language of the
army that finished them, and this play is not Rome's.

## Arc

Known almost entirely through the accounts of those who destroyed them, and held
now by people who are not their heirs.

Their objects are kept by a region that may not descend from them, and their name
is worn by a village that certainly does not.

They come south out of the record's edge in 113 BC, they beat Roman armies for
twelve years, and in 101 BC they end in a single afternoon. What is left is held
in two places that do not know about each other, and neither of them has the
descent.

Rome is the other half of this line and not its antagonist. The war remade the
Roman army and made Marius consul five times, so the Cimbri sit in Rome's story as
firmly as Rome sits in theirs; a Roman play, when the house has one, casts
`plot_03` from here rather than restating it.

## Triggers

**[Plot 0: A name at the edge of the map](plot_00_the_edge_of_the_map.md)**

A Greek geographer who never went there writes the Kimbroi onto the top of Jutland, and that sentence is the oldest thing anyone has about them; the origin is a line on somebody else's map.

**[Plot 1: The people move, 113 BC](plot_01_the_people_move_113.md)**

Not an army but a people - wagons, families, herds - comes south out of the north and asks the Roman Senate for land, and is refused.

**[Plot 2: The blood in the cauldron](plot_02_the_blood_in_the_cauldron.md)**

[The seeresses](persona_the_seeress.md) cut the throats of prisoners over a bronze vessel and read what runs into it, and Strabo writes it down; nineteen centuries later that sentence will be read against [an object in a Danish bog](@chbrain/khai-cultures-denmark/piece_gundestrupkarret.md).

**[Plot 3: The ally who never came, 102 BC](plot_03_the_ally_who_never_came_102.md)**

They come down the Adige into Italy and wait in the Po valley for the Teutones to join them from Gaul; the Teutones were destroyed at Aquae Sextiae the year before, and the Cimbri spend a season looking for an army that is already dead.

**[Plot 4: The wagons at Vercellae, 101 BC](plot_04_the_wagons_at_vercellae_101.md)**

On [the Raudine Plain](place_raudine_plain.md) the line breaks and the women behind it, walled in by their own wagons, take the rope rather than the slave market; [Boiorix](persona_boiorix.md) dies in the field and the people stop.

**[Plot 99: Two custodies](plot_99_two_custodies.md)**

[A cauldron](@chbrain/khai-cultures-denmark/piece_gundestrupkarret.md) that is probably Thracian sits in a Copenhagen case while [a bronze bull](@chbrain/khai-cultures-denmark/piece_cimbrertyren.md) stands in Aalborg, and fifteen hundred kilometres south four hundred people in Luserna answer to the name; neither custody has the descent.

## Stakes

Whether a people can be held at all once nobody is them.

What this play refuses is the easy version, in which [Himmerland](@chbrain/khai-cultures-denmark/place_himmerland.md) is the heir and the objects are the inheritance. The archaeology finds no clear trace of a mass migration out of Jutland; the cauldron is Thracian or Dacian work; the bull is from 1937. The remains are held, not inherited, and a sunken play that let a living culture launder a borrowed origin into a real one would have done the opposite of its job.
