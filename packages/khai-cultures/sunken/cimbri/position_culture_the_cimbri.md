# Position: Being Cimbri

## Taxonomy

Parent group: positions

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Has

Almost nothing of its own. What is held about being Cimbri comes through Roman
and Greek hands: a migration that moved with its families, a rite involving
prisoners and a vessel, four kings with names, and an ending.

No law survives, no song, no account of a marriage or a burial in their own
words, and no continuous text in their language. The position is thin because the
record is thin, and writing it fuller would be writing it false.

## Orders

That a people be understood through what it did to somebody else's history.
Everything datable about the Cimbri is dated by Roman consulships, because the
calendar the record uses is the calendar of the people they were fighting.

## Loses

The interior. A culture is mostly the part that never gets written down, and for
the Cimbri that part is gone entirely: there is no ordinary day in the record, no
year without an army in it.

It loses its own name, too. Cimbri is Latin, and what they called themselves is
not recoverable.

## Drives

It drives a reader to notice how little is needed for a name to outlive a people.
Two custodies, a bog find, a Greek paragraph and a bronze bull have kept the word
Cimbri in use for two thousand one hundred years after the last person who could
have claimed it died on a plain in northern Italy.
