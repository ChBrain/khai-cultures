# Plot: The people move, 113 BC

## Taxonomy

Parent group: plots

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

In 113 BC a people arrives at the edge of Roman awareness in Noricum, and what
arrives is not an army. It is wagons, women, children and herds, moving together,
looking for somewhere to stop.

## Action

They ask for land. The Senate refuses, and sends a consul.

What follows is twelve years in which Rome loses repeatedly to people it has
classified as a migration rather than a state: Noreia in 113, the Rhône in 109,
Burdigala in 107, and at Arausio on 6 October 105 the worst Roman defeat since
Cannae - the ancient accounts claim eighty thousand soldiers and forty thousand
camp followers, and ancient accounts of casualties are propaganda before they are
arithmetic.

The Cimbri do not follow the victory to Rome. They turn away, into Spain and
Gaul, for reasons no source explains and every source finds strange.

## Stage

The Alpine passes. The Rhône valley. Roads Rome built, walked the wrong way.

## Tension

Rome remembers this as the _terror cimbricus_, a fright that reorganised its army
and made Marius consul five times.

That is Rome's account of what the Cimbri were for. From inside, the thing that
moved south was a population, and everything it did before Arausio it did while
carrying its own children. A people that wanted land is remembered as a weather
event, because the people who remembered it were the ones it happened to.
