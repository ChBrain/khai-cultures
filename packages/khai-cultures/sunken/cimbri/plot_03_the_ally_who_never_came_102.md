# Plot: The ally who never came, 102 BC

## Taxonomy

Parent group: plots

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

In the spring of 102 BC the Cimbri come down the valley of the Adige into Italy,
push the proconsul Catulus back across the river behind a burning bridge, and
spread out into the Po valley to wait for the other half of the migration to
join them.

## Action

They had split years earlier. The Teutones and the Ambrones went one way into
Gaul, the Cimbri another, and the plan - as far as any plan can be read out of
Roman accounts of somebody else's movements - was to come into Italy from two
directions and meet.

So the Cimbri winter in the Po valley, and in 101 they move west, looking.

The Teutones are not coming. Marius destroyed them at Aquae Sextiae in 102, while
the Cimbri were still in the Alps, and the news either did not reach them or was
not believed. They spend a season in somebody else's country hunting for an army
that has already been killed.

## Stage

The Adige valley in spring. A bridge burned from the Roman side. A wide fertile
plain with the harvest in it and nobody defending it, and the road west.

## Tension

This is where the Cimbri and Rome become one story rather than two, and the shape
of it is not a duel: it is a Roman consul choosing which half to fight first,
and being allowed to fight them one at a time.

Nothing in the record says the Cimbri knew. That is the part worth holding. A
people that beat Roman armies for a decade lost because it could not find out,
in one winter, something that had happened a month's march away - and the only
account of their searching is written by the people who had already won.

The relationship runs the other way too. Rome reorganised its army around this
war and made Marius consul five times running for it, so the Cimbri are in Rome's
line as thoroughly as Rome is in theirs. A play for Rome, when the house has one,
casts this plot from here rather than restating it.
