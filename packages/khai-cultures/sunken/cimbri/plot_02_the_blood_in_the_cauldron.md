# Plot: The blood in the cauldron

## Taxonomy

Parent group: plots

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

After a victory, grey-haired women in white lead the prisoners up onto a
platform. [One of them](persona_the_seeress.md) cuts a throat over the rim of a
bronze vessel, and the others watch what runs into it and say what is coming.

## Action

Strabo writes this down, and it is almost the only thing anyone records about
what these people did when they were not fighting. Not their law, not their
farming, not their dead. A rite, described by a Greek, at second hand.

They also opened the bodies and read the entrails. The detail is specific enough
to have come from somebody who claimed to have seen it, and distant enough to
have been assembled out of what Greeks already believed about northern peoples.

Both are possible. The house holds it because it is the only interior the record
offers, and because of what it would later do.

## Stage

A platform beside a camp. A vessel large enough to catch a man's blood.

## Tension

Nineteen centuries later, two men cutting peat in a Himmerland bog lift out
[a silver cauldron](@chbrain/khai-cultures-denmark/piece_gundestrupkarret.md),
and Denmark reads Strabo against it.

That is the whole mechanism of the claim, and it is worth naming precisely: the
attribution was not arbitrary, and it was not evidence either. It was a Greek
sentence about a cauldron laid over a Danish object that happened to be one. The
silver is Thracian or Dacian work from the lower Danube, and the Cimbri are
credited with it because of a paragraph in the Geographica.

A people with almost no interior left gets handed one, out of the only rite
anybody bothered to write down.
