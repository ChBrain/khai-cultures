# Plot: Two custodies

## Taxonomy

Parent group: plots

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

The objects are in Denmark and the name is in Italy, and the two custodies have
never heard of each other.

## Action

In Copenhagen, in a case at the Nationalmuseet, stands
[the cauldron](@chbrain/khai-cultures-denmark/piece_gundestrupkarret.md) that two
men cut out of a Himmerland bog on 28 May 1891: nine kilos of silver, ninety-six
per cent pure, sixty-nine centimetres across. In Aalborg,
[a bronze bull](@chbrain/khai-cultures-denmark/piece_cimbrertyren.md) has stood
since 14 April 1937, paid for by a distillery and planted on its own closed
factory, and from there it walked into the arms of Aars Municipality - the same
Aars whose bog held the cauldron - and of the old Aalborg Amt, and into the mark
of an insurance company. [Himmerland](@chbrain/khai-cultures-denmark/place_himmerland.md)
carries the name in its own: Himbersyssel, in the old spelling.

Fifteen hundred kilometres south, on the plateaus above Vicenza and Trento, some
two thousand people speak a Germanic tongue called Cimbrian and call themselves
Cimbri. Only Luserna still raises children in it.

## Stage

A display case. A plinth on a square. A coat of arms. A village school where the
language is taught for an hour a week.

## Tension

Neither custody has the descent, and the two fail in different ways.

Denmark holds the objects and cannot show the people. The archaeology finds no
clear trace of a mass migration out of Jutland; the silver is Thracian or Dacian
work from the lower Danube; and the bull is a twentieth-century bronze, cast
twenty-seven years after a bog find and seventy-three after the loss of Schleswig,
by a region that had reason to want an ancestry.

Luserna holds the name and cannot show the objects. Its Cimbri are the
descendants of Bavarian carpenters, shepherds and woodcutters invited south by
the princes of Trento in the Middle Ages, and the identification with the people
who died at Vercellae is a scholarly guess of the fifteenth century that stuck
hard enough to become an endonym. They are not wrong about who they are. They are
wrong about who they are named after, and it has stopped mattering to them, which
is its own kind of answer.

So there are two inheritances and no heir. That is what custody of a sunken
people looks like when it is described honestly: the objects held by somebody
they probably did not belong to, the name worn by somebody it probably did not
belong to, and the people themselves present in neither.

What the house records is the holding, not a lineage. Nobody is Cimbri.
