# Plot: The Flag Comes Back, 1996

## Taxonomy

[play_england](play_england.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

A football tournament is played in England for the first time in thirty years, and the crowd that turns up to watch its own team is holding a flag it has not much used: not the union of the four nations that England supporters had waved for decades, but the plain red cross on white that belongs to England alone.

## Action

Through the summer the red cross is on faces, on cars, on pub windows and church towers, and a song about thirty years of hurt is sung by strangers in the street by people who do not, as a rule, sing in the street. England go out to Germany in the semi-final on a penalty, and the flag does not go away afterwards. The following year Scotland and Wales vote for parliaments of their own, and by 1999 all three of the other nations have a body that speaks for them by name. England, for the first time, is looking at a flag of its own and no assembly to match it.

## Stage

- [the St George's Cross](piece_st_georges_cross.md): the country's own mark, out of the church tower and onto the terrace, where England learns to cheer for itself under its own name.
- [understatement](position_understatement.md): the habit the tournament suspends for a month, and the one the country goes back to the moment it is over.

## Tension

For three hundred years England could leave the question of what it was to the union it led, and answer for Britain instead. A flag is not an answer, but it is the first time in living memory that the question is asked out loud, by a crowd rather than by a constitution, and asked in the one place where the English have always been allowed to feel something in public.
