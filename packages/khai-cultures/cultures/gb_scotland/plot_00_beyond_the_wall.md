# Plot: Beyond the Wall, 122 to 563

## Taxonomy

[play_scotland](play_scotland.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

The peoples living north of the Tyne and the Solway watch an empire arrive, build a wall across the neck of the island, push a second one a hundred miles further up, hold it for about twenty years, and then come back down and stay behind the first.

## Action

Rome campaigns north again and again and never keeps it, and the consequence is not a victory but an absence: no Roman roads to speak of, no towns, no villas, no Latin administration, and no habit of being governed from far away. What arrives instead comes by sea and from the west. Gaelic-speakers cross the narrow water from Ireland into Argyll; the Picts hold the north and east; a British tongue holds the south-west and a Northumbrian one pushes into the south-east; the Norse will take the isles. In 563 Columba lands on Iona and the faith and the letters come in over the Atlantic from Ireland rather than up the road from Rome. Out of that, over four centuries, one kingdom is assembled from at least four peoples, and it is assembled by marriage, inheritance and war rather than by anybody's plan.

## Stage

- [Scottish Gaelic](@chbrain/khai-cultures-tongues/gd/position_language_gd.md): the tongue that comes over the water from Ireland and gives the country the name of the people who brought it.
- [the Highlands](place_the_highlands.md): the ground the empire looked at and declined, and the ground the sea road reaches when the land road does not.

## Tension

Scotland is the part of the island that was never Roman, and the whole play runs on what that left. It gets its letters and its faith from the west instead of the south, so it never quite belongs to the same story as its neighbour; it is put together out of several peoples, so it has three tongues in its cast and no single one to call its own; and it has no inherited habit of obedience to a distant centre, which is a fine thing to have when the distant centre is Rome and a harder thing to live with afterwards.
