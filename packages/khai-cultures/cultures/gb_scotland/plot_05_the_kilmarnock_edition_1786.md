# Plot: The Kilmarnock Edition, 1786

## Taxonomy

[play_scotland](play_scotland.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

A tenant farmer in Ayrshire, in debt, in trouble with the Kirk session, and with a berth as good as booked on a ship to Jamaica, scrapes together the subscriptions for a small book of his own verse, printed locally, in the tongue he speaks rather than the one the drawing rooms of the capital have decided is correct.

## Action

Six hundred and twelve copies come off the press in Kilmarnock in July and are gone within weeks, and the ship sails without him. What the book does is not simply give the country a poet: the Enlightenment forty miles away in [Edinburgh](place_edinburgh.md) had been improving its Scotticisms out of its prose and teaching itself to write like London, and here is a ploughman doing the highest thing the language can do in the plainest form of it, and being read for it. [Robert Burns](persona_burns.md) then spends much of the rest of his short life going round collecting the old songs before they are lost and mending them where they are broken, and in 1788 he sends off one he says he took down from an old man's singing. It is the song the world now uses to end a year.

## Stage

- [Robert Burns](persona_burns.md): the ploughman who proves the farmer's tongue will carry any thought a gentleman's will, and is believed.
- [the Scots tongue](@chbrain/khai-cultures-tongues/sco/position_language_sco.md): raised here from a clatter the genteel apologise for into the language of the nation's own poet.
- [egalitarianism](position_egalitarianism.md): _a man's a man for a' that_, argued in a song rather than a treatise and consequently remembered.

## Tension

The country ends the century holding two answers to the same question at once. Its philosophers made their reputations by writing an English better than the English, and its poet made his by refusing to, and both are claimed with equal pride. That is the seam the Scots tongue has lived on ever since: honoured once a year in the mouth of the national bard and corrected the rest of the time in the mouths of everybody else.
