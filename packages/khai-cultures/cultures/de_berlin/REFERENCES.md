## The defining question

Before this pass, the play opened at the Berlin Wall in 1961: a city founded around the 13th century, seat of Prussia and of the Kaiserreich, staged nothing of itself before division. Reading the Arc against the plots surfaced three unstaged claims: "ständig im Umbau" (always under construction/unfinished), "Arm, aber sexy" (a direct mayoral quote, unstaged), and "Start-ups" (named but never a scene).

`plot_00` was set at the Edict of Potsdam (1685) rather than the founding of Berlin/Cölln (c. 1237) or Friedrich II's Enlightenment court: the Huguenot resettlement is the earliest point at which something still true of Berlin - that its population and character are made by people arriving rather than by descent - is visibly caused, not merely present. The marsh-crossing founding explains geography, not character; a pure Enlightenment-tolerance framing would have told the flattering version of a policy that was, at the time, demographic triage.

`plot_01` (Weltstadt, 1920-1933) was added because the play's own account of Berlin's nightlife-driven freedom ("Techno-Subkultur", "die Freiheit") implicitly dated that freedom to 1990, when the city had already built and lost an equivalent world city a piece of Berlin's institutions had not staged: the Institut für Sexualwissenschaft and the Weimar-era club scene. This directly answers the "cultural moment, not constitutional" test from `order_the_defining_question.md`: it would have been easy, and wrong, to fill the pre-1961 gap with "capital of Prussia" or "capital of the Kaiserreich" - true, but constitutional, and no more defining of Berlin than any other national capital's administrative history.

`plot_05` and `plot_99` stage what is genuinely Berlin's own rather than Germany's: the startup boom and its self-inflicted rent pressure, BER's decade-long dysfunction, the Mietendeckel's rise and fall, the 2021 referendum, and the 2023 election rerun - none of which belong to Germany's national plot line.

Left unstaged deliberately: "Spree" (Name chapter) is a generic river mention true of most riverside capitals and was judged not load-bearing enough to warrant its own Place. "Berliner Schnauze" (the dialect) is addressed only indirectly, via `plot_00`'s note that the dialect carries French loanwords from the Huguenot era; a full dialect-history plot was judged out of scope for this pass.
