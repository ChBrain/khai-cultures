# North Rhine-Westphalia: References

The provenance behind this culture: the Hofstede source data that tunes the pitch (kept here, never in the prose) and the public-domain historical sources behind the plots, personas, places, and pieces.

## The defining question

Asked against this culture on 2026-08-28: the play's own Name and Arc name "Nordrhein-Westfalen" and stage the Rhenish half at length (Kölner Dom, Karneval, rheinischer Frohsinn), but every plot before this pass opened in 1850, after the two halves already existed, and "Westfalen" itself never appeared as anything but the silent, administratively-joined other half of a 1946 marriage. The Arc's own words, "westfälisch weiter oben herrscht Schweigen", named a temperament the play never staged.

The answer is that North Rhine-Westphalia's two temperaments are not an invention of the 1946 occupation-era merger: Rome built a bishopric on the Rhine by the early 4th century while the Saxon lands to the east had neither city nor script until Charlemagne's conquest (772 to 804) and Corvey's monks gave them their first written self-account in the late 10th century (Widukind of Corvey's "Sachsengeschichte"). The Archbishop of Cologne then ruled the Duchy of Westphalia in person from 1180 to 1803, six centuries of shared rule that never produced one people, which is exactly the gap "Operation Marriage" tried to close a second time in 1946. [plot_00_zwei_urspruenge.md](plot_00_zwei_urspruenge.md) stages this as the culture's origin, not a state founding: the split predates any state that could have joined it.

The Peace of Westphalia (Münster, 1648), the event that actually gave the name "Westfalen" its standing weight in the world three centuries before it named a Land, was first drafted as a paragraph inside the 1946 plot to avoid renumbering the existing plot line for an event chronologically earlier than the old plot_01. On review this was corrected: an event of this consequence, named after this place, signed in a room whose 1577 panelling is still on the wall, is not a paragraph inside another plot. [plot_01_westfaelischer_friede_1648.md](plot_01_westfaelischer_friede_1648.md) now carries it on its own, and the plot line was renumbered accordingly (plot_00 unchanged; the Peace of Westphalia is plot_01; Ruhr Industrialization, Operation Marriage, and the Coal Phase-out move to plot_02, plot_03, and plot_04). [plot_03_creation_1946.md](plot_03_creation_1946.md) keeps its own Tension about the two temperaments intact, independent of the 1648 material, anchored instead in the 1180-to-1803 rule of the Archbishop of Cologne over the Duchy of Westphalia that [plot_00_zwei_urspruenge.md](plot_00_zwei_urspruenge.md) stages.

The house also asks every touched culture for a record of the decade still resolving: [plot_99_hochwasser_2021.md](plot_99_hochwasser_2021.md) stages the July 2021 flood, the most severe natural disaster to hit the Land in living memory, whose reconstruction was still open years later, alongside a second beat from the same decade: the accelerated lignite phase-out (2030 instead of 2038) agreed in October 2022, and the demolition of the village of Lützerath for the Garzweiler open-pit mine in January 2023, so that the plot records both water arriving and coal still being dug in the same Land in the same ten years.
