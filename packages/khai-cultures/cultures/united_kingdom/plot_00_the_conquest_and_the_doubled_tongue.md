# Plot: the Conquest and the doubled tongue, 1066

## Taxonomy

## Owner

## Cue

The island has been arrived at for a thousand years and never emptied. Britons, then legions, then Angles and Saxons, then Danes: each wave takes the land and none replaces the people already on it. In the autumn of 1066 the last of them lands near Hastings, and on the fourteenth of October an English king is killed on a ridge above it.

## Action

William takes the crown, and then does the thing no earlier conqueror managed: he replaces the entire ruling class. Within twenty years almost no English lord holds land of any consequence, and the survey he orders in 1086 writes down who holds what, from whom, and what it is worth, kingdom-wide. What it also does, without meaning to, is fix a language in two layers. The lords speak French and the people speak English, and for three hundred years the two sit one above the other in the same country until they fuse rather than separate. The animal in the field keeps its English name and the meat on the table takes the French one; the man who works is a worker and the man who commands is a governor; and English comes out of it with two words for almost everything, one plain and one grand, and no neutral way to choose between them.

## Stage

- [class](position_class.md): the unwritten order of rank the Conquest installs and the language then carries, so that a thousand years later a sentence still places whoever says it.

## Tension

A conquest is the wrong shape for an origin: it is done to a people, not by them. But this one is where the island stops being conquered and starts being layered, and what it leaves is not a French Britain or an English one but a doubled Britain, which is the country that follows. The class the Normans installed will be the thing the union argues with for nine hundred years and never abolishes, and the tongue they forced will become the tongue the world borrows. Both begin here, and neither was intended.
