# Plot: Windrush, 1948 to 1971

## Taxonomy

[play_united_kingdom](play_united_kingdom.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

The British Nationality Act of 1948 makes every subject of the empire and the new Commonwealth a citizen of the United Kingdom and Colonies, with the right to come and to stay. It is passed without much argument, because nobody in Westminster expects it to be taken up.

## Action

On the twenty-second of June 1948, a fortnight before the health service opens, the Empire Windrush docks at Tilbury with just over a thousand passengers from the Caribbean aboard, many of them ex-servicemen who had already fought for the country they are now arriving in. Others follow, from the Caribbean, then from India and Pakistan, then from east Africa, invited by a country short of labour and reluctant about the people who answer. They staff the new hospitals and drive the buses. They meet lodging-house windows that say no coloured, riots in Notting Hill in 1958, and a member of parliament in 1968 predicting rivers of blood. And in 1971 an Immigration Act ends the right the 1948 Act had granted, keeping those already settled but closing the door behind them - a settlement made by taking back what had been offered, which is how Britain usually resolves things.

## Stage

- [London](place_london.md): where most of them land up, and the city the arrivals remake fastest.
- [Dover](place_dover.md): the island's own gateway, which had faced an occupied continent eight years earlier and now watches the empire come the other way.

## Tension

The empire came home and the country was surprised, which it had no right to be: the ships ran both ways for two hundred years and only one direction had ever been thought of as movement. What arrives with the passengers is the end of the idea that the island is a thing apart from what it ruled. [Aisha](persona_aisha.md) is born out of this, three decades later and a Londoner by birth, at home in a Britain this made and in no need of anyone's permission for it - which is the settlement the 1971 Act could close the door on but not undo.
