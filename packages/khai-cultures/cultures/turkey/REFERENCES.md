Hofstede's model of national culture and names no dimension and carries no
number. The scores and their source are recorded here so the data is on the

The two defining personas, [Ayşe](persona_ayse.md) and [Can](persona_can.md),
are archetypes, not historical individuals: composites that carry the tension
between the older generation (Anatolian faith, tea, the evil eye, the family, the
mother tongue, the children emigrated to Germany) and the younger one (between

the pull of German and global English on the language).

## The defining question

Per the house order on the defining question
(`management/orders/order_the_defining_question.md`), this play's Arc was already
cultural rather than constitutional - a people off the steppe, a language cut
from its own script, a heart divided between a secular state and a devout
Anatolia. The tell found three holes anyway, and all three were things the Arc
says about itself.

**The steppe.** The Arc opens on a people who came from Central Asia and the play
began at Manzikert, four hundred years after they set out. `plot_00` answers the
origin where it belongs: at the two stones of 732 and 735, on which this people
writes about itself for the first time, in its own script, and carves the word
Türk. The origin here is not a victory but an act of writing - and its Tension is
the one this whole play turns on, because the same name has since been written in
three alphabets and the people who first wrote about themselves are the people
who cannot read what they wrote.

**What the Arc calls a nation on two continents** had no scene for the moment
that decided who lived on them. `plot_04` stages the exchange of 1923 and 1924,
in which belonging was settled by a list and a criterion - religion, not language,
so Turkish-speaking Orthodox left and Greek-speaking Muslims arrived and were
called half-infidels for a generation. Anatolia stopped being the mixed place it
had been for a thousand years in two years, and the play had not said so.

**The everyday.** Five dead Company entries - tea, the hamam, the carpet, the
evil eye, and the old woman who holds all four - were the whole material culture
of the play, asserted and unstaged. `plot_05` stages the quietest revolution in
this play and the one that took best: the state planted a crop in 1924 and within
a generation the country drank something else, and nobody counted it a loss. It
is set deliberately beside the alphabet revolution, because both came from above
and only one took something away.

`plot_99` records the decade still resolving and gives the Stakes a changed shape:
the question is no longer whether Europe's door will open but who opens and closes
this country's own.

The numbering is closed rather than gapped, as elsewhere in this house: inserting
the exchange and the tea moved the secular-or-devout plot from 04 to 06.
