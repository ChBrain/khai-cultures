# Plot: the ships at Waimea, 1778

## Taxonomy

[play_hawaii](play_hawaii.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

In January 1778 two British ships anchor off Waimea on Kauaʻi. The islands have been out of contact with any other people for centuries. Cook returns the following year, is received at Kealakekua Bay, and is killed on the shore there in February 1779.

## Action

What arrives is not one thing but three, and the islands have no way to accept the first without the others.

Iron and firearms arrive, and they are wanted. A chief who can trade for muskets and powder can do what no chief has managed since the islands were settled, and within seventeen years Kamehameha does it with two European gunners in his service. The unification in the next plot is bought here.

Disease arrives, and it is not wanted and cannot be refused. The islands had no acquired immunity to anything the ships carried. The scale of the collapse over the following century is disputed - the pre-contact population is one of the hardest numbers in Pacific history, and the estimates differ by a factor of several - but the direction is not disputed by anyone, and it was catastrophic.

> The same ships brought the means to unite the islands and the reason there were fewer people in them every year.

And a market arrives. Sandalwood first, cut for the China trade until the accessible stands were gone; then the whaling fleets; then the idea that these islands are a place other nations have interests in. Everything after this plot happens in front of an audience with ships.

The killing at Kealakekua is the part everyone outside remembers and the least consequential of the four. A single death on a beach changed nothing; the iron, the pathogens and the market changed everything.

## Tension

An isolation that ended the way isolations end, not by decision. The chiefs who traded for iron made a rational choice with what was in front of them, and the same traffic carried what emptied the valleys. The strain this leaves on the line is that no later plot can be read as a fair contest: by 1848 and certainly by 1893, the people arguing for the kingdom are fewer every decade, and the people arguing against it arrived on the ships.
