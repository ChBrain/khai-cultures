# Plot: Pāʻao and the walled heiau, c. 1200

## Taxonomy

[play_hawaii](play_hawaii.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

Tradition says a priest named Pāʻao sails in from Kahiki, finds the chiefly line here too weak, sends back for a high chief to replace it, and brings with him a harder religion: the walled temple that only the initiated may enter, the strict order of Kū, and human sacrifice.

## Action

The severity is the point. What tradition credits to Pāʻao is not new gods but a new distance: the pūloʻuloʻu staves that mark where a commoner must stop, the prostrating kapu that puts a person face-down on the ground before a chief, the luakini heiau walled so that worship happens out of sight. Before this, the accounts say, the heiau was open ground.

The line it starts runs the length of everything after. His feathered war god Kūkāʻilimoku is inherited down the Kohala chiefs until it reaches Kamehameha, who carries it through the conquest of the islands six centuries later. The kapu system he is said to have hardened is the one that governs who eats with whom, who may fish what and when, and what a commoner owes a chief, until it is broken from inside in a single meal.

> A society that can be told where to stop can also be told, later, that the line has moved.

Archaeology does not find him. Modern work discredits the arrival as a historical event, and reads the changes as local development over generations rather than one man's cargo. Many Hawaiians hold him real, and hold him responsible - he is one of the few figures in the tradition who is named in order to be blamed. Both of those are facts about this culture, and only one of them is a fact about the twelfth century.

## Tension

A tradition that keeps the name of the man it says made the religion severe, and keeps it without deciding whether he existed. The strength is a memory long enough and exact enough to hand down a critique of its own institutions across centuries. The strain is that the same account cannot be checked: the chant is the only witness, archaeology says otherwise, and choosing between them means choosing which way of knowing this culture is going to trust about itself.
