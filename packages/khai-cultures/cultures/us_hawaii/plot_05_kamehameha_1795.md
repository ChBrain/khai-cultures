# Plot: Kamehameha takes the islands, 1795

## Taxonomy

[play_hawaii](play_hawaii.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

Kamehameha lands his fleet on Oʻahu and drives the defenders up Nuʻuanu valley until they are forced off the cliff at the head of it. Within a year he holds every island but Kauaʻi, and in 1810 Kaumualiʻi submits without a battle. For the first time the archipelago answers to one chief.

## Action

He does it with the foreign weapons and the inherited god at once. Cannon and muskets from the trading ships, two European gunners in his service, and Kūkāʻilimoku, the feathered war god carried down from the Kohala line since Pāʻao, borne at the front. The unification is not a break with the old order; it is the old order winning with new equipment.

What it produces is the thing that can later be overthrown. Before 1795 there is no Hawaiian state to take - there are islands under separate chiefs, and a foreign power would have to conquer each. After 1795 there is a kingdom with one sovereign, one court and, soon, treaties and consuls and a recognised place among nations.

> A single throne is what makes the islands negotiable, and what makes them seizable in one morning.

Kauaʻi ends it by agreement rather than conquest, which is why the last island in is the one that keeps arguing longest about what it agreed to.

## Tension

The achievement of ending centuries of inter-island war by putting one hand on all of it, using exactly the foreign trade that will later be the lever against it. And the cost built into the achievement: everything is now centralised, so everything can be lost at the centre. In 1893 the taking of one palace in one city will be enough, which it never could have been before this plot.
