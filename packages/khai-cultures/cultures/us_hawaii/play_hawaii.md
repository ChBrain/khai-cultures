# Play: Hawaii

## Estate

[khai-cultures](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-cultures): the house that holds this production and answers for it.

## Name

Hawaii, the Aloha State in the Pacific Ocean region of the United States. ISO US-HI, language en.

## Arc

The story starts before the canoes, in a genealogy where the islands are the eldest sibling and the people the youngest. Navigators read star and swell to find the place; a priest is said to have brought a harder religion to it; a chief cut it into watersheds and gave a commoner the right to walk away from an unjust lord. Two British ships found the place for the rest of the world in 1778, bringing iron, firearms and the epidemics that emptied the valleys; Kamehameha made one kingdom of it in 1795, the court abolished its own state religion in 1819, and the Māhele turned kin into property in 1848 - which is how American sugar interests came to own enough of it to take the government in 1893. A naval base sat here until Japan's bombers found it in 1941. Through all of it ʻohana held the islands together and mālama ʻāina kept faith with the land, and the one count that has risen rather than fallen is the number of people who speak Hawaiian.

## Triggers

**[Plot 0: Hāloa, the elder sibling](plot_00_haloa.md)**

Before any canoe: Papa and Wākea bear the islands, a stillborn child becomes the first kalo, and the people are the youngest relative in the house. Not a founding - a place in a genealogy.

**[Plot 1: Polynesian Voyaging c. 1000 AD](plot_01_voyaging.md)**

Polynesian navigators travel thousands of miles across the open Pacific, using stars and ocean currents to settle the Hawaiian Islands.

**[Plot 2: Pāʻao and the walled heiau, c. 1200](plot_02_paao.md)**

Tradition says a priest from Kahiki brings the walled temple, the prostrating kapu and human sacrifice. Archaeology does not find him; the tradition keeps his name in order to blame it.

**[Plot 3: Māʻilikūkahi divides Oʻahu, c. 1480](plot_03_mailikukahi.md)**

A chief cuts the island into ahupuaʻa running ridge to reef, writes the first code of laws, forbids human sacrifice, and lets a commoner leave an unjust lord.

**[Plot 4: the ships at Waimea, 1778](plot_04_cook_1778.md)**

Two British ships anchor off Kauaʻi after centuries without outside contact. Iron and firearms arrive wanted; disease arrives and cannot be refused; and the islands become a place other nations have interests in.

**[Plot 5: Kamehameha takes the islands, 1795](plot_05_kamehameha_1795.md)**

Cannon, muskets and an inherited war god end the inter-island wars and make the archipelago one kingdom - and so one thing that can be seized at the centre.

**[Plot 6: the ʻAi Noa, 1819](plot_06_ai_noa_1819.md)**

Liholiho eats with the women of the court, the kapu breaks, and the temples are ordered down - months before the first missionaries land.

**[Plot 7: the Māhele, 1848](plot_07_mahele_1848.md)**

The kingdom divides the land into private title to make itself harder to annex. The makaʻāinana are given two years to file and end up with under one percent.

**[Plot 8: The Overthrow of the Hawaiian Kingdom 1893](plot_08_overthrow.md)**

American sugar planters, backed by US marines, overthrow Queen Liliʻuokalani, establishing a provisional government that would lead to annexation.

**[Plot 9: The Pearl Harbor Attack 1941](plot_09_pearl_harbor.md)**

Japanese forces launch a surprise aerial bombardment on the naval base at Pearl Harbor, drawing the United States into World War II.

**[Plot 99: ka ʻōlelo Hawaiʻi, the count of speakers](plot_99_olelo_hawaii.md)**

About fifteen hundred speakers when the first language nest opened in the mid-1980s; around thirty thousand now. The one number in this line that has moved the other way.

## Stakes

Whether an island carrying heavy tourism and development can still mālama the ʻāina enough to bring its native forests and endemic birds back before it's too late.
