# Austria: References

The provenance behind this culture: the Hofstede source data that tunes the pitch
(kept here, never in the prose) and the public-domain historical sources behind
the plots, personas, places, and pieces.

## Hofstede source data

The pitch [pitch_at.md](pitch_at.md) is written from Austria's profile on Geert
Hofstede's model of national culture and names no dimension and carries no
number. Austria's profile differs sharply from Germany's - far lower power
distance, higher achievement, a touch more indulgence - which is part of why the
two German-speaking cultures feel so distinct. The scores and their source are
recorded here so the data is on the record without entering the prose.

The Anschluss plot is named against the Moscow Declaration of 1943, which called
Austria both the first free country to fall victim to Hitlerite aggression and
responsible for its participation in the war on Germany's side; Austria long
remembered only the first half. It is staged on the same standard of honesty as
this house's own Machtübernahme und Zivilisationsbruch 1933 bis 1945, Germany's
plot for the same years: the crime is named as the country's own, not only as
something endured, and no individual beyond the head of the regime is named.

The two defining personas, [Grete](persona_grete.md) and [Lukas](persona_lukas.md),
are archetypes, not historical individuals: composites that carry the tension
between the older Vienna of Kaffeehaus, titles, and imperial nostalgia and the
younger, global, ironic generation. [Lukas](persona_lukas.md) was born after 1986
as well as after 1955, so [plot_10_waldheim_1986.md](plot_10_waldheim_1986.md)
does not cast him as a witness to the affair; it casts him as the generation that
inherits its outcome without having had to fight for it, which is what his own
Shadow chapter already names.

## The defining question

Per the house order on the defining question (`management/orders/order_the_defining_question.md`),
Austria was named as its own example: a play jumping from Vienna around 1900 to
the Staatsvertrag 1955 and never crossing 1938 to 1945. This revision is that
dialogue's answer: it stages the empire the Arc opens on (1683, the Wiener
Klassik), the collapse the Arc is actually built on (1918, staged here for the
first time), the exclusion from Germany the Arc's "nicht deutsch" line asserts
(1866), and the catastrophe itself (1938), told as the country's own doing and
not only something suffered. The Waldheim plot (1986) stages the first public
rupture of the "first victim" doctrine that
[plan_die_oesterreichische_identitaet.md](plan_die_oesterreichische_identitaet.md)
already named as waived rather than done; that target stays waived, not
completed, because the country's reckoning with 1938 to 1945 remained partial
even after Waldheim and after Vranitzky's 1991 speech, and no plot here invents a
reckoning more finished than the one the country actually had.
