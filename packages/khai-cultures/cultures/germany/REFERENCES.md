# Germany: References

pitch (kept here, never in the prose) and the public-domain historical sources
behind the plots, personas, places, and pieces.

no number. The scores and their source are recorded here so the data is on the
record without entering the prose.

The 1933 to 1945 plot is staged from the public historical record and names no
individual perpetrator beyond the head of the regime; the term Zivilisationsbruch
is Dan Diner's and is standard in German historiography. The Stolpersteine and
the Erinnern process are linked from that plot's Tension as what the country did
later, not as anything present in the scene.

The Rettungsgasse named in [place_die_autobahn.md](place_die_autobahn.md) is a
German rule (StVO) but not a German invention: Austria, Switzerland and Czechia
require the same corridor. The file stages the moral weight the practice carries
here, and claims no origin.

The two defining personas, [Elke](persona_elke.md) and [Jonas](persona_jonas.md),
are archetypes, not historical individuals: composites that carry the
generational tension between the East-shaped older generation and the
reunification-native young.
