## Where the record is thin, and what these plots do about it

This culture has a hard documentary floor and a much older civic memory, and the
two are not merged anywhere in these files.

- **The foundation of 301 and the words attributed to Marinus are traditional.**
  The _Vita Sanctorum Marini et Leonis_ survives in a manuscript tradition dated
  to the late ninth or early tenth century, and UNESCO's own nomination material
  treats the saint's words as the long-standing basis of the idea of perpetual
  freedom rather than as a fourth-century record. That is cultural reception, and
  it is staged as such: a legend that defines a people is part of what that
  people is, and `plot_01` is not corrected.
- **The Placito Feretrano is a property judgment.** It does not declare
  independence from Rimini or Montefeltro, and the hearing took place before
  Montefeltro authorities, inside the regional order rather than outside it. What
  is culturally true is that later readers made an ancestral proof out of a suit
  over fields. `plot_02` stages the suit and says what was made of it afterwards.
- **The 1243 act names consuls, not Captains Regent**, and records no reason for
  the pairing or for the six-month term. The anti-tyranny explanation is a later
  and meaningful reading of a durable practice, not a founding rationale, and
  `plot_03` keeps that distinction rather than putting a constitutional theory in
  a thirteenth-century mouth.
- **Faetano's voluntary adherence is attested and its mechanism is not.** No
  council minute, no assembly, no delegate, no text of accession survives.
  `plot_04` leaves the decision offstage, because a document can call an
  accession voluntary while it stays ambiguous for the people living through it.
- **The Cava dei Balestrieri is not medieval.** It was quarried between 1884 and
  1894 for the Palazzo Pubblico and became a crossbow arena in 1971, so it is not
  evidence of a late-antique stonecutting trade and is not used as any.
