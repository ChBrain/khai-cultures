# Plot: The Convoy, 1939 to 1945

## Taxonomy

[play_nato](play_nato.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

A greaser from Cardiff, a Norwegian second mate and a Greek deckhand stand on the same tanker in the middle of an ocean, and none of them is a soldier. They have no rank anyone would salute, and in the first years of the war a man's wages stop on the day his ship goes down, which is a thing the men on board know about each other and do not discuss.

## Action

The alliance is named after an ocean, and this is the ocean. For five years and eight months, longer than any other campaign of the war and without a single pause in it, convoys cross the North Atlantic because the crossing is the war: the island at the eastern end cannot eat, fuel or fight without them.

The crews are the point. They are merchant seamen and not navies, they are of every nationality on both shores, and the fleet they sail is stitched together out of whoever has ships. Norway's merchant fleet, some thousand vessels and among the largest afloat, does not go home when the country falls in 1940 - it goes over to the Allied side, is run from London and New York, and carries a share of the oil that keeps the whole thing moving. Greek, Dutch, Danish, Polish and Belgian ships do their version of the same. Canada builds escorts and mans them faster than it can train the crews. The Americans are in it before they are in the war.

Some thirty thousand British merchant seamen alone do not come back, a proportion worse than most of the fighting services, and the men doing it are civilians in civilian ships being paid a wage.

**What is learned here is not that the West should have an alliance.** It is a much narrower and more durable thing: that the ocean is not a moat, that keeping a lane open is a job done by people who are not soldiers, and that ships from eight countries can only sail in one formation if somebody has agreed in advance what the signals mean. [The same words](position_the_same_words.md) start here, on a signal lamp, out of necessity, four years before anyone drafts a treaty.

## Tension

Everything the alliance later says about itself is stated in the language of governments: pledges, articles, consensus among sovereign equals. What actually happened first was that the same lane had to be kept open by whoever had a hull, and the people who kept it open were not party to any of it. The question the whole rest of this play inherits is whether an arrangement so completely described in treaty language can still remember that its origin was logistical and civilian - or whether, having forgotten, it will find the fittings do not fit on the morning it needs them to.
