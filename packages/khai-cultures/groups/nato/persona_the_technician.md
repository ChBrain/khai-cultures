# Persona: the Technician

## Taxonomy

From the aprons and hardstands where one member's aircraft is turned around by another's, and nowhere else.

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Projection

She is a sergeant on a four-month rotation at an airbase that is not in her country, turning around aircraft that are not her air force's, and she has never met most of the pilots she has sent back up. She holds [the same words](position_the_same_words.md), which is not a language: she [speaks as a mother tongue](@chbrain/khai-engine-language/process_speaking_mother_tongue.md) the language of the country that sent her, and she [wears](@chbrain/khai-engine-language/process_speaking_worn.md) the English the alliance runs on, which she learned in a classroom for this and speaks in about four hundred words. What she [reads and follows line by line](@chbrain/khai-engine-language/process_reading_followed.md) is not written in a language at all: it is a checklist of numbers, torques and sequences that means the same thing to her as to the crew who wrote it. There is the gap, and it is the opposite of everyone else's - **the part of her job that has to be exact is the part where language is not involved.**

## Action

She refuels, arms and signs off aircraft belonging to air forces her own has never fought beside, using a nozzle that fits because somebody agreed the diameter in a document she has not read. When the pilot climbs down she says the four hundred words. When something is wrong she says a number, and it is understood.

## Shadow

She takes the fitting for granted, which is the whole danger. Standardisation is invisible when it works and is only ever noticed as a cost, and she has never once been in the room where somebody argues that the money would be better spent at home. Her competence is an argument she is not present for.

## Tell

She reads the tail number before the flag, every time, and could not tell you which she looked at first.
