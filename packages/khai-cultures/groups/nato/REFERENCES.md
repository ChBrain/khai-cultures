# NATO: References

The provenance behind this group. A group carries no Hofstede data of its own (it
has no single national profile); it is a referencing entry over its member
cultures.

## The grouping

NATO, the North Atlantic Treaty Organization, is the transatlantic collective-
defence alliance founded by the North Atlantic Treaty (Washington, 4 April 1949).
The membership recorded in the registry is **build-derived** from the member plays
this group casts, not authored by hand. It binds thirty-two democracies of North
America and Europe under Article 5, by which an armed attack on one is treated as
an attack on all, and decides by consensus among sovereign equals. It is not a
single language or a single state.

Two details are credited precisely because they are easy to state loosely.

The spelling alphabet is universally called the NATO alphabet and was not made by
NATO: it was developed for **civil aviation** and adopted by the alliance in the
1950s, which is why it is the one used worldwide by pilots, shipping, police and
anyone spelling a name over a telephone. The plot says so.

The line in [plot_00_the_convoy.md](plot_00_the_convoy.md) about a merchant
seaman's wages stopping the day his ship went down refers to **the first years of
the war** in the British merchant service; the practice was ended by agreement
during the war, and the plot is written to that limit rather than as a statement
about the whole campaign. The figure of some thirty thousand British merchant
seamen lost is the commonly published one and is cited, not claimed as exact.

[The Technician](persona_the_technician.md) is an archetype and not a named
person, and [the same words](position_the_same_words.md) is this group's own
position rather than any member's: both belong to the space between the members
and are staged only in this group's plots.

## A note on the arc

`management/design/groups-and-references.md` §2.1.1 warns that a group is the
easy place to get `order_the_passport.md` wrong, "because the obvious material
for an alliance is the alliance" - and no group in this house is a harder case
than this one, whose whole obvious material is a treaty article, a summit table
and an accession list. Every Cue now has a non-state subject: three merchant
seamen of three nationalities on the same tanker, aircrews landing coal into a
blockaded city every few minutes, a farmer in Lower Saxony whose field has a
number, a Bavarian conscript whose barracks becomes a business park, a Polish
officer sent on a language course at forty-one, and somebody in a call centre
spelling a surname.

Article 5 and the enlargements are all still here and still named. What changed
is who the scene opens on.
