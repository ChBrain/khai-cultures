# Plot: Alpha Bravo Charlie, and the Nozzle

## Taxonomy

[play_nato](play_nato.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

Somebody in a call centre in Manila spells a surname to a customer in Ohio - _Alpha, Bravo, Charlie_ - and neither of them has any idea they are using a military alphabet, or that a defence alliance adopted it in the 1950s and is the reason it is the one everybody uses.

## Action

The alphabet is the visible edge of the alliance's actual output, which is not deterrence and not summits. It is several thousand agreements at the altitude of a hose fitting: one fuel for every aircraft and most vehicles, a cartridge two members can hand each other, a pallet that fits every hold, a casualty tag anyone can read, a sequence for passing control of an aircraft to a controller in a country you have never flown over. Almost nobody in the thirty-two has read one. All of them depend on all of them.

[The Technician](persona_the_technician.md) is where this stops being paperwork. On a four-month rotation at a base that is not in her country, she turns around aircraft belonging to air forces her own has never fought beside. She speaks about four hundred words of the English the alliance runs on. The checklist she works to is numbers and torques, and it means precisely the same thing to her as to the crew who wrote it, so **the exact part of her job is the part where language is not involved.** She reads the tail number before the flag and could not say which she looked at first.

That is the shape of the present. Since 2004 the Baltic air-policing rotation has been flown by a different member every few months, and since 2017 multinational battlegroups have stood in Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania and Poland - formations in which a company from one country is resupplied by another and commanded by a third. In 2023 and 2024 [Finland](@chbrain/khai-cultures-finland/play_finland.md) and [Sweden](@chbrain/khai-cultures-sweden/play_sweden.md) joined, ending two centuries of staying out between them, and the practical work of that accession is not the signature. It is years of making the fittings fit.

And the argument that never resolves runs underneath it all: the standard is usually somebody else's, everybody knows whose, and a member with a better nozzle fits the agreed one anyway. Standardisation is invisible when it works and shows up in a budget only as a cost.

## Stage

- [the same words](position_the_same_words.md): the alliance's most-used export, spoken daily by people who have never heard of it, and its least-defended line item.
- [the Technician](persona_the_technician.md): the person the whole arrangement resolves to, who takes the fitting for granted and is never in the room where it is argued about.
- [Finland](@chbrain/khai-cultures-finland/play_finland.md) and [Sweden](@chbrain/khai-cultures-sweden/play_sweden.md): the newest members, for whom joining was a signature and then years of adjusting couplings.

## Tension

The thing the alliance is famous for is a promise, and a promise cannot be tested without being spent. The thing it actually does every day is small, unglamorous and continuously verifiable: it makes thirty-two sets of equipment behave as one. **The first is what gets debated and the second is what would decide anything**, and the second is also the one that quietly loses every budget round, because a fitting that has never had to fit looks exactly like an expense. Whether an alliance can keep paying for its plumbing through a long peace is the open question, and nobody finds out the answer until the morning the hose does not fit.
