# Plot: One Written Language, 1200 to 1650

## Taxonomy

[play_these_islands](play_these_islands.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

A poet trained in a bardic school in Ireland takes service with a lord in the Hebrides, and finds he does not have to learn anything: the metres are the same metres, the standard is the same standard, and the family he founds there will be supplying poets to Scottish chiefs for four hundred years.

## Action

The learned classes of Ireland and Gaelic Scotland hold one literary language between them, taught in schools that take years to get through and maintained by hereditary families who move across the water as their patrons require. It is nobody's spoken dialect and that is the point of it: a written standard deliberately held above the local speech on both sides, so that a poem composed in Munster is read in Argyll without translation and without anyone noticing there was a sea in the way. A single manuscript compiled in Perthshire in the sixteenth century holds Irish and Scottish verse side by side because there was no side to be by. Then the states take an interest. The Gaelic lordships collapse in Ireland after 1607, and in Scotland the chiefs are required from 1609 to send their heirs to be schooled in the Lowlands, in English, away from the learning; the schools that maintained the standard go, and inside a couple of generations the two Gaelics are two languages with two spellings and no shared page.

## Stage

- [Ireland](@chbrain/khai-cultures-ireland/play_ireland.md): the source of the standard, the schools that taught it, and the families that carried it over.
- [Scotland](../../cultures/gb_scotland/play_scotland.md): the other half of the shared page, and the half where a statute was written specifically to end it.
- [Scottish Gaelic](@chbrain/khai-cultures-tongues/gd/position_language_gd.md): what was left when the common standard went, close enough to its Irish sibling that the strait is still narrower than the border.
- [the Highlands](../../cultures/gb_scotland/place_the_highlands.md): the ground the poets crossed to, and the ground the schooling was aimed at.

## Tension

The archipelago's first shared culture ran east and west across the water and had nothing to do with either capital, and both capitals ended it deliberately, within two years of each other and for the same reason: a learned class that answers across a sea answers to nobody in particular. What is left of it is a resemblance that people notice and cannot account for. Everything else in this play is movement under pressure; this is the one stretch where these islands shared something at the top rather than at the bottom, and it is the only one nobody has proposed reviving.
