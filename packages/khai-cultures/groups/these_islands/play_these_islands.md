# Play: these islands

## Estate

[khai-cultures](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-cultures): the house that holds this production and answers for it.

## Name

These islands: Ireland and the four nations of the United Kingdom, two states and five cultures on one archipelago. The phrase is the one both governments reach for when they need a word for the whole, because the words that exist are each claimed by one side.

## Arc

Two states that cannot agree on a name for the place they share, and have never stopped moving freely across it. The oldest thing here is a shared learning: for four centuries the learned classes of Ireland and Gaelic Scotland wrote one literary language and their poets crossed the water to serve in each other's halls, until states on both sides of it decided that was a problem. What replaced the shared high culture was a shared low one, made by movement in the other direction and under duress: hunger emptying the west of Ireland into Liverpool and Glasgow, then a century of Irish labour building British roads and Irish women staffing British wards, arriving to notices in windows and staying anyway. And running under all of it, unlegislated for most of its life and never once repealed, an arrangement by which people from either state may live, work and vote in the other without so much as a passport. It survived a war of independence, a partition, a neutrality, a departure from the Commonwealth and a departure from the European Union. It is the most durable thing these islands have built together and almost nobody can name the year it started.

## Stakes

Whether five cultures on one archipelago, who have moved among each other for so long that a great many of them are each other's family, can go on holding an arrangement that no treaty made and no vote protects, now that the two states around it answer to different unions, and whether a place that has never agreed what to call itself can be said to be one thing at all.
