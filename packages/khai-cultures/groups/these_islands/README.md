# These Islands: a khai group

The archipelago staged as a khai **group** — a play in the shared `groups/`
collection that **references its member cultures** rather than being a country of
its own. Ireland and the four nations of the United Kingdom: two states, five
cultures, one archipelago.

## Estate

[khai-cultures](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-cultures): the Cultures house
that holds this production and answers for it.

## The name, which is the hard part

There is no neutral noun for this place, and the group is named for that rather
than around it.

- **_The British Isles_** is the ordinary term in Britain and is avoided by the
  Irish state and by most people in Ireland. A group whose name is rejected by one
  of its five members would be a file arguing with its own Company.
- **_The Atlantic Archipelago_** is a historians' coinage. Nobody says it.
- **_The British-Irish Council_** names an institution, and naming the group after
  the institution is exactly the failure `order_the_passport.md` warns about.
- **_These islands_** is the phrase both governments actually reach for when they
  need a word for the whole, and it is the register the 1998 Agreement used for
  the relationships across it. It is neutral because it was built to be.

So the group takes the workaround as its title, and the fact that a workaround was
needed is its Tension, its pitch's Undertow, and half of what
[the neighbour](position_the_neighbour.md) is about. This is the only group in the
house whose members do not share a word for the group.

## A group, not a culture

It has **no `geo.json`** and it is **not counted** in the registry version: the
minor stays the culture count.

- **Members:** [Ireland](../../../khai-cultures-ireland/play_ireland.md),
  [England](../../cultures/gb_england/play_england.md),
  [Scotland](../../cultures/gb_scotland/play_scotland.md),
  [Wales](../../cultures/gb_wales/play_wales.md),
  [Northern Ireland](../../cultures/gb_northern_ireland/play_northern_ireland.md).
- **Kind:** `group`.
- **Spine:** the movement of people across the water, in both directions, over
  eight centuries, and the arrangement that still lets it happen.

## Why movement and not the Council

`management/design/groups-and-references.md` §2.1.1 says a group's arc is what its
members did **together** and warns that the obvious material for an alliance is
the alliance. The obvious material here is the British-Irish Council, the Good
Friday Agreement's Strand Three, and none of it is staged: the Agreement belongs
to [Northern Ireland](../../cultures/gb_northern_ireland/play_northern_ireland.md),
which stages it already.

What the five actually did together is move among each other, continuously, and
then decline to stop. That runs from a shared literary language held across the
sea for four centuries, through two great migrations under duress, to a
free-movement arrangement that no treaty made and that has outlasted every treaty
that did.

This group deliberately does **not** duplicate
[the Four Nations](../the_four_nations/play_the_four_nations.md), which stages what
the four built with each other inside one state. The two share four members and no
material.

## Provenance

"These islands" is in ordinary official use on both sides as the neutral term. The
staging and the architecture are original work.
