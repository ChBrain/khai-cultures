# Plot: The Arrangement Nobody Signed, from 1923

## Taxonomy

[play_these_islands](play_these_islands.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

A man steps off the boat at Holyhead a year after the two states finished shooting at each other, walks past where a passport control would be if there were one, and gets on a train, and nobody has asked him anything and nobody will.

## Action

Officials on both sides work out, quietly and without a treaty, that the alternative to letting people move is checking them, and that checking them means putting a control on a land border neither government wants to admit is a border. So they agree to police the outer edge together and to leave the inside alone, and they write almost none of it down. The arrangement then survives everything thrown at it. It survives Ireland staying out of the war the other was fighting. It survives Ireland leaving the Commonwealth in 1949, after which the other state's law goes to the trouble of declaring that Irish citizens are not foreigners in it. It survives both joining the same European union together in 1973 and it survives one of them leaving in 2020. Along the way it acquires something almost no two sovereign states grant each other: citizens of each may vote in the other's elections, and do.

- [Northern Ireland](../../cultures/gb_northern_ireland/play_northern_ireland.md): the land border the whole thing exists to avoid manning.
- [the Cousin](persona_the_cousin.md): the person for whom none of this is policy, only the reason she does not need to think about it.

## Tension

The most consequential agreement in these islands was never signed, never ratified and never put to anybody, and it has outlasted every agreement that was. That is its strength and it is exactly its weakness: a thing no treaty made can be ended by two administrations deciding otherwise on a Tuesday, and the people who rely on it most are the ones least likely to hear about it in advance. It has been reaffirmed in writing exactly once, in 2019, by two governments who had just discovered they could not remember what it rested on.
