# These Islands: References

The provenance behind this group. A group carries no Hofstede data of its own (it
has no single national profile); it is a referencing entry over its member
cultures, and it carries a pitch because a pitch is a tone and not a score.

## The grouping

Two states and five cultures on one archipelago. The membership recorded in the
registry is **build-derived** from the member plays this group casts, not authored
by hand.

The state above four of them, [united_kingdom](../../../khai-cultures-united-kingdom/play_united_kingdom.md),
is a culture in its own right and is **not** a member here: including both it and
its four parts would count the same ground twice. The four appear because the
relationships this group stages are with them severally, not with the state.

## On the name

There is no neutral noun for this archipelago. _The British Isles_ is standard in
Britain and avoided by the Irish state; _the Atlantic Archipelago_ is an academic
coinage nobody uses; naming the group after the British-Irish Council would name
an institution rather than a culture. _These islands_ is the phrase both
governments reach for when they need one, and is the register of the 1998
Agreement's treatment of "the totality of relationships within these islands". The
group takes the workaround as its title and stages the need for a workaround.

## A note on the arc

The obvious material for a group of these five is the British-Irish Council and
the 1998 Agreement, which is precisely the failure `order_the_passport.md`
describes, and the Agreement is already staged where it belongs, in
[gb_northern_ireland](../../cultures/gb_northern_ireland/plot_04_good_friday_1998.md).
Every Cue here has a non-state subject: a poet taking service across the water, a
family selling up for a deck passage, a woman answering a newspaper advertisement,
a man walking off a boat at Holyhead, and a queue for passports that is not at a
border.
