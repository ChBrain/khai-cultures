# Plot: Liverpool and the Clyde, 1845 to 1852

## Taxonomy

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

A family from Mayo with no food and no rent sells what it has for a deck passage, and the boat it can afford does not go to America. It goes across, to the nearest port on the far shore, for a few shillings, in a night.

## Action

The nearest shore takes the overflow of a catastrophe. In the worst year something near three hundred thousand people land at Liverpool, into a city of about a quarter of a million, and by the census a few years later something like a quarter of that city was born in Ireland. Glasgow and the Clyde towns take their own share on top of a Highland clearance already running. The receiving cities are not equipped and do not want to be: the arrivals go into the cellars and the courts, the fever follows them, and the newspapers of the receiving country invent a settled vocabulary about the character of the people arriving that will still be in use a hundred years later. What is created in those seven years is permanent. It is not a diaspora across an ocean but a population on the other island, close enough to go home for a funeral, and from this point on a very large number of British families are also Irish families and know it.

## Tension

The Atlantic crossing is the one that got the songs and the memorials, and the crossing that actually made these islands what they are is the twelve-hour one that cost a few shillings. It is remembered thinly on both shores and for opposite reasons: in Ireland because leaving for Britain reads as failing to get properly away, and in Britain because a migration that produced no distinct language and no distinct religion after a generation stops being visible, and a thing that stops being visible stops being credited.
