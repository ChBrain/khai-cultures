# Plot: The Household Examination, from 1686

## Taxonomy

[play_nordics](play_nordics.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

The parish priest comes to the farm once a year, sits everybody down, and hears each of them read aloud and say the catechism back, and what he thinks of their reading goes into the parish book beside their names, where the whole district can see it.

## Action

A church law in Sweden in 1686 makes this the routine, and Denmark-Norway arrives at the same place by a different road, making confirmation compulsory in 1736 and requiring reading of anyone confirmed. Confirmation is not a private matter of faith: without it you cannot marry, and in practice you cannot take up an adult place in the parish at all. So the incentive runs into every household, including the ones with no interest in the catechism, and it is enforced by neighbours who will hear how you did. Iceland, with no schools worth the name and farms a day apart, does the same thing at the fireside through the winter, one voice reading aloud while the rest work the wool. Inside a hundred years the poorest and coldest corner of Europe is the one where nearly everybody can read.

And nearly nobody can write. Writing is a separate skill, taught to those who will need it, and it lags reading by the better part of a century. What the north builds first is not a literate public in the modern sense but an enormous audience: millions who can take in what is put in front of them and cannot answer it on paper.

## Stage

- [Sweden](@chbrain/khai-cultures-sweden/play_sweden.md): the church law that made the annual examination routine and wrote the results down.
- [Denmark](@chbrain/khai-cultures-denmark/play_denmark.md): the same end by the other road, through a confirmation nobody could marry without.
- [Finland](../../cultures/finland/play_finland.md): inside the Swedish realm and under the same law, examined in its own language by clergy who had to learn it.
- [Iceland](../../cultures/iceland/play_iceland.md): no schools, no towns, and the highest literacy in Europe, taught at the fireside over the winter.

## Tension

The most admired thing about these societies was installed as an instrument of control, by a church that wanted its doctrine in every head and got there by making the sacraments conditional on passing a test. It worked, and then it did what such things do: a population that can all read is a population that can read anything, and within a century the same farms were taking in newspapers, pamphlets, revival tracts and the temperance movement, and organising. The reading was ordered from above and the reading public that came out of it was not.
