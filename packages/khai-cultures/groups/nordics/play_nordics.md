# Play: the Nordics

## Estate

[khai-cultures](https://github.com/ChBrain/khai-cultures): the house that holds this production and answers for it.

## Name

The Nordics, the five countries of the European north: Denmark (DK), Sweden (SE), Norway (NO), Finland (FI), and Iceland (IS), four of them tongues apart and yet bound into one recognisable family.

## Arc

Five countries that cannot all understand each other and spent three centuries
arranging to. What binds them is not a crown and not a treaty but a reading
public, and it was built deliberately, from the pulpit, before any of them had
schools. After the Reformation the state churches made reading a condition of
adult life: you could not be confirmed unless you could read, and you could not
marry unless you were confirmed, and the parish clergy came to the house once a
year to check. Reading was taught to everybody and writing was not, so the north
arrived at the nineteenth century almost universally able to read and largely
unable to write back, which is a very particular kind of population. On that
foundation three of the five discovered they could follow one another's languages
without learning them, built a movement on it that failed at the first war, and
kept the comprehension after the politics collapsed. Then they made the
comprehension into law and the movement of people into an arrangement, forty years
before the rest of Europe managed either. What the five have now is the deepest
mutual civil life on the continent and a shared tongue that is quietly becoming
English.

## Triggers

**[Plot 0: The Thing and the Danish Tongue, 930 to 1300](plot_00_the_thing_and_the_danish_tongue.md)**

Free men meet in the open to make law by assembly, from a plain in Iceland to the shires of Sweden, and all of them do it in one language they call the Danish tongue whoever is speaking it.

**[Plot 1: The Household Examination, from 1686](plot_01_the_household_examination.md)**

The church makes reading a condition of marrying, comes to the house once a year to test it, and writes the result in the register, and inside a century the poorest region of Europe is the one that can read.

**[Plot 2: The Movement That Failed and the Understanding That Did Not, 1840 to 1864](plot_02_the_movement_that_failed.md)**

Students toast a united north in three languages nobody has to translate, and when the war comes the north does not turn up, and the politics dies and the comprehension does not.

**[Plot 3: No Passport, from 1952](plot_03_no_passport.md)**

Five countries agree that their people may cross, live and work among each other without papers, five years before the rest of Europe starts negotiating the same thing.

**[Plot 99: The Meeting Switches to English, since 1990](plot_99_the_meeting_switches_to_english.md)**

The young of three countries that could always follow each other test worse at it than their parents and better at English, and the room, out of courtesy, changes language.

## Stakes

Whether a shared social model and a common north hold a real family together, or whether Denmark, Sweden, Norway, Finland, and Iceland remain five distinct countries that simply resemble one another - and discover, in that very resemblance, how different they are.
