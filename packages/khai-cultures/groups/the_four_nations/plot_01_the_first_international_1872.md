# Plot: The First International, 1872

## Taxonomy

[play_the_four_nations](play_the_four_nations.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

Four thousand people pay a shilling to stand at a cricket ground in Partick on the last Saturday in November to watch eleven men from Glasgow play eleven men from England, in a fixture nobody has ever staged before because no two nations have ever put teams on a field against each other.

## Action

It finishes nought each. The Scotland side is drawn entirely from one club, Queen's Park, and plays a passing game against an English side that dribbles, which is the first tactical argument in the history of international football and one Scotland eventually wins. What matters more than the result is the category the afternoon creates. Before this, a team represents a club, a school or a town. After it there is such a thing as a national side, and the first two ever to meet are two nations of the same state, playing each other because they are near enough to travel and different enough to want to.

## Stage

- [the Saltire](../../cultures/gb_scotland/piece_saltire.md): a nation's own mark on a fixture list, forty years before any of this becomes a constitutional question.

## Tension

The world's oldest international fixture is a domestic one, and every national side that has ever existed anywhere descends from an afternoon when two parts of one country decided they were two countries for ninety minutes. It set the precedent before anyone examined it: the four are separate in sport not because a treaty allows it but because they did it first, and by the time anybody might have objected the rest of the world had copied them.
