# Plot: The Board That Writes the Laws, 1886

## Taxonomy

[play_the_four_nations](play_the_four_nations.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

The four associations have been playing each other for a decade with four slightly different rulebooks, and every fixture between them starts with a negotiation about whose laws apply, which is fine until the disagreements start deciding matches.

## Action

They form a board between them to hold one set of laws, four bodies with an equal say and no state involved at any point. It is a housekeeping arrangement for a domestic problem, and it becomes the constitution of a world sport: the game is already going abroad, the abroad adopts the laws these four agreed, and when a world governing body is founded it does not write its own laws but joins the board. Today that board has eight votes, one for each of the four and four for the world body, and six are needed to change anything. So the world cannot alter a law of football without at least two of these four agreeing, and the four cannot alter one without the world. A hundred and forty years on, nobody has found a reason to dismantle it that outweighs the reason it works.

## Stage

- [England](../../cultures/gb_england/play_england.md): the association that wrote the first code and did not get to keep it to itself.
- [Scotland](../../cultures/gb_scotland/play_scotland.md): the second association, and the one whose separate existence made a board necessary at all.
- [Wales](../../cultures/gb_wales/play_wales.md): a nation with no parliament of its own holding one vote in eight over a global rulebook.
- [Northern Ireland](../../cultures/gb_northern_ireland/play_northern_ireland.md): the seat that stayed after the island it sat on was partitioned, and is held to this day.

## Tension

Four cultures with, at the time, no legislature between them wrote a body of law that the rest of the world still obeys, and they did it by agreeing with each other rather than by being governed. The arrangement is indefensible on any principle of proportion and unarguable on the evidence, which is why every attempt to make it fairer has stopped at the point of noticing it works. And it left the four with something the Union never gave them: a standing they hold as four, jointly, that no one of them could hold alone.
