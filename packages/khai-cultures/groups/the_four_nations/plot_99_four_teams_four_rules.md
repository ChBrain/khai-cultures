# Plot: Four Teams, Four Rules, since 1999

## Taxonomy

[play_the_four_nations](play_the_four_nations.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

Four sides line up for four different anthems before four different crowds, and one of the four has no anthem of its own and sings the state's, which nobody in the other three has ever had to do.

## Action

Devolution arrives in 1999 and hands three of the four something the fixture list had handed all four in 1884: a body that speaks for them by name. The sport does not change, because it had nothing to learn. The four keep four teams, four associations and four seats on the board, and when an Olympic team is proposed for the whole state the associations resist it, on the stated grounds that a single side once assembled is an argument for a single side always. Then a pandemic runs the country the way the sport always had. Health is devolved, so there are four sets of rules, four sets of numbers and four people at four lecterns saying different things on the same afternoon, and the phrase used for it is _the four nations approach_, which is a sporting expression that had been waiting a hundred and forty years for a government to need it.

## Stage

- [the four teams](position_the_four_teams.md): the arrangement itself, older than the devolution that copied it and harder to reform.
- [the St George's Cross](../../cultures/gb_england/piece_st_georges_cross.md): the flag of the nation that has a team, a flag and no anthem, and borrows the state's.
- [Y Ddraig Goch](../../cultures/gb_wales/piece_y_ddraig_goch.md): flown by a crowd whose team wears the badge of an English prince.
- [Gaelic games](../../cultures/gb_northern_ireland/process_gaa.md): the fourth nation's other set of teams, playing in a competition organised across a border the fixture list does not recognise.

## Tension

The four have now been given by statute a version of what they had already built for themselves, and the built one is the more durable: assemblies have fallen over twice in Belfast this century and no association has. What the decade records is a state whose parts are more reliably four on a Saturday than on any other day, and a habit of separate administration that arrived through sport and was borrowed by government rather than the other way round. Whether an arrangement that was made to settle a fixture can go on carrying what people actually feel about being four is the question, and nobody designed it to.
