# Plot: The Song Grounds, from 1869

## Taxonomy

[play_baltics](play_baltics.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

A farmhand walks two days to Tartu in June 1869 to stand in a field and sing. His grandfather was a serf. The land he works belongs to a German baron, the empire above the baron is Russian, the schooling above him is in German, and the language he is about to sing in has no country, no army, no literature to speak of and no standing at all - it is what the people who own the land call the peasants' dialect.

## Action

Eight hundred and fifty singers turn up. Fifteen thousand people come to listen, in a province where nothing has ever assembled fifteen thousand people for a purpose of their own. Four years later Riga does it, and half a century after that Kaunas.

Nobody is permitted to hold a political meeting. Everybody is permitted to hold a choir, because a choir is culture, and the barons and the governors who license these things are not wrong to think singing is harmless - they are only wrong about the arithmetic. **What a song celebration does is count people.** It does not petition or resolve; it assembles, and the assembly is the entire statement, and the statement gets read correctly by everyone standing in the field long before anyone in a chancellery notices there is one.

The three do it separately, in three languages, two of which are related to each other and neither of which is related to the third. They are not cooperating; they are each independently discovering the same instrument. It becomes [the festival](position_the_festival.md), and by the twentieth century the cycle is fixed, the grounds are purpose-built, and a country of a million and a bit can put thirty thousand people on a stand.

[The Singer](persona_the_singer.md) is the fifth generation of that farmhand's line to do it. She knows forty songs by heart without having decided to learn one.

## Stage

- [the festival](position_the_festival.md): invented here, licensed as culture because it was not politics, and it never afterwards had to be anything else.
- [the Singer](persona_the_singer.md): the person the whole institution is, five generations of turning up.

## Tension

Three peoples who share no language and were never a single polity acquire the same instrument within fifty years of one another, and it is the instrument that makes each of them a nation - not a war, not a dynasty, not a border. Everything the three do afterwards is done with it, including the part where they get free. The question the whole play carries is what an institution built to count _a people singing in its own tongue_ does with the people inside the same country who would be singing something else, and the answer is not settled and is not comfortable.
