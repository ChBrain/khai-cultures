# The Baltics: References

The provenance behind this group. A group carries no Hofstede data of its own (it
has no single national profile); it is a referencing entry over its member
cultures.

## The grouping

"The Baltic states" is the standard name for the three nations of the eastern
Baltic shore. The membership recorded in the registry is **build-derived** from
the member plays this group casts, not authored by hand.

| ISO | Country   | Member culture                                          |
| --- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| EE  | Estonia   | [estonia](../../cultures/estonia/play_estonia.md)       |
| LV  | Latvia    | [latvia](../../cultures/latvia/play_latvia.md)          |
| LT  | Lithuania | [lithuania](../../cultures/lithuania/play_lithuania.md) |

The grouping is bound by a parallel modern history (independence around 1918,
Soviet annexation in 1940, deportations, and the restoration of independence in
1991), the Singing Revolutions, the Baltic Way human chain of 23 August 1989, and
joint accession to the European Union and NATO in 2004, with standing cooperation
through the Baltic Assembly and Baltic Council. It is not a single language
(Estonian is Finnic, not Baltic) or a single state.

Figures are given in round terms in the prose because they are estimates and
contested at the edges: participation at the first celebrations, the numbers taken
in 1941 and 1949, the size of the 1988 crowds and of the 1989 chain, the share of
Russian speakers in each country, and the count of grey-passport holders. All are
the commonly published ranges, cited and not claimed as measurements.

[The Singer](persona_the_singer.md) is an archetype and not a named person, and
[the festival](position_the_festival.md) is this group's own position rather than
any member's - it is one institution under three names, `laulupidu`,
`dziesmu svētki` and `dainų šventė`, and belongs to the space between the members.

## A note on the arc

`management/design/groups-and-references.md` §2.1.1 warns that a group is the easy
place to get `order_the_passport.md` wrong. The obvious material here is
annexation, restoration and joint accession, and none of it opens a scene. Every
Cue has a non-state subject: a farmhand walking two days to sing, a family given
an hour to pack, people driving to the song grounds after work for no announced
reason, two million joining hands, and an engineer watching a frequency reading.

The occupation, the restoration and the accessions are all still here and still
named. What changed is who the scene opens on.
