# Plot: An Hour to Pack, 1941 and 1949

## Taxonomy

[play_baltics](play_baltics.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

Somebody knocks before dawn on 14 June 1941, and a family is told it has an hour. What you take is what you can carry, decided by people who have never had to make that decision and will spend the rest of their lives revising it: the wrong coat, the samovar instead of the bread, the photographs or not the photographs. At the station the men are put in different wagons from the women and children, and most of the families do not know this is a permanent separation until years later.

## Action

That week around forty thousand people are taken from the three, in the same operation, on the same days, by the same instruction. In March 1949 it happens again and larger - some ninety thousand in under a week, this time weighted toward farming families, because the point of the second one is collectivisation and the way you collectivise a countryside is to remove the people who know how to farm it alone.

Between the two, and after, a proportion of the men do not wait for the knock and go into the forest instead. In Lithuania something like fifty thousand pass through the partisan bands; the fighting runs into the 1950s, and the last of them are still hiding in the 1970s, one of them dying in 1978 when he is finally found.

The three did not coordinate any of this. It was done _to_ them, identically, by the same hand on the same dates, and that is precisely what welds them together: not a treaty and not a shared tongue, but the discovery that the calendar of your worst week is also your neighbour's. **The fourteenth of June is a day of mourning in all three countries and has never needed to be negotiated between them.**

[The Singer](persona_the_singer.md)'s grandmother is on one of the lists. Everyone on the stand has a version of it, which is why nobody mentions it at a rehearsal.

## Tension

This is where the group actually comes from, and it is worth being exact about it: the three are not a family because they are alike - two of the languages are cousins and the third is not a relative, one is Catholic and two are Lutheran, one remembers being a great power and two remember being estates. **They are a family because the same thing happened to them at the same hour.** A bond made that way is unbreakable and has one weakness, which the rest of the play tests: it is a bond of shared object, and a shared object can go away.
