# Plot: The Night Song Festivals, 1987 to 1988

## Taxonomy

[play_baltics](play_baltics.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

People start driving to the song grounds in Tallinn in the evening, after work, for no announced reason, and sing until it gets light. It is not a scheduled celebration. There is no programme and nobody has organised it in the sense the word usually carries. It happens several nights running in June 1988 and the number keeps going up.

## Action

By 11 September a crowd variously counted at around three hundred thousand is on and around the grounds - something in the order of a quarter of the entire Estonian people in one field. The same summer, Latvians and Lithuanians are doing their own version of it, and the flags that come out are the ones from between the wars, which have been illegal for forty-eight years and which a surprising number of households have kept.

Nothing about this is improvised except the timing. **The instrument is a hundred and twenty years old and everybody already knows how to use it.** [The festival](position_the_festival.md) was licensed in the 1860s on the grounds that culture is not politics, and here is the bill: a state that permitted choral singing for a century has produced a population that can assemble a quarter of itself, in tune, without a leaflet.

A painter looking at it writes the phrase _the singing revolution_, and it sticks, and it is accurate in a way slogans usually are not. There is no barricade in it and nothing to disperse. An armoured column can clear a square; it cannot do anything at all with a hundred thousand people who are only singing, and the authorities know it, and everyone in the field knows they know it.

[The Singer](persona_the_singer.md) is on the stand for all of it and has been since she was nine.

## Tension

The three get free by using the one thing their occupiers had allowed them to keep, which is the neatest historical irony available to any of them and is also a genuine method rather than a lucky accident. But the method has a shape: it works by assembling _a people_, in _its_ tongue, and demonstrating that there are a great many of them. That is unanswerable against an empire and it is a much harder instrument to pick up once the question is not who is outside the nation but who is inside it.
