# Plot: The Licence to Teach Anywhere, from 1088

## Taxonomy

## Owner

## Cue

A boy of fifteen leaves a village in Swabia on foot with a letter and no money, and walks to Bologna. It takes him most of a summer. He does not have a passport, because there is nothing yet that issues one, and he does not need a visa, because there is nobody at the places he crosses who thinks to ask.

## Action

What he is walking towards is not a country. It is a room where the teaching is in Latin, the books are the same books, and the examination is the same examination as the one they set in Paris and Oxford and Salamanca and, later, Prague and Kraków. When he passes it he is given a _licentia ubique docendi_, a licence to teach anywhere - and _anywhere_ is meant literally. It is honoured by universities that have never corresponded and by princes who are at war with each other.

So a thing exists on this continent, for four hundred years, that has no state behind it at all: a qualification that travels with the person holding it. Scholars move constantly and think nothing of it. Erasmus of Rotterdam, born in one place and buried in another, spends his working life in Paris, Leuven, Cambridge, Turin, Venice, Basel and Freiburg, writing to correspondents in every one of them in a language none of them speaks at home. The network has no capital and no treaty. It has a shared tongue, a shared curriculum, and an agreement that a person examined over there is examined.

Then the vernaculars rise, the confessions split, and the states harden. The Latin goes, the licence stops being honoured across the new borders, and by the nineteenth century a scholar needs papers. **The thing that was normal for four centuries becomes the thing that has to be reconstructed by treaty**, and it takes until the second half of the twentieth to get some of it back.

## Tension

The Union's founding documents read as though free movement were an invention of 1957, and the reason they read that way is that the founders were rebuilding rather than inventing, and rebuilding is less flattering. This continent already knew how to let a person carry their standing across it. What it had never worked out was how to do that once every square metre of it belonged to a state that issues documents. Everything after this is an attempt on that problem, and the question it leaves open is whether the answer they finally built - a right granted by twenty-seven governments to each other's nationals - is the old thing recovered or a much narrower thing wearing its name.
