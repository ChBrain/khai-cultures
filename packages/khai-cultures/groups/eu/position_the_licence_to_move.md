# Position: the licence to move

## Taxonomy

Parent group: positions

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Has

A permission that belongs to the person and not to the state that issued it. Somebody who holds it can get on a train in one country, get off in another, take a job there without asking anyone, be paid the same as the people already there, have their qualification recognised, register with a doctor, and stay. Nobody grants it at the border; there is no border to grant it at.

Its ancestor is older than any of the states that now honour it. The medieval universities issued a _licentia ubique docendi_, a licence to teach anywhere, and a scholar carrying one could arrive in Paris or Bologna or Salamanca and be examined on the same books in the same language by people who had never met them. What the licence certified was not where you were from. It was that you were the kind of person this place was for.

It is the one thing in the Union that most people have actually used. The single market is an abstraction to almost everyone who lives in it; a summer in another country, a cousin working abroad and coming home at Christmas, a phone that costs the same on both sides of a river - those are the licence, held in the hand.

## Orders

That the person, not the passport, is the unit. Everything the licence orders follows from that: a qualification travels with its holder, wages cannot be set by nationality, a family comes too, and the country you arrive in owes you the treatment it gives its own. It orders reciprocity absolutely - there is no version of this that one country can hold and not extend - which is why it is the hardest thing in the Union to have partially.

## Loses

Ground wherever movement is recast as arrival. The licence survives being unpopular; it does not survive being made conditional, because a conditional licence is a visa with better manners. It loses most quietly to distance: a right nobody in a town has ever exercised is indistinguishable, to that town, from a right that does not exist.

## Drives

It drives everyone who has ever gone and everyone who has had somebody go. It is what a nineteen-year-old with a suitcase is carrying, what a lorry driver crossing six frontiers before lunch is not thinking about, and what the people who leave a place and the people who stay in it argue about without ever naming.
