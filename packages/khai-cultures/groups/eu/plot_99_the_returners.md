# Plot: The Returners, since 1987

## Taxonomy

[play_eu](play_eu.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

Three thousand students go abroad for a term in the first year of a small exchange scheme, and nobody expects anything of it. It is named after a sixteenth-century scholar who spent his life moving between the same cities the scheme now sends people to, which the people who named it knew perfectly well.

## Action

By its fourth decade the scheme has moved something like fifteen million people, and it has done the one thing no treaty article can do: it produced a generation for whom the arrangement is not a policy but a memory of being twenty. Roughly a third of those who go end up living in another country afterwards; the programme has counted the children born to couples who met on it in the high hundreds of thousands. **This is the Union's only genuinely popular institution, and it is popular because it is the only one that happened to people rather than to their governments.**

[The Returner](persona_the_returner.md) is what comes back. She has never shown a document to cross a frontier and cannot say when she stopped noticing, and she says _back home_ about two places in the same conversation without hearing herself. She holds [the licence to move](position_the_licence_to_move.md) as a fact of nature, which is the mistake in it: she was genuinely surprised when [the one that left](@chbrain/khai-cultures-united-kingdom/play_united_kingdom.md) took its universities out of the scheme, not that it happened but that it _could_.

And the argument runs the other way at the same time. The continent that built this spent the 2010s discovering that its own people move too - that a nurse leaving Riga is a nurse Riga has lost, that a town emptied of everyone under thirty does not experience free movement as a freedom, and that the resentment this produces is not xenophobia and does not answer to being called it. The licence is held most warmly by the people who used it and most coldly by the people whose children used it and did not come back.

Then in 2022 the Union opened to several million people in a matter of weeks, by activating a directive written twenty-one years earlier and never once used, and did it with an argument that lasted days rather than the years the previous time. Nobody has satisfactorily explained the difference to themselves.

## Stage

- [the Returner](persona_the_returner.md): the generation the exchange made, who has the thing and did not negotiate it.
- [the licence to move](position_the_licence_to_move.md): most felt by those who used it, most resented where it emptied a town.
- [Poland](../../cultures/poland/play_poland.md): the member that sent the most people west and is now receiving them, and the one where both halves of the argument are held by the same families.
- [Ireland](@chbrain/khai-cultures-ireland/play_ireland.md): a country whose entire modern history is emigration, and the first to find that membership reversed the direction.

## Tension

The scheme worked, and what it produced is a constituency rather than a consensus: several million people for whom the Union is a place they were young in, and several million more for whom it is the reason the young are gone. **Both are true, and they are the same policy.** The open question is not whether free movement survives - it has survived being unpopular before - but whether a right that is only felt by the people who exercise it can be defended by them, in towns where nobody has, against the one argument the Union has never found an answer to: that leaving is easier than staying, and somebody has to stay.
