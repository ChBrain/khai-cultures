# References: Bern

## The pitch layer

The pitch is written from Switzerland's national Hofstede profile, inflected by what a plan-first city on a peninsula does to it: the same low power distance and high individualism, read through a place that converts disputes into procedures and gives them a generation to resolve. No dimension is named in the prose.

Source: Geert Hofstede, Hofstede Insights (the Country Comparison tool and the six-dimensions model of national culture), the national profile for Switzerland. No separate sub-scores for the cantons are claimed.

## Two decisions, recorded

**The origin is a landform.** Asked where this culture comes from, the available answers are a ducal foundation and a federal decision, and both are constitutional facts of the kind the house order on the defining question warns against staging as the culture. What actually explains Bern is that it was built on a peninsula where correcting is expensive and space cannot be added, so it had to plan before it built - and it has done everything that way since, including losing an empire in one morning and settling a secession in thirty-one years.

**The old lordship is staged and not apologised for.** Three centuries of ruling a French-speaking population that was never asked is in `plot_02`, plainly, and the mistrust it left is named in the culture-position and reappears in the Jura question. The house rule is that a play never stages an apology in place of a deed; what is owed here is the deed, said accurately, and the consequence where it is still lived.
