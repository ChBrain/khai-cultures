# Plot: Kanata

## Taxonomy

[play_canada](play_canada.md)

## Owner

## Cue

Two men lift a boat out of the water and carry it over their heads along a path through the trees, from one lake to the next. The boat weighs less than either of them. It is made of bark, split root and pitch, it was built here out of what grows here, and it can be repaired anywhere along the route with the same three things. Ahead of them are three thousand kilometres of water with walking in between.

## Action

There is no road across this country and there will not be one for a very long time. There is rock, forest and cold, and there is water: a chain of lakes and rivers running from the Atlantic to the middle of the continent and on to the western mountains. The people who had lived here for thousands of years had already solved it, and the solution is [the canoe](piece_the_canoe.md) - light enough to carry over a portage, shallow enough for a river in summer, and mendable on the bank with bark and gum.

When the French come for fur, they do not invent a route. They are taken along one. The trade runs on Indigenous canoes, Indigenous routes, Indigenous knowledge of the ice and the season, and Indigenous consent, because a European alone in this country in February is a dead man. Out of that working life comes a people who did not exist before it, the Metis, born of the trade itself.

So the country's shape is the shape of the water. The line the railway will follow in 1867 is the line the paddlers were already following, and the cities will stand where the portages were.

And the name is of a piece with it. Cartier asked what this place was called, was told kanata - a word for a village, the settlement in front of him - and wrote it down as the name of everything. The country is called by a borrowed word for a smaller thing, taken down slightly wrong, and it kept it.

## Tension

A country that begins as a partnership on the water learns its first skill early: you do not have to become each other in order to get somewhere together. That is the mosaic before it has a name, and it is the reason this federation would later be built by joining rather than by breaking.

And the shadow is in the same fact, because a partnership lasts exactly as long as it is needed. The trade required Indigenous knowledge, labour and permission, and the respect it showed was the respect of dependence. Once the rail was laid and the fur was worth less, the need ended - and what had been a relationship became, in one lifetime, the Indian Act and the schools. [The reckoning](plot_04_reconciliation.md) this country owes is not the story of strangers who arrived and took. It is the story of a working relationship that stopped being useful to one side, and of what that side did next.
