# References: Uri

## The pitch layer

The pitch is written from Switzerland's national Hofstede profile, inflected by what a small alpine transit canton does to it: the same low power distance and high individualism, read through a valley in which everyone knows whom they are contradicting. No dimension is named in the prose.

Source: Geert Hofstede, Hofstede Insights (the Country Comparison tool and the six-dimensions model of national culture), the national profile for Switzerland. No separate sub-scores for the cantons are claimed.

The two defining personas, [Ruedi](persona_ruedi.md) and [Nadia](persona_nadia.md), are archetypes, not historical individuals: composites carrying the tension between a side valley that has to be kept up by somebody and a generation that leaves through the mountain each Monday.

## Two decisions, recorded

**Suvorov is staged and not cast.** The 1799 crossing is this valley's one contribution to world history and it keeps its plot, but the general holds Russian and French as his tongues and neither is in the tongues package yet. Casting him would have written two `../` links into a culture that is otherwise able to be published on its own, so the event is staged and the man is described rather than given a Projection. A canton's Company is its own people; when `ru` and `fr_fr` have moved, he can be cast if a later hand wants him.

**Tell is not cast either, and for a different reason.** The legend belongs to the Confederation's play, which stages him as a persona; a culture may reach another's positions and never its cast. What Uri owns is not the man but the monument and the performance, which is the more honest thing for it to own in any case.
