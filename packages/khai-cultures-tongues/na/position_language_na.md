# Position: dorerin Naoero

## Has

An Oceanic grammar that sits badly in its own family. Nauruan is Micronesian by
descent, but its sound changes and its vocabulary fit none of the branches
cleanly, and it is usually left standing on its own inside the group rather than
filed beside Kiribati or Marshallese. It is the language of one raised coral
island and of nowhere else, with no dialect continuum to a neighbour and no
larger relative to be read against.

It carries the distinctions Oceanic languages keep and English lets go: a we
that includes the person spoken to and a separate we that shuts them out, and a
dual standing apart from the plural, so that the number of people in a sentence
is settled before anything is said about them. Its vowels are many and they
shift with what surrounds them, which is the fact every account of the language
reaches for first and the fact its writing has never comfortably held.

## Orders

It requires a speaker to decide, in the first word, whether the listener is
inside the group being spoken of. There is no neutral we to retreat into.

It also requires a writer to choose an orthography, because the language ships
two. The missionary spelling, made by German Protestant translators early in the
twentieth century, was revised by a committee in 1938 that set out to reduce the
diacritics and bring the letters closer to the ones a typewriter already had.
Neither displaced the other. Modern written Nauruan runs both, so the same word
appears with and without its marks depending on who is writing and what they
learned, and the marks - the tilde on a vowel, the ñ - are the first thing to go
when the tool is English.

## Loses

It loses ground to English in every setting where the writing is the record.
Nauru's government, courts and commerce run in English; the phosphate money that
built the state was counted in English, and so was its collapse. Nauruan is the
national language of the republic and the home language of nearly all its
citizens, but the number of those citizens is near twelve thousand, and there is
no diaspora large enough to hold a second centre of the language anywhere else.

It also loses the consistency that lets a language be searched, indexed and
quoted. A tongue with two live spellings has no single written form to be
looked up by, and a corpus in which the diacritics are optional is a corpus in
which two spellings of one word do not match.

## Drives

It drives its speakers to hold the relationship in the grammar rather than in
the courtesy: who is in and who is out is not a matter of tone here, it is a
matter of which pronoun was used, and it cannot be softened after the fact.

And it drives anyone writing it to make a decision about marks that a speaker
never has to make aloud. The language is not in difficulty when it is spoken.
It is in difficulty when it is typed.
