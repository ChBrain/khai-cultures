# Position: English

## Taxonomy

Parent group: positions

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Has

Almost no inflection left: nouns take a plural and a possessive and nothing else,
adjectives never agree, and the case system survives only in a handful of pronouns.
What carries the grammar instead is word order, which is why the same words in a
different order are a different sentence rather than a clumsy one. It holds a
doubled lexicon, a Germanic floor under a Romance and Latin ceiling, so that nearly
every idea can be said twice at two altitudes, ask or enquire, get or obtain, and
the choice is never neutral. It holds phrasal verbs in the thousands, where a plain
verb and a small preposition make a meaning neither one carries. And it has one
second person for everyone, singular and plural, intimate and formal alike.

## Orders

It orders the word order, because order is the grammar: a speaker cannot move a
phrase for emphasis the way an inflected language allows without changing who did
what. It orders a register choice in almost every sentence, since the doubled
lexicon means the plain word and the learned word are both available and picking
either says something about the speaker they did not intend to say. And it orders
an article before almost every noun, a distinction between the thing and a thing
that many languages leave to context and this one requires.

## Loses

It lost the second person it once had, so there is no way to mark distance or
intimacy in the pronoun itself; the work moves to names, titles and tone, which are
easier to get wrong and easier to deny. It loses the freedom to say something
plainly without also placing oneself, because the register choice is unavoidable
and legible. And it loses precision to its own flexibility: a noun that can be a
verb and a verb that can be a noun make a sentence that parses two ways more often
than a language with endings would allow.

## Drives

It accustoms its holder to signal standing by vocabulary rather than by form, so
that class and schooling travel in word choice where another language would carry
them in a pronoun. It trains an ear for the small preposition that changes
everything, since put off and put up are not variations on putting. And because so
many of its speakers hold it as a second language, it accustoms whoever holds it
first to being understood, and to the habit of assuming they will be.
