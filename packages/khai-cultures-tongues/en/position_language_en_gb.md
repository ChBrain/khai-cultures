# Position: British English

## Has

It keeps a spelling in which a noun and its verb are two different words. A
licence is the paper and to license is the act; a practice is the place and to
practise is what happens there; and a reader who meets either in print has been
told which one is meant before reaching the end of the sentence. The variety that
collapsed both pairs into one spelling has to wait for the grammar to say so.

It can leave the article off an institution and mean something by it. In hospital
is not in the hospital, at university is not at the university, and the missing
word is doing the work: with it, a building; without it, the thing the building
is for. A person can be in hospital and a visitor can be in the hospital on the
same afternoon, and the two sentences are about different situations.

And it can make a body plural when the body is behaving like people. The
committee are divided says something the committee is divided cannot, because
the first has members in it and the second has only an institution. The choice is
available in every sentence where an organisation takes a verb.

## Orders

It orders a list of spellings that cannot be worked out, only learnt. Colour,
honour, favour but not honorary; centre, theatre, metre but not perimeter;
travelled and cancelled with the second l that the other side drops. No rule
generates the list, so getting it right is not evidence of understanding the
language but of having been taught in one place rather than another.

It orders a decision it then refuses to settle. The largest dictionary of the
language prefers -ize, and most of what is written in this variety prefers -ise,
and both are correct here in a way neither is anywhere else. So whoever writes in
it must pick one, hold to it, and know that the choice will be read.

And it orders agreement by sense, which means asking, before every verb that
follows a collective, whether the thing is one thing or a number of people. It is
a small question and it falls due constantly, and it cannot be deferred to
context the way it can where the answer is always singular.

## Loses

It lost gotten, and with it a distinction the other side kept: has got is
possession and has gotten is acquisition, and where only the first survives, I've
got used to it and I've come to have it must share a form. A variety usually
loses its irregular past forms to tidiness; this one lost one that was carrying a
meaning.

It lost the standing. It is the older of the two large written forms and it is
now the marked one: it is the setting that has to be selected, the spelling the
machine underlines until told otherwise, and the version of a word that has to be
specified rather than assumed. Being first confers nothing here, and its own
words go out and come back changed.

And it loses its own islands on the page. Outwith, amn't, and the perfect built
with after doing are said daily inside the same borders and do not reach the
written standard, which planes them off as it planes off any variation it did not
sanction. What it holds, it holds in writing; what it drops, it drops there too,
and speech keeps them alive with nowhere to put them down.

## Drives

The unlearnable spelling list drives its holder to treat spelling as membership
rather than as a system. Nobody derives colour; they were taught it. So a
misspelling in this variety is not read as a slip in reasoning but as evidence of
where someone was schooled, which is a heavier thing for a small mistake to
carry.

Agreement by sense drives a habit of asking what a body actually is before saying
anything about it - a bench of judges or a court, eleven players or a club - so
that the grammatical question and the question of what an institution consists of
arrive at the same moment and get answered together.

And being the marked form of one's own language drives a wariness the unmarked
one never has occasion to learn. Whoever holds this knows every day that theirs
is the version that must be chosen, and comes to expect that being first is not
the same as being the default - which is a lesson available in the grammar long
before it is available anywhere else.
