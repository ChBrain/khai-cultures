# Tongues: References

Each variety carries the provenance of its own prose. What is recorded here is
what travels with the file when it leaves the culture that wrote it: who wrote
it, whether a speaker of the tongue has read it, and what the reading against the
position mnemonic found when it moved.

| `bar/position_language_bar.md`         | Written here as the anchor the family needed. The file that carried this name held Munich's Mittelbairisch under the family's title - the same mistake das bayerische Hochdeutsch was making with Bairisch features - so its content moved down to `bar_mitt` and the anchor was written fresh. What belongs at family level is what every Bairisch variety shares: the -l, the article before a name, es and enk from the old dual, and the missing preterite. Its strongest chapter is Loses, and it is the reason this package exists: Bairisch is spoken in Bavaria, Austria and South Tyrol by some fourteen million people, so the same dialect is a Mundart in one state and close to the state language in another - was in Wien fast s Amtsdeitsch is, gilt in München scho ois Bauernsproch.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| `bar/position_language_bar_mitt.md`    | This is the file that used to be the anchor, carrying Munich's variety under the family's name. | `hsb/position_language_hsb.md`         | **The prose is flagged for native review.** Written here. Not a German dialect and not written as one: Sorbian is West Slavic and its nearest relatives are Czech and Polish, so nothing in it is stated as a departure from German - that frame is what turns a minority language into a footnote to a majority one. Its Has is the dual, a whole number category for exactly two, surviving on the noun and the verb both, which only a handful of European languages still carry. Its Loses is the office fact that no other tongue here has: it has no monolingual speaker at all, so a conversation can switch at any moment and usually does, and the dual is the first thing younger speakers drop - the tongue loses first what most distinguishes it. Flagged for native review: no Sorbian speaker has read this prose.
