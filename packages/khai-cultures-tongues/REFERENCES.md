# Tongues: References

Each variety carries the provenance of its own prose. What is recorded here is
what travels with the file when it leaves the culture that wrote it: who wrote
it, whether a speaker of the tongue has read it, and what the reading against the
position mnemonic found when it moved.

| `bar/position_language_bar_wien.md`    | **The prose is flagged for native review.** Written here. It exists because two Austrian personas said they spoke Wienerisch and linked the Austrian _standard_ - the same fault Bavaria's and Berlin's files had before Bairisch and Berlinisch were split out, the better-known name standing in for the actual tongue. The rule that earns it a file is the Wiener Monophthongierung: where rural Middle Bavarian keeps the diphthong, Vienna has a long a. The minimal pair is clean and famous in both directions - oans zwoa drei against ans zwa drei, Stoa against Staa, hoass against haass - and it runs through every word carrying that vowel. Visible in writing, and the whole Viennese dialect literature is written with it. The second layer is lexical and belongs to no other Bairisch: Powidl, Buchtln, Kolatschn from Czech, Beisl and Hawara from Yiddish, Fiaker and Trafik from further off, words the city drew in because the monarchy came to it. Deliberately NOT claimed: the l-vocalisation (Goid, Woid, Soiz), which bar_mitt already holds and which is Middle Bavarian and not Viennese - the check for this batch confirms none of those three forms appears in this file; the -erl diminutive, which is de_at's; and es/enk, which is the bar anchor's. The Meidlinger L is named in the Has and explicitly not claimed, because it is a phonetic realisation and the house rule is that a difference which is only sound cannot carry a text file. Writing it also turned up an error in a shipped file: bar_mitt claimed Munich and Vienna spoke `dieselbe Sortn` and gave both the oa, which is wrong about Vienna. bar_mitt now says the eastern end takes the oa one step further to the long a, so the two files agree and the correction is where the mistake was. The research came back and three things in the first draft were wrong, so the file was corrected before it merged. First, the monophthongisation is NOT categorical: it is variable and socially stratified, present when a speaker goes broad and receding when they pull in, and the same person does it both ways inside one conversation. Notably it is also not a youth recession - younger Viennese speakers still produce the monophthongised forms (Moosmueller and Enzinger 2015), so the file says the ladder and does not say the tongue is dying. The Orders chapter, which had demanded the monophthong, now says the opposite and truer thing: it does not demand the vowel, it demands that you know what the vowel says about you. Second, the lexical claim was an exclusivity claim and exclusivity is wrong. Powidl, Beisl, Tschick, Hawara, Trafik, Haeferl and Gschropp are Austrian, Bavarian or wider Habsburg-contact vocabulary, culturally dense in Vienna but not Vienna's alone; only Fiaker is safely Vienna-specific. The file now says so in its own text and states what the real distinction is - not that the city has words the countryside lacks, but that all of them arrived in one place at once, because the city drew in the languages they came from. Third, the monophthongisation covers both inherited diphthongs and the first draft claimed only one; Haus to Haaas was added. The contrast was also reframed: it is Vienna against the surrounding East Middle Bavarian countryside, not Vienna against Munich. The Meidlinger L handling was confirmed correct - a phonetic realisation, not a writable contrast. bar_mitt's correction was softened to match: the eastern end takes the oa one step further oft, not always. |

| `de/position_language_de_at.md`        | Migrated and thinned. Its opening clause restated the anchor (`traegt dieselbe Wortbau- und Kasusfaehigkeit wie das Deutsch ueberhaupt`) and its Orders opened with `wie jedes Deutsch`, which is exactly the thinness the de anchor was written to cure. What it keeps is its own: Erdapfel, Jaenner, Sackerl, Marille, Paradeiser, Karfiol, the productive -erl, and the soft barely-aspirated onset. What it gained is the grammatical fact it was missing, and it is a real one: the sein-perfect with the verbs of standing, sitting and lying - ich bin gestanden, ich bin gesessen, ich bin gelegen, where the north takes haben. Migrating it finishes the German-language tongues of the DACH group except de_de, and it only cost two castings, because the four cultures that would have carried the rest were finished in the three batches before this one.
