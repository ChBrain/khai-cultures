# United Kingdom: References

The provenance behind this culture: the Hofstede source data that tunes the pitch
(kept here, never in the prose) and the public-domain historical sources behind
the plots, personas, places, and pieces.

## Hofstede source data

The pitch [pitch_uk.md](pitch_uk.md) is written from the United Kingdom's profile
on Geert Hofstede's model of national culture. Hofstede publishes a single
profile for Great Britain, and this is the national profile of the UK as a whole;
the constituent nation cultures (England, Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland) that
follow inherit it as their documented base, since no separate Hofstede sub-scores
are published. The pitch names no dimension and carries no number. The scores and
their source are recorded here so the data is on the record without entering the
prose.

Source: Geert Hofstede, Hofstede Insights (the Country Comparison tool and the
six-dimensions model of national culture). Scores are approximate, as published
by Hofstede Insights / The Culture Factor Group. This is the national profile for
Great Britain; no separate sub-scores for the constituent nations are claimed.

## The defining question

Per the house order on the defining question
(`management/orders/order_the_defining_question.md`), the tell was run on this
play's own words. Its Arc said the island became **a global power**; its Stakes
spoke of **the world it once ruled**; its Company held **the common tongue**,
**fair play and the orderly queue**, a playwright, a bus and a flag. Not one of
those appeared in a plot. Four plots stood here - Magna Carta, the Union, 1940,
the NHS - and they are four moments of the constitution. The play staged a state
and called it a culture, which is the failure this question exists to find, and
the ten dead Company entries were the symptom rather than the disease.

Three plots answer it. **1066** because the play had no origin at all and because
the Conquest is where the island stops being conquered and starts being layered:
the doubled vocabulary and the order of rank carried in the choice between its
halves are the two most durable facts about this culture and they begin in the
same event. **1897** because an empire of a quarter of the world's people cannot
be a thing the Stakes allude to and no scene admits; it is staged at the moment
of most self-regard on purpose, with Kipling's Recessional in the same summer, so
that the mood and the doubt are in one scene. **1948 to 1971** because the empire
came home and that is what made the Britain the modern cast actually lives in.

Two decisions are recorded rather than left for the next hand.

**The numbering was closed, not gapped.** Inserting 1897 between 1707 and 1940
renumbered the Finest Hour to 04 and the NHS to 05. Other cultures in this house
carry a gap at 01 from earlier prepends, and this one does not: `plot_00` and
`plot_99` are held outside the sequence by design, because they answer different
questions, but the sequence between them is a reading order and a hole in it
means nothing to a reader except that something was removed.

**The Empire is staged once, and at a parade.** A single plot cannot carry
Bengal, Amritsar, the concentration camps of the South African war, partition or
the Irish famine, and a plot that gestured at all of them would be a survey
rather than a scene. What the jubilee stages honestly is that the arrangement
felt permanent to the people in the crowd, and its Tension says in as many words
that a procession is not a famine and that all of it is in the same decades and
not in the parade. If a later hand judges that this culture owes a scene to what
the empire did rather than to how it felt, that scene is owed and this one does
not discharge it.
