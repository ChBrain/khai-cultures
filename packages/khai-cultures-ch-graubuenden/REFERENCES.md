# References: Graubünden

## The pitch layer

The pitch is written from Switzerland's national Hofstede profile, inflected by what it does to a canton of a hundred valleys and three languages that has never had a centre: the same low power distance and high individualism, read through a place where the first question is always whose competence it is. No dimension is named in the prose.

Source: Geert Hofstede, Hofstede Insights (the Country Comparison tool and the six-dimensions model of national culture), the national profile for Switzerland. No separate sub-scores for the cantons are claimed.

## Two decisions, recorded

**Rumantsch Grischun is a piece and not a tongue file.** It is a language, and the temptation was to give it a position in the tongues package beside `rm`. That would have been wrong on this house's own rule: a language position says what a tongue gives whoever holds it, and this one is held by nobody as a first language - it was built for administration and works there. What it is, in this culture, is an object that was made: a grammar, a dictionary and a rule. So it is a piece, `rm` stays the tongue, and the plot is about what happened when the object was put into schools.

**Jenatsch is staged and not cast.** He is the one figure of this canton with a European afterlife, and a persona for him would have to choose between the versions - the patriot, the turncoat, the murderer - that four centuries of writing have not settled. What the play needs from him is what he did and what it cost, and that fits in a plot. A Company is a canton's own people, and this canton's people are not that man.
