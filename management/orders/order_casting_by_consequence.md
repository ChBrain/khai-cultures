# Order: Casting by Consequence

## Direction

A play's `Company` is a closed cast and the coverage ratchet asks that every
member be fielded by a plot. Contemporary personas cannot be: Germany's Jonas was
born after the Mauerfall, Czechia's Tomáš into what the Velvet Revolution
produced, Austria's Lukas after Waldheim. They stand in no scene the play stages,
and the ratchet's own escape hatch, a waiver, is the usual answer.

There is a second answer, and the house was already using it before anyone wrote
it down. **A plot may cast a persona as what the event produced rather than as
someone present at it.** Ohio's Harper works out of the Cleveland the collapse
left behind; Pennsylvania's John is a retired steelworker in a city with no
steel, the last generation to hold the job the strike was about. Neither was
there. Both are what happened.

This order names the practice, fixes where it goes, and gives it the one test it
already had.

## Implementation

**The test is already written, in Virginia's waiver.** Someone drew this line,
named its two precedents, and refused the device where it did not apply:

> Unlike Ohio's Harper or Pennsylvania's John, she carries no thread back to an
> event on the board: the Northern Virginia of federal contracts is a world the
> eighteenth-century plots did not make and could not anticipate, so casting her
> in one would be **invention rather than consequence**.

That is the rule, fully formed, and this order does no more than move it where a
writer will find it. The reason it kept being re-derived is that it was sitting
in a waiver file for a culture that refused it.

**Consequence goes in the Tension, never the Stage.** The `Stage` is the scene's
cast, and a persona cast by consequence was not in the scene; the `Action` is
what happened, and they were not there for it. The `Tension` is what the event
left standing, which is exactly what they are. The house bears this out: of 941
personas cast anywhere, three are cast only in a Tension, and they are Harper,
John and Lukas — the practice and its placement are the same fact.

A disclaiming clause is not a substitute for the right chapter. Switzerland's
`Sprachenartikel 1938` casts Gian and Marco in its Action and its Stage and then
writes `obwohl beide diesen Tag selbst nicht erlebt haben` to undo it. The prose
is honest and the placement is not: a Stage line still lists them as cast in a
1938 scene, and the sentence that takes it back is doing work the chapter should
have done.

**The mechanism must be named, not the atmosphere.** Harper works in the building
the collapse emptied. Lukas's `Schmäh` specifically no longer carries the thing
Waldheim exposed. "Shaped by history" casts nobody, because it is true of
everyone; a consequence that would fit any persona in the culture is not a
consequence, it is a sentence.

**A chain is not a thread.** Croatia's Ivan and Serbia's Stefan are defined by
emigration for work, which their plays do not stage; the connection to the last
plot runs through events that are not on the board. Both waivers say so
themselves and both ask for a plot rather than a casting. That is the correct
answer and this order does not overturn it: where the honest fix is a plot, the
device must not be used to avoid writing one.

**A waiver that has been superseded is removed in the same change.** Casting by
consequence retires the waiver that stood in for it; leaving both behind states
two different things about the same persona. This is the one part of the order a
script can decide, so it is a gate and not a paragraph.

**What the machine may and may not do.** It can refuse a waiver for a persona
that is now cast, and it can list the personas cast only by a Tension so that the
list is read. It cannot tell consequence from invention — that is the Virginia
test, and it is a person's to apply. The queue is reported, never gated, for the
same reason the tongue-drift queue is.
