# Cultures without a today

This house stages living cultures and the arc that produced them. The `geo.json`
ISO code is not a territorial claim; it is a **mental model**, and what it answers
is one question: _which culture would you meet if you went there today?_ That is
why a country code works as the anchor even for a culture that predates the
country, and why a sub-national culture takes its parent's code without that
meaning the culture is a contented province.

Two classes of culture the anchor cannot hold. They look like one problem and are
not.

## The first is resolution, and groups may already answer it

Occitania spans four French regions and matches no ISO code. Brittany's `FR-BRE`
excludes Nantes, its own ducal capital. The Basque Country and Catalonia cross
the Pyrenees, and splitting either along the state line would stage a border
rather than a culture.

These are all cases where a culture exists today, has speakers today, and would
be met if you went there today. The anchor is real; it is only at the wrong
resolution, or it stops at a frontier the culture does not. The house already has
the machinery: a **group**, as `dach` is, holds several anchored cultures and
says what binds them. Nothing new is needed in principle, only the judgement of
which cases are groups.

## The second is time, and the house has no answer

The Celts. The Huns. Ancient Greece, Rome, Carthage, the Aztec. Here the question
the anchor answers has no meaning: **there is no going there today.** You can
stand on the ground and meet somebody else.

And the map is a different shape as well as a different age. Ancient Greek is not
Greece: it is the Aegean and Ionia and Magna Graecia and the Black Sea colonies,
later Alexandria and Bactria, a culture defined by a language and a set of cities
and never by a contiguous territory at all. A single ISO code would be false in
both directions at once - too small for where the culture reached, too large for
where it now is.

**But the map is not the blocker.** The rule that excludes these cultures is the
house's own, and it is `plot_99`:

> every culture says where it now stands, in `plot_99`: a record of the decade
> still resolving, never a forecast

A culture whose decade stopped resolving in 146 BC cannot have that file as
defined. `plot_00` already gives every culture a where-it-comes-from, and the
chain from `plot_00` to `plot_99` is a time axis running through the inside of a
culture. What a lost culture needs is not a new kind of map. It needs the far end
of that axis to be allowed to sit in the past - and then something has to say
what `plot_99` means when the present tense is gone.

## What is not yet decided

- Whether a lost culture is a **culture** at all in this house, or a different
  khai type, or a group of the cultures that carry its inheritance forward.
- What replaces `plot_99` when there is no still-resolving decade. A terminal
  plot? The same file with a different contract? A play that simply ends?
- Whether `geo.json` gains a **when** beside its where, and what a renderer does
  with it.
- Whether the honest anchor for a culture like Ancient Greek is a territory at
  all, rather than a tongue and a list of cities.
- What happens to the [defining question](../orders/order_the_defining_question.md)
  when no native speaker can be wrong about the answer. `review: "native"` has
  nobody to send it to.

## Why this is parked and not decided

The living house is 316 cultures and not finished, and the arc of how today got
here is the thing being built. Lost cultures are a second axis, not more of the
first, and taking them on before the first is steady would put a second unfinished
system inside an unfinished one.

Parked deliberately, with the reasoning above, so that whoever picks it up starts
from `plot_99` and not from the map. The map question is real and separate: it is
about resolution, borders and identifiers, and it is being asked of the
cartography directly.
