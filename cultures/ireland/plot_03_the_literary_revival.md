# Plot: The Literary Revival

## Taxonomy

[play_ireland](play_ireland.md)

## Owner

- Project: khai-cultures

## Cue

At the turn of the twentieth century, a generation sets out to give Ireland a national literature of its own, some of them reaching back to Irish, more of them writing the country in English.

## Action

The lasting work proves to be in English: from [exile](position_emigration.md), [James Joyce](persona_joyce.md) turns the one single Dublin day into Ulysses and remakes the novel out of it, while Yeats and the Abbey build a theatre in English. Ireland's gift to the world arrives in the coloniser's own tongue. The other half of the same revival never goes near a page at all: [the GAA](process_the_gaa.md) makes the parish into a team and the county into a nation, and [an seisiún](process_an_seisiun.md) hands the tunes on from player to player by the ear, so what the writers do in English the field and the bar do in a language of their own.

## Stage

- [James Joyce](persona_joyce.md): the exile who makes English the medium of the nation's whole genius.
- [Dublin](place_dublin.md): the city Joyce maps out street by street and him abroad.
- [the GAA](process_the_gaa.md): the amateur game that gives the parish and the county a shape to belong to.
- [an seisiún](process_an_seisiun.md): the tunes carried on by ear, the revival's half that was never written down.

## Tension

The nation finds its voice in the very language it lost its own to: the literary triumph is, as well, the quiet proof that English is after taking the daily ground the revival hoped to win back, and that the country's greatness and its loss are written the both in the one set of words.
